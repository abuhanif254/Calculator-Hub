Comparing a `$30/hour` wage against a `$65,000` salaried job offer looks simple until unpaid holidays, variable shift schedules, overtime eligibility, and payroll benefits enter the picture. Converting hourly pay into an annual salary requires anchoring your pay rate to the exact number of paid hours and paid weeks you actually work each year—not just assuming a flat 52-week calendar.

## How the 2,080-Hour Rule Turns Hourly Pay Into Annual Salary

Across U.S. human resources and payroll accounting, a standard full-time year equals **2,080 hours** (40 hours per week multiplied by 52 weeks). When an hourly position includes paid holidays and paid vacation, every week of the year generates income. When time off is unpaid, however, subtracting unpaid weeks from 52 prevents overstating your true gross earnings.

The calculator converts your hourly wage across every pay cadence using five linked equations:

```text
Paid Weeks per Year = 52 - Unpaid Weeks
Annual Gross Salary = Hourly Wage × Hours per Week × Paid Weeks per Year
Weekly Gross Pay    = Hourly Wage × Hours per Week
Biweekly Gross Pay  = Weekly Gross Pay × 2
Monthly Average Pay = Annual Gross Salary / 12
Daily Gross Pay     = Hourly Wage × (Hours per Week / Days per Week)
```

Notice two critical details in these equations:

- **Monthly pay is divided by 12, not multiplied by 4 weeks.** A 52-week year averages `4.333` weeks per month (`52 / 12`), so multiplying weekly pay by 4 undercounts your monthly gross income by `7.7%`.
- **Unpaid weeks directly compress annual and monthly averages.** Reducing paid weeks from `52` to `50` trims `80` billable hours from a 40-hour schedule, even though your weekly paycheck during active workweeks stays unchanged.
- **Part-time schedules scale proportionally.** Entering `30` hours per week at `$30/hour` across 52 weeks produces `$46,800` annually (`$3,900/month`)—letting part-time clinicians, adjuncts, and retail leads benchmark reduced schedules accurately.

## Walking Through $30/Hour: Paid Time Off vs. Unpaid Leave

Suppose you receive a full-time offer at **$30.00/hour**, working **40 hours per week** across **5 days** (`8 hours/day`). Compare how two PTO policies reshape your annual gross income.

### Scenario A: Full Paid Time Off (0 Unpaid Weeks, 2,080 Paid Hours)

1. **Paid Weeks:** `52 - 0 = 52 weeks`
2. **Weekly Pay:** `$30.00 × 40 = $1,200.00`
3. **Daily Pay:** `$30.00 × (40 / 5) = $240.00`
4. **Biweekly Paycheck:** `$1,200.00 × 2 = $2,400.00`
5. **Annual Gross Salary:** `$30.00 × 40 × 52 = $62,400.00`
6. **Monthly Average Gross:** `$62,400.00 / 12 = $5,200.00`

### Scenario B: Unpaid Holidays and Vacation (4 Unpaid Weeks, 1,920 Paid Hours)

Many staffing-agency, retail, hospitality, and contract-to-hire roles pay only for hours clocked. If you take 10 unpaid holidays (2 weeks) plus 2 weeks of unpaid vacation (`4 unpaid weeks` total):

1. **Paid Weeks:** `52 - 4 = 48 weeks`
2. **Annual Gross Salary:** `$30.00 × 40 × 48 = $57,600.00`
3. **Monthly Average Gross:** `$57,600.00 / 12 = $4,800.00`
4. **52-Week Equivalent Rate:** `$57,600.00 / 2,080 = $27.69/hour`

Those four unpaid weeks quietly erase **$4,800.00** per year—a `7.69%` income drop compared to an identical `$30/hour` job with paid vacation and holidays.

## Overtime, W-2 Benefits, and 1099 Rate Traps to Watch

Gross base conversion is only the opening move when weighing competing job offers. Four real-world payroll mechanics regularly swing total compensation by `$8,000` to `$20,000` a year:

### 1. FLSA Overtime Multipliers Can Flip Offer Rankings
Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt hourly employees receive `1.5×` their base rate beyond 40 hours per workweek. At `$30/hour`, overtime pays `$45/hour`. Averaging just 5 hours of overtime across 50 working weeks adds **$11,250** (`$45 × 5 × 50`), lifting gross pay to `$73,650`—well ahead of a `$68,000` exempt salaried job expecting the same 45-hour weeks with zero overtime pay.

### 2. Unpaid Meal Breaks Shrink Paid Hours
An 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. schedule with a mandatory 30-minute unpaid lunch equals 40 paid hours, not 42.5. Verify whether meal breaks are clocked out before entering your weekly hours, or your annual projection will run `6.25%` high.

### 3. Employer-Paid W-2 Benefits Add 20% to 30% Load
Two identical `$62,400` gross totals rarely deliver equal take-home wealth. A W-2 employer pays half of your FICA payroll taxes (`6.2%` Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of `$184,500`, plus `1.45%` Medicare—`7.65%` total), subsidizes health premiums, and may match 401(k) deferrals toward the 2026 IRS employee limit of `$24,500`. Always add employer health subsidies and retirement matches before comparing hourly and salaried offers.

### 4. Gross-to-Net Tax Gaps on 1099 Contracts
If your hourly rate is a 1099 consulting rate rather than W-2 payroll, subtract self-employment tax (`15.3%` on 92.35% of net earnings up to `$184,500`, half deductible on Form 1040), unbilled administrative weeks, and individual ACA health insurance premiums before equating that hourly rate to a salaried offer.
