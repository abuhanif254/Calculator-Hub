# Newton's Second Law: Understanding Force and Mass

Why is it so much harder to push a broken-down truck than a broken-down bicycle? 
The answer lies in Sir Isaac Newton's Second Law of Motion, arguably the most famous equation in classical mechanics: **F = ma**.

In this guide, we will break down the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration, and show you how to use our free [Force Calculator](/en/calculators/force-calculator) to instantly solve your physics homework.

---

## 🍏 What is Force?

In physics, a force is simply a **push or a pull** on an object with mass that causes it to change its velocity (to accelerate).
Force is a vector quantity, meaning it has both a magnitude (how strong the push is) and a direction (where the push is going).

**The Equation:** *Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma)*

* **F:** Force, measured in **Newtons (N)**. One Newton is the amount of force required to accelerate 1 kg of mass at 1 m/s².
* **m:** Mass, measured in kilograms (kg).
* **a:** Acceleration, measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).

---

## ⚖️ Mass vs. Weight

A common mistake students make is confusing mass with weight. 
* **Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. Your mass is exactly the same whether you are on Earth, the Moon, or floating in deep space. 
* **Weight** is actually a *Force*. It is the force of gravity pulling down on your mass. Because gravity is weaker on the Moon, your weight is lower on the Moon, but your mass never changes.

---

## 🚛 The Truck vs. The Bicycle

Let's return to the broken-down truck. 
According to F=ma, if you want an object to accelerate, you have to apply Force. 
Because the truck has a massive amount of Mass (m), it requires a massive amount of Force (F) to achieve even a tiny Acceleration (a). 

Conversely, the bicycle has a very tiny Mass, so even a small push (Force) will cause it to accelerate quickly!
