# Future Value Explained: Predicting the Worth of Your Investments

A dollar today is not worth a dollar tomorrow. Because of inflation, a dollar sitting in a mattress loses purchasing power every single day. Because of compound interest, a dollar invested in the stock market gains purchasing power every single day.

This fundamental concept is called the **Time Value of Money (TVM)**. In this guide, we will break down how to calculate exactly what your current investments will be worth decades from now, and show you how to use our free [Future Value Calculator](/en/calculators/future-value-calculator).

---

## 🧮 The Mathematics of Future Value (FV)

To calculate Future Value, you need to know three variables:
1. **Present Value (PV):** The amount of money you have right now.
2. **Interest Rate (r):** The expected annual rate of return.
3. **Time (n):** The number of years the money will be invested.

**The Basic Formula:**
*FV = PV × (1 + r)^n*

*Example:* You invest **$10,000** today into an index fund that returns **8%** per year. You plan to leave it there for **20 years**.
* FV = 10,000 × (1 + 0.08)^20
* FV = 10,000 × (4.66)
* **FV = $46,609**

Without adding a single extra penny, your $10,000 will grow into $46,609 simply by leaving it alone for two decades!

---

## 📈 Compounding Frequency Matters

The basic formula assumes that your interest is compounded annually (once a year). However, many investments and bank accounts compound *monthly* or *daily*. 

The more frequently your money compounds, the higher your Future Value will be!
* $10,000 at 8% compounded **annually** for 20 years = $46,609.
* $10,000 at 8% compounded **monthly** for 20 years = $49,268.

By simply switching to an account that compounds monthly, you earn an extra $2,600 completely for free!
