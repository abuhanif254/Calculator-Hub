# How to Calculate Your Final Grade: Weighted Averages Explained

As the end of the semester approaches, students everywhere are faced with the same stressful question: *"What do I need to score on the final exam to pass this class?"*

Because most modern classrooms use a **weighted grading system**, you cannot simply add all your points together and divide by the total. A 100% on a homework assignment is not mathematically equal to a 100% on a midterm!

In this guide, we will break down how weighted averages work, how your Grade Point Average (GPA) is calculated, and how to use our free [Grade Calculator](/en/calculators/grade-calculator) to reverse-engineer your final exam.

---

## ⚖️ Understanding Weighted Grading Systems

In a weighted system, the teacher assigns a specific percentage (a "weight") to different categories of work. For example:
* **Homework:** 10%
* **Quizzes:** 20%
* **Midterm Exam:** 30%
* **Final Exam:** 40%

Even if you have completed 50 individual homework assignments and only 1 midterm, the midterm is mathematically worth *three times as much* as all of your homework combined.

### The Mathematical Formula
To calculate your current grade, you must multiply your average score in each category by the weight of that category, and then add them all together.
*(Homework Avg × 0.10) + (Quiz Avg × 0.20) + (Midterm Score × 0.30) = Current Grade*

## 🎯 How to Calculate What You Need on the Final

This is the most common use case for our calculator. Let's say your current grade (before the final) is an 82%. You want to get a 90% in the class. The final exam is worth 40% of your total grade.

**The Formula:**
*(Target Grade - Current Weighted Grade) / Weight of Final = Required Final Exam Score*

If you do the math manually, you might discover that you need a **102%** on the final exam to secure an A in the class. Since the highest possible score is usually 100%, it is mathematically impossible for you to get an A. Knowing this *before* you start studying can save you hours of unnecessary stress!

---

## 🎓 Unweighted vs. Weighted GPA

When looking at your overall transcript, your GPA represents your academic performance across all classes.
* **Unweighted GPA:** Every class is treated equally on a 4.0 scale (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0). An A in standard Gym class is worth the same as an A in standard Biology.
* **Weighted GPA:** Harder classes (like AP or Honors courses) are given extra weight, often pushing the maximum scale up to a 5.0. An A in AP Biology might be worth 5.0, while an A in standard Gym is still worth 4.0.
