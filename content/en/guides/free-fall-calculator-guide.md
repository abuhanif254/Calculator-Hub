# The Physics of Free Fall: Gravity in a Vacuum

If you drop a feather and a bowling ball from the roof of a building, the bowling ball will hit the ground first. Common sense tells us that heavier objects fall faster than lighter objects. 

However, in physics, common sense is often wrong! In this guide, we will explore the fascinating mechanics of **Free Fall**, explain the role of air resistance, and show you how to use our free [Free Fall Calculator](/en/calculators/free-fall-calculator).

---

## 🪶 The Feather and the Bowling Ball

Why does the bowling ball hit the ground first? Because of **Air Resistance**. The feather is light and fluffy, so it catches the air and floats down slowly. 

If you were to suck all the air out of the room to create a perfect vacuum, and then drop the feather and the bowling ball again, **they would hit the ground at the exact same time.**
In the absence of air resistance, *all objects fall at the exact same rate, regardless of their mass!*

---

## 📉 The Constant Acceleration of Gravity

When an object is in free fall (meaning gravity is the *only* force acting upon it), it accelerates downwards. 

On Earth, this acceleration is a constant **9.8 m/s²** (usually represented by the variable *g*).
This means that for every second an object is falling, its velocity increases by 9.8 meters per second. 
* **At 0 seconds:** Velocity = 0 m/s (You just dropped it).
* **At 1 second:** Velocity = 9.8 m/s.
* **At 2 seconds:** Velocity = 19.6 m/s.
* **At 3 seconds:** Velocity = 29.4 m/s.

---

## 🧮 The Free Fall Equations

To solve free fall problems, we use standard kinematics equations. Because the initial velocity is usually zero (you drop the object from rest), the equations simplify beautifully:

* **To find Velocity (v):** *v = g * t*
* **To find Distance fallen (d):** *d = ½ * g * t²*
* **To find Time (t):** *t = √(2d / g)*

*(Where 'g' is 9.8 m/s², and 't' is time in seconds).*
