# The Complete Guide to Fractions: Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying & Dividing

For many students (and adults), fractions are the most intimidating part of basic mathematics. Unlike whole numbers, fractions have two parts—a numerator and a denominator—and the rules for combining them change depending on the operation you are performing.

In this comprehensive guide, we will break down the fundamental rules of fraction arithmetic, explain how to simplify your answers, and show you how to use our free [Fraction Calculator](/en/calculators/fraction-calculator) to check your homework or solve real-world measurement problems.

---

## 🍕 Understanding the Anatomy of a Fraction

A fraction represents a part of a whole.
* **Numerator (Top Number):** Represents how many parts you *have*.
* **Denominator (Bottom Number):** Represents how many parts make up the *whole*.

If you have a pizza cut into 8 slices, and you eat 3 of them, you have eaten **3/8** of the pizza.

---

## ➕ Adding and Subtracting Fractions

The golden rule for adding and subtracting fractions is that **the denominators must be exactly the same**. You cannot directly add 1/2 and 1/3, because the "pieces" are different sizes.

### Step 1: Find a Common Denominator
You must find a number that both denominators can divide into evenly (the Least Common Multiple).
* Example: 1/2 + 1/3
* The smallest number both 2 and 3 go into is 6.

### Step 2: Convert the Fractions
Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction to match the new denominator.
* 1/2 becomes 3/6 (multiplied by 3/3)
* 1/3 becomes 2/6 (multiplied by 2/2)

### Step 3: Add/Subtract the Numerators
Once the denominators match, you simply add or subtract the top numbers. The denominator stays the same.
* 3/6 + 2/6 = **5/6**

---

## ✖️ Multiplying Fractions

Multiplying fractions is actually much easier than adding them! You do *not* need a common denominator.

### The Rule: Multiply Straight Across
1. Multiply the numerators together to get the new numerator.
2. Multiply the denominators together to get the new denominator.
* Example: 1/2 × 3/4
* Top: 1 × 3 = 3
* Bottom: 2 × 4 = 8
* Answer = **3/8**

---

## ➗ Dividing Fractions

Dividing fractions uses a clever trick often taught as **"Keep, Change, Flip."**

1. **Keep** the first fraction exactly as it is.
2. **Change** the division sign to a multiplication sign.
3. **Flip** the second fraction upside down (this is called the reciprocal).
4. Multiply straight across!

* Example: 1/2 ÷ 3/4
* Keep, Change, Flip: 1/2 × 4/3
* Top: 1 × 4 = 4
* Bottom: 2 × 3 = 6
* Answer: 4/6 (which simplifies to **2/3**)
