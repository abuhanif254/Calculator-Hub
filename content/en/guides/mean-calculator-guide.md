# How to Find the Mean: Arithmetic, Geometric, and Harmonic

When someone asks for the "average," they are almost always referring to the **Arithmetic Mean**. However, in statistics and finance, there are several other types of means that are crucial for specific types of data.

In this guide, we will explore the three most common means (known as the Pythagorean means) and how to use our [Mean Calculator](/en/calculators/mean-calculator).

---

## ➕ 1. The Arithmetic Mean

This is the standard average. It is calculated by adding all the numbers in a dataset together and dividing by the total count of numbers.

* **Best used for:** Normally distributed data without extreme outliers (e.g., test scores).
* **Formula:** $(x_1 + x_2 + ... + x_n) / n$

---

## ✖️ 2. The Geometric Mean

The geometric mean is used for values that are meant to be multiplied together or are exponential in nature, such as growth rates, compounding interest, or biological populations.

Instead of adding the numbers, you multiply them all together and then take the $n$th root (where $n$ is the total count of numbers).

* **Best used for:** Percentages, investment returns, and ratios.
* **Formula:** $\sqrt[n]{x_1 \times x_2 \times ... \times x_n}$

---

## ➗ 3. The Harmonic Mean

The harmonic mean is highly specialized and is primarily used for calculating the average of rates or ratios, such as speed (miles per hour) or fuel efficiency. 

* **Best used for:** Averages of rates over the same distance or time.
* **Formula:** $n / ( (1/x_1) + (1/x_2) + ... + (1/x_n) )$
