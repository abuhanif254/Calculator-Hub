# The Ultimate Guide to Calculating Volume: Formulas for Every Shape

While area measures the flat 2D surface of an object, **volume measures the amount of 3D space an object occupies.** 

Whether you are trying to figure out how much water is needed to fill a swimming pool, how much gas fits in a cylindrical tank, or just trying to pass a geometry test, calculating volume is an essential real-world skill. 

In this guide, we will break down the essential formulas for the most common 3D shapes and show you how to skip the complex math by using our free [Volume Calculator](/en/calculators/volume-calculator).

## 🧊 1. Volume of a Rectangular Prism (Box/Cube)

This is the most common and easiest shape to calculate. Think of a cardboard box, a shipping container, or a room in your house.

**The Formula:**
*Volume = Length × Width × Height (or Depth)*

**Example:** A swimming pool is 20 feet long, 10 feet wide, and 5 feet deep.
* 20 × 10 = 200
* 200 × 5 = 1,000 cubic feet of water.

*(Note: For a perfect cube, where all sides are equal, the formula is simply Side³).*

---

## 🛢️ 2. Volume of a Cylinder

A cylinder is any shape with two parallel circular bases connected by a curved surface (like a soup can, a pipe, or a water silo).

To find the volume of a cylinder, you must first calculate the *area* of the circular base, and then multiply it by the height.

**The Formula:**
*Volume = π × r² × h*
(Where **π** is Pi [~3.14159], **r** is the radius of the circle, and **h** is the height).

**Example:** A soup can has a radius of 2 inches and a height of 5 inches.
* π × (2)² × 5
* 3.14159 × 4 × 5 = 62.83 cubic inches.

---

## 🍦 3. Volume of a Cone

A cone has a circular base that tapers to a single point (like a traffic cone or an ice cream cone). 
Mathematically, a cone is exactly **one-third** the volume of a cylinder that has the exact same base and height!

**The Formula:**
*Volume = (1/3) × π × r² × h*

---

## ⚽ 4. Volume of a Sphere

A sphere is a perfectly round 3D object (like a basketball or a globe). Because there are no straight lines or flat bases, the formula relies entirely on the radius (the distance from the exact center to the edge) and Pi.

**The Formula:**
*Volume = (4/3) × π × r³*
