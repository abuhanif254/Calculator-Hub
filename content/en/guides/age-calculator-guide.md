# The Mathematical Formula for Age: How to Calculate Exact Age

"How old are you?" It seems like the simplest question in the world. But mathematically, calculating someone's exact age down to the day involves complex calendar logic. 

Furthermore, depending on where you are in the world, the answer to that question might actually change! In this guide, we will explore the math behind age calculation, cultural differences in aging, and how to use our free [Age Calculator](/en/calculators/age-calculator) to find out exactly how many days you've been alive.

---

## 🎂 Western Age Calculation vs. Traditional Age

Before we dive into the math, it is important to note that age is a cultural construct.

**The Western Method**
In most Western countries (and standard mathematical formulas), age is based on full years passed since birth. 
* When you are born, you are 0 years old. 
* You turn 1 year old exactly 12 months after your birth date.

**The Traditional East Asian Method**
In traditional East Asian age reckoning (historically used in China, Korea, and Japan), a person is born at the age of **1**. Furthermore, everyone ages by one year not on their birthday, but on the New Year (either the Gregorian New Year or the Lunar New Year, depending on the specific tradition). 
* Under this system, a baby born on December 31st turns 2 years old the very next day on January 1st! 
* *(Note: South Korea legally abolished this system in 2023 to align with international standards, but it is still culturally prevalent).*

## 🧮 The Mathematics of Exact Age

If you were born on **May 15th, 1990**, and today is **October 20th, 2024**, how old are you exactly?

The human brain usually calculates this by finding the difference in years, and then adjusting based on the month.
1. **Subtract the Years:** 2024 - 1990 = 34 years.
2. **Check the Months:** Because October (the 10th month) is *after* May (the 5th month), you have already had your birthday this year. You are 34.
3. *If today was March 20th*, you would not have had your birthday yet, so you would subtract 1 and be 33.

### The Leap Year Dilemma
What if you were born on **February 29th** (a Leapling)? 
When is your exact age incremented in non-leap years? 
Legally, most countries (like the UK and Hong Kong) consider March 1st to be your legal birthday in common years. However, in New Zealand and Taiwan, the legal birthday is February 28th.
