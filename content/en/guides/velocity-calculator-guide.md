# Understanding Velocity: Speed With a Direction

If you ask a race car driver how fast they are going, they might say "100 miles per hour." In physics, this is called **Speed**. It tells you how fast an object is moving, but it is missing a critical piece of information: *where* the object is going. 

**Velocity** is speed with a direction. In this guide, we will break down the mechanics of velocity, the difference between scalar and vector quantities, and how to use our free [Velocity Calculator](/en/calculators/velocity-calculator) to ace your physics homework.

---

## 🏎️ Speed vs. Velocity

* **Speed (Scalar Quantity):** Speed only has a magnitude (a number). It does not care about direction. Example: *60 miles per hour*.
* **Velocity (Vector Quantity):** Velocity has both a magnitude *and* a direction. Example: *60 miles per hour, North*.

**Why does this matter?**
Imagine you drive your car in a giant circle at a constant 60 mph. 
Your *speed* is a constant 60 mph. However, because you are constantly turning the steering wheel, your *direction* is constantly changing. Therefore, in physics, your *velocity* is constantly changing, even though your speedometer never moves!

---

## 📏 Distance vs. Displacement

To calculate speed, you use **Distance** (the total path you traveled).
To calculate velocity, you use **Displacement** (the straight-line distance from your starting point to your ending point).

* **Example:** You walk 10 meters East, and then turn around and walk 10 meters West back to where you started.
* **Distance:** You walked a total of 20 meters.
* **Displacement:** Because you ended exactly where you started, your displacement is 0 meters.
* **Result:** You had a positive speed, but your average velocity for the trip was exactly 0!

---

## 🧮 The Velocity Formula

Calculating average velocity requires dividing the change in position by the change in time.

**Formula:** *v = Δx / Δt*
*(Velocity = Displacement / Time)*

* **v:** Velocity (usually measured in meters per second, m/s).
* **Δx:** Change in position (Displacement).
* **Δt:** Change in time.
