# Simple vs. Compound Interest: What is the Difference?

When you borrow money or invest it, the interest is calculated in one of two ways: **Simple** or **Compound**. 

Understanding the difference is critical because one method makes your money grow slowly, while the other makes it grow exponentially. In this guide, we will explain the mechanics of simple interest and show you how to use our free [Simple Interest Calculator](/en/calculators/simple-interest-calculator).

---

## ➖ What is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is calculated *only* on the original principal amount. You do not pay (or earn) interest on the accumulated interest from previous years. 

**The Formula:**
*Interest = Principal × Rate × Time*

**Example:** You borrow $10,000 at a 5% simple interest rate for 3 years.
* Year 1 Interest: $10,000 × 5% = $500
* Year 2 Interest: $10,000 × 5% = $500
* Year 3 Interest: $10,000 × 5% = $500
* **Total Interest Paid:** $1,500.

Notice how the interest charge is exactly $500 every single year? It never changes because it is always based on the original $10,000. 

---

## 📈 How is Compound Interest Different?

Compound interest is calculated on the principal *plus* any interest that has already accumulated. You are earning "interest on your interest."

**Example:** You invest $10,000 at a 5% compound interest rate for 3 years.
* Year 1 Interest: $10,000 × 5% = $500. (New Balance: $10,500)
* Year 2 Interest: $10,500 × 5% = $525. (New Balance: $11,025)
* Year 3 Interest: $11,025 × 5% = $551.25. (New Balance: $11,576.25)
* **Total Interest Earned:** $1,576.25.

If you are an investor, you want *compound* interest because it makes your wealth grow faster. If you are a borrower, you want *simple* interest because it makes your loan cheaper!

---

## 🚗 Where is Simple Interest Used?

Most mortgages and credit cards use compound interest (or amortized interest that acts similarly). Simple interest is most commonly used for:
1. **Auto Loans:** Most car loans are simple interest. If you pay extra toward the principal, you reduce the balance that the daily interest is calculated on, saving you money.
2. **Personal Loans:** Short-term loans from family members or peer-to-peer lenders.
3. **Certificates of Deposit (CDs):** Some short-term CDs pay simple interest at maturity.
