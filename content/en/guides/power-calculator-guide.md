# Physics of Power: The Rate of Doing Work

If you carry a heavy box up a flight of stairs, you do a specific amount of Work. 
If you *run* up the stairs with that exact same box, you still do the exact same amount of Work. However, you will feel much more exhausted. Why? Because you generated more **Power**.

In physics, Power is the rate at which Work is done over time. In this guide, we will explain the formula, the units of measurement, and how to use our free [Power Calculator](/en/calculators/power-calculator).

---

## ⏱️ Work vs. Power

* **Work:** Force multiplied by Displacement. It does not care how long the task takes. (Measured in Joules).
* **Power:** Work divided by Time. It measures how *fast* the energy is being transferred. (Measured in Watts).

By running up the stairs instead of walking, you completed the same amount of Work in a much shorter Time. Because Time is the denominator in the equation, a smaller time means a larger Power output.

**The Equation:** *P = W / t*

* **P:** Power, measured in **Watts (W)**. One Watt is equal to one Joule of work per second (J/s).
* **W:** Work, measured in Joules (J).
* **t:** Time, measured in seconds (s).

---

## 💡 What is a Watt?

The Watt is named after James Watt, the inventor of the steam engine. 
When you buy a 60-Watt lightbulb, it means that the bulb consumes 60 Joules of electrical energy every single second it is turned on. 
When you look at the engine specifications of a car, its power is often measured in Horsepower. (One Horsepower is exactly equal to 745.7 Watts).
