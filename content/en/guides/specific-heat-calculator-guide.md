# Specific Heat Calculator: Understanding Thermal Capacity

Have you ever noticed that if you leave a metal spoon and a wooden spoon in a pot of boiling soup, the metal spoon gets too hot to touch almost instantly, while the wooden spoon stays cool for a long time? 

This happens because different materials require different amounts of energy to change their temperature. In chemistry and physics, this property is called **Specific Heat Capacity**. In this guide, we will explore how it works and how to use our free [Specific Heat Calculator](/en/calculators/specific-heat-calculator).

---

## 🌡️ What is Specific Heat?

**Specific Heat ($c$)** is defined as the exact amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of exactly $1 \text{ gram}$ of a substance by $1^\circ\text{C}$ (or $1 \text{ Kelvin}$).

* **Low Specific Heat:** Materials like metals (iron, copper, aluminum) have low specific heat. It takes very little energy to heat them up, which is why we use them to make frying pans!
* **High Specific Heat:** Water has an incredibly high specific heat ($4.184 \text{ J/g}^\circ\text{C}$). It takes a massive amount of energy to change the temperature of water. This is why a pot of water takes forever to boil, and why oceans help regulate the Earth's climate.

---

## 🧮 The Heat Transfer Equation

To calculate how much energy is required to heat something up, or how much its temperature will change, we use the standard calorimetry equation.

**The Formula:**
**$q = m \times c \times \Delta T$**

* **$q$** = Heat energy (in Joules)
* **$m$** = Mass of the substance (in grams)
* **$c$** = Specific Heat Capacity
* **$\Delta T$** = Change in temperature ($T_{final} - T_{initial}$)

To find the Specific Heat ($c$) of an unknown substance, you simply rearrange the equation: $c = \frac{q}{(m \times \Delta T)}$.
