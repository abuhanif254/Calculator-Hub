# Electricity Cost: How Much Do Appliances Cost to Run?

Have you ever wondered exactly how much money it costs to leave a space heater running all night, or how much it costs to run a gaming PC 24/7? 

Your power company bills you based on the total amount of energy your home consumes, but deciphering the bill can be tricky. In this guide, we will break down the math behind your utility bill and show you how to use our free [Electricity Cost Calculator](/en/calculators/electricity-cost-calculator) to audit your home's power consumption.

---

## 🧾 Understanding Your Utility Bill

Utility companies do not bill you for the "Watts" you use; they bill you for the **Kilowatt-Hours (kWh)** you consume.
* **Watts (W):** A measure of how much power an appliance draws at a single moment in time.
* **Kilowatt-Hours (kWh):** A measure of how much total energy an appliance consumes over a period of time.

One Kilowatt-Hour is equal to 1,000 Watts of power being used continuously for exactly one hour. 
If your utility company charges you **$0.15 per kWh**, it means you have to pay 15 cents for every 1,000 Watts you sustain for an hour.

---

## 🖥️ Example: The Gaming PC vs The Space Heater

Let's do the math on two common appliances, assuming your utility rate is $0.15 per kWh.

**Appliance 1: A 1,500W Space Heater**
If you leave a 1500W space heater running for 8 hours while you sleep:
* 1500 Watts × 8 hours = 12,000 Watt-hours (or **12 kWh**).
* 12 kWh × $0.15 = **$1.80 per night.** 
*(If you do this every night in winter, it costs $54 a month!)*

**Appliance 2: A 500W Gaming PC**
If you leave a gaming PC running under a heavy load for 4 hours a day:
* 500 Watts × 4 hours = 2,000 Watt-hours (or **2 kWh**).
* 2 kWh × $0.15 = **$0.30 per day.**
*(This costs about $9 a month).*
