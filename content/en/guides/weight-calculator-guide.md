# Mass vs. Weight: What's the Difference?

In everyday conversation, we use the words "mass" and "weight" interchangeably. If someone asks how much you weigh, you might say "70 kilograms" or "150 pounds." 
However, in physics, these two words mean entirely different things! 

In this guide, we will finally clear up the confusion, explain why your weight changes depending on where you are in the universe, and show you how to use our free [Weight Calculator](/en/calculators/weight-calculator).

---

## 🧱 What is Mass?

**Mass** is the actual amount of matter (atoms and molecules) inside an object. 
* It is measured in **Kilograms (kg)**.
* Mass is an intrinsic property. It **never changes**, regardless of where you are. If your mass is 70 kg on Earth, it will be 70 kg on the Moon, and 70 kg floating in deep space. You are still made up of the exact same amount of atoms.

---

## ⚖️ What is Weight?

**Weight** is a Force. Specifically, it is the force of gravity pulling down on your mass. 
* Because it is a force, it is measured in **Newtons (N)**, not kilograms!
* Weight **changes** depending on the strength of gravity. 

If you travel to the Moon, where gravity is much weaker, gravity will not pull down on your mass as hard. Therefore, your *weight* will decrease drastically, even though your *mass* is identical!

**The Equation:** *W = m * g*
*(Weight = Mass × Gravity)*

---

## 🪐 Weight Across the Solar System

Let's take a 100 kg astronaut and see how much they weigh across the solar system (measured in Newtons):
* **On Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²):** Weight = 980 N
* **On the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²):** Weight = 162 N
* **On Mars (g = 3.71 m/s²):** Weight = 371 N
* **On Jupiter (g = 24.79 m/s²):** Weight = 2,479 N

If the astronaut tried to stand on Jupiter, their own skeleton would be crushed under the immense Weight of their own Mass!
