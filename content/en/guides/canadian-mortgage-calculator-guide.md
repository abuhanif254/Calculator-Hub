# Canadian Mortgages vs. US Mortgages: The Crucial Differences

If you read financial advice on the internet, 90% of it is written for Americans. But if you are buying a house in Toronto or Vancouver, applying standard American mortgage math will result in completely inaccurate payment estimations!

Canadian real estate financing operates under strict federal regulations that fundamentally change how interest is calculated and how loans are structured. In this guide, we will explore these crucial differences and show you how to use our [Canadian Mortgage Calculator](/en/calculators/canadian-mortgage-calculator) to get accurate numbers.

---

## 🍁 Difference #1: Semi-Annual Compounding

This is the most critical mathematical difference between the two countries.
* **In the US:** Mortgage interest is compounded **monthly**.
* **In Canada:** By law, fixed-rate mortgage interest must be compounded **semi-annually** (twice a year), even though you still make your payments monthly.

Because the interest is compounded less frequently, the *effective* interest rate in Canada is slightly lower than the stated rate! If a Canadian bank quotes you a 5.00% fixed rate, your actual effective rate for payment calculation is roughly 4.95%. 

*(Note: Variable-rate mortgages in Canada are still compounded monthly, just like in the US).*

---

## 🍁 Difference #2: The 5-Year Renewal Cycle

In the US, it is standard to lock in an interest rate for a full 30 years. You know exactly what your payment will be in year 29.

In Canada, this is practically unheard of. 
* Canadian mortgages are typically **amortized over 25 years**, but the interest rate is only locked in for a **term of 1 to 5 years** (with 5 years being the most popular).
* At the end of your 5-year term, your mortgage matures. You must **renew** the remaining balance at whatever the current market interest rate happens to be.
* This means Canadian homeowners face significant "interest rate shock" every 5 years if rates have gone up since they originally bought the house!

---

## 🍁 Difference #3: CMHC Mortgage Insurance

If you put less than 20% down on a house in Canada, you are legally required to purchase mortgage default insurance through the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) or a private insurer. 

* The premium ranges from **2.80% to 4.00%** of your loan amount, depending on the size of your down payment.
* Unlike American PMI (which is a separate monthly fee that can eventually be cancelled), the CMHC premium is usually added directly to your total mortgage principal, meaning you pay interest on the insurance fee for the entire 25-year amortization period!
