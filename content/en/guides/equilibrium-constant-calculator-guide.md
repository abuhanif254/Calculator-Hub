# Equilibrium Constant Calculator: Measuring Chemical Balance

In introductory chemistry, you are taught that reactions go in one direction: Reactants turn into Products, and then the reaction stops. 

In reality, many chemical reactions are **reversible**. As the reactants form products, the products simultaneously break back apart into reactants! Eventually, the reaction settles into a state of balance called "Dynamic Equilibrium". In this guide, we will explain how chemists measure this balance using the **Equilibrium Constant ($K_{eq}$)**, and how to use our free [Equilibrium Constant Calculator](/en/calculators/equilibrium-constant-calculator).

---

## ⚖️ What is Dynamic Equilibrium?

Dynamic equilibrium occurs when the forward reaction (making products) and the reverse reaction (breaking products back into reactants) are happening at the exact same speed.

Because they are happening at the same speed, the overall amount of stuff in the beaker stops changing. It looks like the reaction has completely stopped, but on a microscopic level, both reactions are still raging!

---

## 🧮 The Equilibrium Constant (Keq)

To measure where the "balance point" of a reaction lies, chemists calculate $K_{eq}$. 
It is simply the ratio of **Products divided by Reactants** at equilibrium.

**The Formula:**
For the general reaction: $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$
**$K_{eq} = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}$**

*(Note: The lowercase letters represent the stoichiometric coefficients from the balanced equation).*

**What does the number mean?**
* **$K_{eq} > 1$:** The reaction favors the products. (The forward reaction "won").
* **$K_{eq} < 1$:** The reaction favors the reactants. (The reverse reaction "won").
* **$K_{eq} = 1$:** The products and reactants are perfectly balanced.
