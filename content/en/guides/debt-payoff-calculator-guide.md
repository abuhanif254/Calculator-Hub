# Debt Snowball vs. Avalanche: The Ultimate Payoff Strategy

If you have multiple debts, you should never try to pay them all off at the exact same time. If you spread your extra cash across five different credit cards, you will make agonizingly slow progress on all of them.

To get out of debt quickly, you must make the minimum payments on everything, and ruthlessly attack **one debt at a time** with every spare dollar you have. 

But which debt do you attack first? There are two primary strategies. In this guide, we will compare them and show you how to use our free [Debt Payoff Calculator](/en/calculators/debt-payoff-calculator) to build your roadmap to financial freedom.

---

## ⛄ The Debt Snowball (Behavioral Strategy)

Popularized by financial guru Dave Ramsey, the Debt Snowball ignores interest rates entirely. 
**The Rule:** You attack the debt with the **smallest balance** first.

* **Example:** You have a $500 medical bill (0% APR) and a $10,000 credit card (25% APR). 
* **The Strategy:** You pay minimums on the credit card and throw all your extra money at the $500 medical bill until it is gone.

**Why it works:** Human beings are not math equations. We are emotional creatures. Getting out of debt requires intense motivation. By knocking out small debts quickly, you get immediate psychological "wins" that give you the momentum to tackle the bigger mountains later.

---

## ⛰️ The Debt Avalanche (Mathematical Strategy)

The Debt Avalanche ignores balances entirely.
**The Rule:** You attack the debt with the **highest interest rate** first.

* **Example:** You have a $500 medical bill (0% APR) and a $10,000 credit card (25% APR).
* **The Strategy:** You pay minimums on the medical bill and throw all your extra money at the 25% credit card.

**Why it works:** This is the mathematically optimal path. Because you are killing the highest-interest debts first, you will pay less total interest to the banks, and you will become debt-free several months earlier than if you used the Snowball method. 

---

## ⚖️ Which One Should You Choose?

* If you are easily discouraged and need to feel progress quickly, use the **Snowball**.
* If you are a spreadsheet nerd with iron discipline, use the **Avalanche**. 

Ultimately, the best debt payoff plan is simply the one you can stick to.
