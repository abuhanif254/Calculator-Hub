# The Ultimate Guide to Calculating Sales Tax

Whether you are a consumer trying to budget for a large purchase or a business owner pricing your products, understanding sales tax is unavoidable. 

Unlike VAT (Value-Added Tax) used in Europe, the US sales tax system is highly fragmented. In this guide, we will break down the math and show you how to use our [Sales Tax Calculator](/en/calculators/sales-tax-calculator) to get accurate numbers every time.

---

## 🏛️ How the US Sales Tax System Works

In the United States, there is no federal sales tax. Instead, sales tax is levied at the state level, and often at the local (county or city) level as well.

### The Combined Rate
When you buy a product, the total tax you pay is usually a combination of:
1. **State Tax** (e.g., California's 7.25%)
2. **County Tax** (e.g., Los Angeles County's 1.00%)
3. **City/District Tax** (e.g., Santa Monica's 1.25%)
* **Total Combined Rate:** 9.50%

---

## 🧮 Calculating Sales Tax (The Formula)

Calculating the tax amount on a purchase requires a simple percentage calculation.

**Formula: Tax Amount = Price × (Tax Rate / 100)**

*Example:* You buy a $500 TV in a city with a 9.5% sales tax rate.
* Tax Amount = 500 × (9.5 / 100)
* Tax Amount = 500 × 0.095 = **$47.50**

**Formula: Total Cost = Price + Tax Amount**
* Total Cost = $500 + $47.50 = **$547.50**

---

## 🛒 For Business Owners: Origin vs. Destination Sourcing

If you run an e-commerce business, charging the correct tax is critical for compliance. Most states use **Destination-Based Sourcing**, meaning you charge the tax rate of the state/city where the *buyer* is located. A minority of states use **Origin-Based Sourcing**, meaning you charge the tax rate of the state where *your business* is located.
