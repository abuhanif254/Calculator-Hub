# Electrical Power: Calculating Watts (P=VI)

Have you ever wondered why a microwave heats up food so quickly, while a tiny nightlight barely produces any heat at all? The answer lies in their **Electrical Power** consumption. 

In this guide, we will break down the concept of Watts, explain the famous Power equation (P=VI), and show you how to use our free [Electrical Power Calculator](/en/calculators/electrical-power-calculator).

---

## 💡 What is Electrical Power?

In electronics, Power is the rate at which electrical energy is converted into another form of energy (like heat, light, or mechanical motion). 

It is measured in **Watts (W)**. 
A 1000-Watt microwave converts electrical energy into heat energy 100 times faster than a 10-Watt LED bulb converts electrical energy into light energy.

---

## 📐 The Power Equation (P = VI)

Often referred to as *Watt's Law*, the formula for electrical power is beautifully simple. It connects Power directly to Ohm's Law.

**P = V * I**

* **P:** Power, measured in **Watts (W)**.
* **V:** Voltage (pressure), measured in Volts (V).
* **I:** Current (flow), measured in Amperes (A).

Because Power is Voltage multiplied by Current, you can achieve the exact same Power output using different combinations.
For example, a **1200W** appliance could run on:
* 120 Volts at 10 Amps (Standard US outlet)
* 240 Volts at 5 Amps (Standard European outlet)

Both scenarios provide the exact same amount of electrical power!

---

## ⚡ Power vs. Energy (Watts vs. Joules)

It is crucial to understand the difference between Power and Energy.
* **Energy (Joules):** The total amount of work done.
* **Power (Watts):** How *fast* that energy is used (Joules per second).

If you leave a 100-Watt bulb on for 1 second, it consumes 100 Joules of energy. If you leave it on for 10 seconds, it consumes 1000 Joules of energy. The *Power* (100W) never changed, but the total *Energy* consumed increased over time.
