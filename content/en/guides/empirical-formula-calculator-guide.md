# Empirical Formula: The Simplest Ratio

Imagine you are a forensic chemist and you find a mysterious white powder. You run it through a mass spectrometer, which tells you the powder is 40.0% Carbon, 6.7% Hydrogen, and 53.3% Oxygen by mass. 

But what actually *is* the chemical? To find out, you must calculate its **Empirical Formula**. In this guide, we will explain what that means and show you how to use our free [Empirical Formula Calculator](/en/calculators/empirical-formula-calculator).

---

## 🔬 What is an Empirical Formula?

In chemistry, an empirical formula is the **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms in a compound. It doesn't tell you exactly how many atoms are in one molecule, but it tells you the exact *proportion* of those atoms.

For example, Hydrogen Peroxide has a true molecular formula of $H_2O_2$. 
But its empirical formula is just **HO**. The ratio of Hydrogen to Oxygen is simply 1 to 1!

---

## 🧮 Calculating It From Mass Percentages

Let's return to our mysterious white powder (40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O).

**Step 1: Assume 100 grams of powder**
If we have exactly 100g, then the percentages simply become grams:
* Carbon = 40.0 g
* Hydrogen = 6.7 g
* Oxygen = 53.3 g

**Step 2: Convert grams to Moles**
Divide each weight by the element's atomic mass (from the periodic table).
* C: 40.0 / 12.01 = 3.33 moles
* H: 6.7 / 1.008 = 6.64 moles
* O: 53.3 / 16.00 = 3.33 moles

**Step 3: Divide by the smallest number**
To find the simplest ratio, divide all the mole values by the smallest mole value you just found (3.33).
* C: 3.33 / 3.33 = **1**
* H: 6.64 / 3.33 = **2**
* O: 3.33 / 3.33 = **1**

The simplest ratio is 1:2:1. Our empirical formula is **$CH_2O$**. 
*(Fun fact: This is the empirical formula for carbohydrates like Glucose!)*
