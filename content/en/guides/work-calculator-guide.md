# The Physics of Work: Force and Displacement

In everyday language, if you spend three hours pushing against a solid brick wall, you did a lot of "work" and you will be exhausted. In physics, however, you did absolutely zero Work. 

In physics, **Work** has a very strict, mathematical definition. It only occurs when a force causes an object to move. In this guide, we will break down the formula, explain the concept of displacement, and show you how to use our free [Work Calculator](/en/calculators/work-calculator).

---

## 🧱 The Scientific Definition of Work

For Work to occur, two things must happen:
1. You must apply a **Force** to an object.
2. The object must **Move** (Displacement) in the direction of the force.

If the object does not move, no Work was done, regardless of how much energy you expended. This is why pushing against a brick wall equals zero Work.

**The Equation:** *W = F * d*

* **W:** Work, measured in **Joules (J)**.
* **F:** Force, measured in Newtons (N).
* **d:** Displacement (distance), measured in meters (m).

---

## 📐 Angles and Work

The basic formula (W = F * d) assumes you are pushing perfectly in the same direction the object is moving. But what if you are pulling a heavy suitcase by a strap at a 45-degree angle?

Only the horizontal part of your force actually contributes to moving the suitcase forward. The vertical part of your force (pulling up on the strap) does no work because the suitcase isn't moving up! 

To account for this, the full physics formula is:
**W = F * d * cos(θ)**
*(Where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement).*

---

## 🏋️ Work vs. Energy

Work and Energy are intimately connected. In fact, they are both measured in the exact same unit: the **Joule**.
Doing Work on an object transfers Energy into that object. When you lift a barbell off the ground, you are doing Work, which transfers Gravitational Potential Energy into the barbell!
