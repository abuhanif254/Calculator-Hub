# QR Code Best Practices: Scannability, Formats, and Error Correction

QR (Quick Response) codes have become an essential bridge between the physical and digital worlds. Whether you are adding a QR code to a restaurant menu, a business card, or a massive billboard, ensuring it scans reliably for every user is critical.

In this guide, we will explore the anatomy of a QR code, common mistakes that cause scanning failures, and how to use our free [QR Code Studio](/en/tools/qr-code-studio) to generate professional-grade codes.

---

## 🏗️ The Anatomy of a QR Code

A QR code is more than just random static. It contains specific structural elements that allow smartphone cameras to read them instantly:
* **Positioning Squares (Finder Patterns):** The three large squares in the corners. They tell the scanner where the code is and its orientation.
* **Alignment Patterns:** Smaller squares inside the code that help the scanner read the code even if it's distorted or printed on a curved surface (like a bottle).
* **Quiet Zone:** The blank margin around the QR code. This is crucial—without this white space, the scanner cannot distinguish the code from its surroundings.

---

## ⚙️ Understanding Error Correction Levels

One of the most powerful features of a QR code is **Error Correction**. This mathematical algorithm (Reed-Solomon) allows a QR code to remain scannable even if part of it is damaged, dirty, or covered up (for example, by a custom logo).

When generating a code in QR Code Studio, you can choose from four levels:
1. **Level L (Low):** Restores ~7% of data. Best for minimal, dense codes where space is tight.
2. **Level M (Medium):** Restores ~15% of data. The standard setting for most general purposes.
3. **Level Q (Quartile):** Restores ~25% of data. Good for industrial environments where codes might get scratched.
4. **Level H (High):** Restores ~30% of data. Essential if you are embedding a custom logo right in the middle of the QR code.

*Note: Higher error correction makes the QR code visually denser (more "pixels").*

## 🎨 Best Practices for Scannability

Even the highest error correction won't save a poorly designed QR code. Follow these rules to ensure a 100% scan rate:

### 1. High Contrast is Mandatory
Smartphone cameras need contrast to separate the "modules" (the black dots) from the background. Always use a dark color on a light background. Never use a light color on a dark background (inverted QR code), as many older scanners cannot read them.

### 2. Respect the Quiet Zone
Leave a margin of at least 4 modules (pixels) around the entire QR code. If you print a QR code with text touching its edges, it will fail to scan.

### 3. Sizing for Print
A good rule of thumb for print sizing is the 10:1 ratio. The scanning distance should be 10 times the size of the QR code. For example, if your QR code is 1 inch (2.5 cm) wide, it will scan perfectly from 10 inches (25 cm) away.

## 📂 Formats: SVG vs. PNG

When exporting your code from our QR Code Studio, you must choose the right file format based on your use case:

* **SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics):** Use this for **Print**. An SVG is a mathematical formula, meaning you can scale it up to the size of a skyscraper without it ever getting blurry. Always use SVG if sending to a professional printer or placing it in Adobe Illustrator.
* **PNG (Portable Network Graphics):** Use this for the **Web**. PNG is a raster format (made of pixels). It is perfect for email signatures, websites, or social media posts. Our tool exports high-resolution PNGs with transparent backgrounds.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Do QR codes expire?
Static QR codes (like the ones generated by our tool) **never expire**. The data is hardcoded into the pattern itself. As long as the destination URL remains active, the QR code will work forever.

### Can I track who scans my QR code?
Our QR Code Studio generates static, privacy-focused codes, meaning we do not track your users. If you need analytics (like location or scan counts), you will need to create a Dynamic QR Code using a specialized marketing service.

### Why won't my logo QR code scan?
If you added a logo, you likely covered up too much of the data. To fix this, increase the Error Correction level to **H (High)** and make sure your logo does not cover the three large positioning squares in the corners.
