# The Truth About Debt Consolidation: How to Lower Your Interest Rates

Juggling multiple credit card payments, a personal loan, and a medical bill every month is stressful. But the real problem isn't the number of bills—it's the interest rates. Credit cards often charge 20% to 25% APR, making it mathematically impossible to pay off the principal balance if you only make the minimum payments.

Debt consolidation is a strategy to combine all of these high-interest debts into one single monthly payment with a much lower interest rate. In this guide, we will break down the mechanics of consolidation and show you how to use our free [Debt Consolidation Calculator](/en/calculators/debt-consolidation-calculator).

---

## 🔄 How Debt Consolidation Works

When you consolidate your debt, you are taking out a brand new loan to pay off all your existing smaller loans. 

**For example:**
* You have $5,000 on Credit Card A (22% APR).
* You have $3,000 on Credit Card B (24% APR).
* You have a $2,000 personal loan (15% APR).

Instead of making three separate payments at high rates, you go to a bank or credit union and take out a single **$10,000 Debt Consolidation Loan**. If your credit score is decent, the bank might offer you a **10% APR**.
You use that $10,000 to instantly pay off the credit cards and the personal loan. Now, you only owe the bank, and your interest rate has been slashed by more than half!

---

## 🛑 The Hidden Danger: Running Up the Cards Again

Debt consolidation works mathematically, but it often fails behaviorally. 

When you use a consolidation loan to pay off your credit cards, those credit cards suddenly have a $0 balance. For many people, this feels like "free money." They continue spending recklessly on the empty credit cards, while *also* paying the monthly bill for the new consolidation loan.
**If you do not change your spending habits, debt consolidation will actually put you in twice as much debt.**

---

## 💳 Balance Transfer Cards vs. Personal Loans

There are two main ways to consolidate debt:

1. **Personal Consolidation Loans:** You get a fixed interest rate (e.g., 10%) and a fixed timeline to pay it off (e.g., 36 months). This forces you to make steady progress.
2. **0% Balance Transfer Credit Cards:** Some credit cards offer a 0% introductory APR for 12 to 18 months if you transfer your existing balances to them. This is incredible for saving money on interest, *but* there is a massive catch. They usually charge a 3% to 5% transfer fee upfront. Worse, if you don't pay off the entire balance before the 18 months are up, the interest rate often skyrockets back to 25%, and some cards retroactively charge you for all the interest you "saved"!
