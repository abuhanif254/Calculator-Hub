You do not need a **20% down payment** to purchase a home. Qualified conventional buyers routinely close with **3% to 5% down**, FHA buyers put down **3.5%**, and eligible military or rural borrowers qualify for **0% down**—putting the median first-time buyer down payment well below 10%.

The 20% benchmark exists solely because a `Loan-to-Value (LTV)` ratio of `80%` or lower eliminates **Private Mortgage Insurance (`PMI`)**, a policy that protects the lender against borrower default. Waiting years to assemble 20% cash solely to dodge temporary `PMI` often backfires when home prices rise faster than savings, or when it forces you to strip essential liquidity after closing. ## Loan-to-Value (LTV), PMI Mechanics, and Total Cash-to-Close

Every down payment decision balances three variables: upfront cash required, interest rate pricing (`LLPA` adjustments), and monthly mortgage insurance. Lenders measure equity risk through the initial `LTV` ratio:

```text
LTV         = (Purchase Price − Down Payment) / Purchase Price
Monthly PMI = (Loan Balance × Annual_PMI_Rate) / 12
Cash Needed = Down Payment + Closing Costs (2%–5% of Price) + Post-Closing Reserves
```

- **Upfront Equity & Rate Pricing:** Higher `LTV` loans (`95%`–`97%`) carry slightly higher interest-rate add-ons for borrowers with mid-tier credit scores, whereas putting `20%` down (`80%` LTV) often unlocks a `0.125%` to `0.250%` rate improvement.
- **PMI Rate Tiers:** For conventional loans, annual `PMI` scales steeply with credit score and `LTV`. A `760+` FICO borrower putting `5%` down might pay `0.45%`–`0.65%`, whereas a `660` FICO borrower at `3%` down could pay `1.20%`–`1.50%`.
- **Automatic Cancellation (HPA):** Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, conventional `PMI` is temporary. Borrowers may petition for removal at `80%` scheduled or appraised `LTV` (typically requiring 2 years of seasoning if relying on appreciation), and servicers must cancel it automatically once principal amortizes to `78%` of the original purchase price.

## Step-by-Step Worked Example: 5% vs. 20% Down on a $450,000 Home

Suppose you are buying a `$450,000` home with strong credit (`740+` FICO), comparing a `5%` conventional down payment against `20%` down, assuming `3%` closing costs (`$13,500`) in both cases.

### Option 1: 5% Down Payment ($22,500 Down)

1. **Cash at Closing:** `$22,500` down payment + `$13,500` closing costs = **`$36,000`** total cash required.
2. **Initial Loan & Rate:** `$427,500` loan (`95% LTV`) at `6.500%` for 30 years yields a monthly `P&I` of **`$2,702.09`**.
3. **Monthly PMI (`0.65%`):** `($427,500 × 0.0065) / 12 = $231.56/month`.
4. **Combined Monthly P&I + PMI:** **`$2,933.65/month`**. At standard amortization (without appreciation), the balance reaches `78% LTV` (`$351,000`) around month `103` (`8.6 years`), meaning cumulative `PMI` paid before automatic cancellation totals roughly `$23,850`—or far less if appreciation allows removal at year 3.

### Option 2: 20% Down Payment ($90,000 Down)

1. **Cash at Closing:** `$90,000` down payment + `$13,500` closing costs = **`$103,500`** total cash required (`$67,500` more upfront).
2. **Initial Loan & Rate:** `$360,000` loan (`80% LTV`) at `6.375%` (`0.125%` pricing improvement) yields **`$2,245.93/month`** with `$0` PMI.
3. **Opportunity Cost Check:** Putting `5%` down saves `$67,500` in upfront liquidity. Even if paying `$231.56/month` in PMI costs `$11,115` over 4 years before hitting `80% LTV` via amortization and appreciation, keeping `$67,500` invested at `7%` yields roughly `$21,000` in compound gains—while entering the market four years sooner avoids missing `4%` annual home appreciation (`+$76,400` in price escalation).

## Real-World Strategy, Traps, and Regulatory Caveats

### Three Common Down Payment Mistakes

- **FHA Trap for High-Credit Borrowers:** Because FHA rates often look `0.25%` lower than conventional quotes, high-credit (`720+`) buyers sometimes choose FHA `3.5%` over conventional `5%`. However, FHA's `1.75%` upfront fee adds `$7,599` to the debt immediately, and annual `0.55%` MIP never drops off under `10%` down without a costly refinance. Conventional wins overwhelmingly above a `700` credit score.
- **Zero-Reserve Closing:** Spending every last dollar to hit `20%` down leaves you vulnerable to immediate post-closing expenses—moving, locks, appliances, and deductibles. A `10%` down payment paired with `$45,000` in liquid reserves is structurally safer than `20%` down with `$2,000` in checking.
- **Raiding Retirement Accounts:** Borrowing or withdrawing from retirement accounts sacrifices tax-advantaged compounding within 2026 statutory limits (`$24,500` 401(k), `$7,500` IRA, `$4,400`/`$8,750` HSA). While first-time buyers can withdraw up to `$10,000` penalty-free from an IRA (income taxes still apply on traditional IRAs), reducing cash down to `5%`–`10%` keeps retirement capital intact.
- **Overlooking Zero-Down Entitlements:** Eligible veterans, active-duty personnel, and qualifying surviving spouses should benchmark a `0%` down VA loan (`1.25%`–`3.30%` funding fee, `$0` monthly PMI), while rural buyers meeting county and income caps should check `0%` down USDA financing.
