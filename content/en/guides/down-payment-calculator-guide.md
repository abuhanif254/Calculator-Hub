# The 20% Myth: How Much Down Payment Do You Really Need?

For generations, homebuyers were told they absolutely must save a 20% down payment before buying a house. In today's market, saving 20% for a $400,000 home ($80,000 in cash) could take a decade, during which house prices will likely continue to rise, leaving you permanently priced out.

The truth is, you do not need 20% to buy a house. In fact, the median down payment for first-time buyers is currently just 6%. In this guide, we will break down the pros and cons of smaller down payments and show you how to use our free [Down Payment Calculator](/en/calculators/down-payment-calculator) to test different scenarios.

---

## 🛑 The 20% Rule and PMI

The reason 20% is the "golden rule" is because of **Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)**. 

If you put down less than 20%, the bank considers you a high-risk borrower. If the housing market crashes and you default, the bank wants to ensure they can sell the house and get all their money back. To protect themselves, they force you to pay for PMI every month.
* PMI usually costs between **0.5% and 1.5%** of your total loan amount per year.
* *Example:* On a $300,000 loan, PMI could cost you an extra **$250 every single month**.
* This money protects the bank, not you, and is completely unrecoverable. 

Once you reach 20% equity in your home (either by paying down the principal or through the home's value appreciating), you can request to have the PMI removed.

---

## 📉 Low Down Payment Options

If you don't have 20%, you have plenty of government-backed options:

1. **Conventional Loans (3% to 5%):** Many conventional loans only require 3% down for first-time buyers. You will pay PMI, but the overall interest rates are highly competitive.
2. **FHA Loans (3.5%):** Backed by the Federal Housing Administration. These are great for buyers with lower credit scores. However, the mortgage insurance (MIP) is often permanent and cannot be removed without refinancing the entire loan.
3. **VA Loans (0%):** Available to veterans and active military. No down payment required, and absolutely no PMI!
4. **USDA Loans (0%):** Designed to encourage rural development. Available to low-to-moderate-income buyers purchasing homes in designated rural areas. 

---

## ⚖️ The Opportunity Cost of Saving 20%

Is it worth waiting 5 years to save 20% just to avoid a $200/month PMI fee? Often, the answer is no.

If you wait 5 years, the house you want might appreciate from $400,000 to $500,000. You avoided PMI, but the house now costs you $100,000 more! It is often mathematically smarter to buy *now* with 5% down, pay the PMI for a few years, and ride the wave of housing appreciation.
