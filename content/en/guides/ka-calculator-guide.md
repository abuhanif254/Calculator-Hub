# Ka Calculator: The Acid Dissociation Constant

When you drop a strong acid (like Hydrochloric Acid) into water, every single molecule breaks apart (dissociates) into ions. But when you drop a *weak* acid (like Acetic Acid) into water, only a tiny fraction of the molecules break apart. 

To measure exactly what percentage of a weak acid breaks apart, chemists calculate the **Acid Dissociation Constant ($K_a$)**. In this guide, we will explain the chemistry behind $K_a$ and how to use our free [Ka Calculator](/en/calculators/ka-calculator).

---

## 🧪 What Does Ka Tell Us?

The $K_a$ value is a ratio of **Products over Reactants**. 
When a weak acid ($HA$) breaks apart in water, it forms Hydrogen ions ($H^+$) and Conjugate Base ions ($A^-$).

* **Large $K_a$:** The acid easily breaks apart, creating lots of products ($H^+$ ions). This means it is a relatively strong acid.
* **Small $K_a$:** The acid resists breaking apart, creating very few products. This means it is a weak acid.

*(Note: Most weak acids have incredibly small $K_a$ values, such as $1.8 \times 10^{-5}$).*

---

## 🧮 The Ka Formula

To calculate the constant, you simply multiply the concentrations of the products and divide by the concentration of the leftover, unbroken acid.

**The Formula:**
**$K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}$**

* **$[H^+]$** = Molar concentration of Hydrogen ions.
* **$[A^-]$** = Molar concentration of the Conjugate Base.
* **$[HA]$** = Molar concentration of the unbroken Weak Acid at equilibrium.

Chemists usually find these exact concentrations by setting up an **ICE Table** (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) in the lab.
