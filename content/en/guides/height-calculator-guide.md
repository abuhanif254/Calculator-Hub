# How Tall Will My Child Be? The Science of Height Prediction

From the moment a child takes their first steps, parents are obsessed with tracking their growth. Door frames across the world are covered in pencil marks marking physical milestones. But how tall will they actually be when they stop growing?

While we can't predict the future with 100% certainty, pediatricians and geneticists have developed mathematical models that are surprisingly accurate. In this guide, we will explore the science of human growth and show you how to use our free [Height Calculator](/en/calculators/height-calculator) to predict your child's final adult height.

---

## 🧬 The Genetics of Height

Height is a highly heritable trait. Scientists estimate that roughly **80% of an individual's height is determined by the DNA sequence** they inherited from their parents. 

The remaining 20% is determined by environmental factors, primarily childhood nutrition and overall health. A child who suffers from severe malnutrition or chronic illness during critical growth windows may not reach their full genetic height potential.

Because genetics play such a massive role, the easiest way to predict a child's height is to look directly at the parents.

---

## 🧮 The Mid-Parental Height Formula

The Mid-Parental formula is the oldest and simplest way to predict a child's height. It assumes the child will end up somewhere in the middle of the mother and father's heights, adjusted for biological sex.

**For a Boy:**
1. Add the mother's height and the father's height (in inches).
2. Add **5 inches**.
3. Divide by 2.

**For a Girl:**
1. Add the mother's height and the father's height (in inches).
2. Subtract **5 inches**.
3. Divide by 2.

*Margin of Error:* This simple formula usually predicts final height with a margin of error of about 4 inches in either direction.

---

## 📈 The Khamis-Roche Method

If you want a more accurate prediction, the Khamis-Roche method is the modern gold standard used by pediatricians. 

Instead of just looking at the parents, this complex algorithm looks at the child's *current* growth trajectory. It requires three data points:
1. The child's current height.
2. The child's current weight.
3. The mid-parental height.

By comparing the child's current physical measurements against standardized growth charts, the Khamis-Roche method can predict adult height with a much smaller margin of error, especially if the child is between the ages of 4 and 9.
