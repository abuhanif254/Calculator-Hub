Estimating concrete accurately is both a budget calculation and a strict curing-time race. Wet concrete begins hydrating immediately; running short mid-pour stalls placement while the first section sets, creating a weak **cold joint** vulnerable to cracking and frost heave. Conversely, over-ordering ready-mix wastes `$140 to $200` per unused cubic yard and creates difficult hardened-waste disposal.

North American ready-mix is sold by the **cubic yard** (`27 cubic feet`, or `0.7646 cubic meters`), whereas bagged mix is sold by weight (`60 lb` and `80 lb` bags yielding `0.45` and `0.60` cubic feet). ## Geometric Volume Formulas and Unit Conversions

Before converting to cubic yards or bags, standardize every dimension to **feet** (divide inches by `12`) or **meters** (divide centimeters by `100`):

```text
Rectangular Slab / Footing:
  V_ft³ = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × [Thickness (in) / 12]

Cylindrical Column / Sonotube / Pier:
  V_ft³ = π × [Diameter (in) / 24]² × Depth (ft)

Conversions & Waste Allowance:
  V_yd³     = V_ft³ / 27
  V_m³      = V_yd³ × 0.7646
  Order_yd³ = V_yd³ × 1.10        (10% subgrade & spillage margin)
  80 lb Bag = (Order_yd³ × 27) / 0.60
  60 lb Bag = (Order_yd³ × 27) / 0.45
```

### Bagged Yield vs. Dry Weight

Dry bags (`60 lb / 27 kg` and `80 lb / 36 kg`) differ in wet yield: `80 lb` bags yield **0.60 ft³** (`45 bags/yd³`), while `60 lb` bags yield **0.45 ft³** (`60 bags/yd³`). Always calculate bag requirements from printed wet-volume yield rather than dividing cured concrete density (`~4,050 lb/yd³`) by bag weight.

## Step-by-Step Worked Patio and Footing Calculation

Suppose you are pouring a **12 ft × 16 ft** patio at **4 inches** thick alongside **four cylindrical pergola footings**, each **12 inches in diameter** and **36 inches (3 ft) deep**.

1. **Slab Volume:** Convert 4 inches to feet (`4 / 12 = 0.3333 ft`). Multiply: `12 ft × 16 ft × 0.3333 ft = 64.00 ft³`.
2. **Footing Volume:** A 12-inch diameter gives radius `0.50 ft`. One 3-foot pier equals `3.1416 × (0.50)² × 3 = 2.356 ft³`; four piers total `4 × 2.356 = 9.42 ft³`.
3. **Total Net Cubic Yards:** Combine volumes (`64.00 + 9.42 = 73.42 ft³`) and divide by 27: `73.42 / 27 = 2.719 net yd³`.
4. **Apply 10% Contingency:** `2.719 × 1.10 = 2.991 yd³` (`2.29 m³`). Round up and order **3.00 cubic yards** from a ready-mix plant.
5. **Bags vs. Ready-Mix Decision:** At `0.60 ft³` per 80 lb bag, `2.991 yd³` (`80.76 ft³`) requires `80.76 / 0.60 = 135 bags` (**10,800 lb**, or `4.9 metric tons`), or `180 bags` of 60 lb mix. Hand-mixing 135 bags risks cold joints across a monolithic slab, making a 3.00-yard ready-mix delivery far safer.

## Subgrade Prep, Mix Ratios, and Field Pitfalls

Accurate geometry still fails on site if excavation depth or water ratios drift:

- **Subgrade Low Spots:** A **0.5-inch depression** beneath a 20 ft × 20 ft pad consumes `400 × (0.5 / 12) = 16.67 ft³` (**0.62 yd³**), erasing almost the entire 10% buffer. Compact and screed a 4-inch crushed gravel base before pouring.
- **Short-Load Fees:** Ready-mix plants often charge `$75–$175` short-load fees under 3–5 cubic yards. For 1.0–2.5 yd³ jobs, compare ready-mix totals against metered **volumetric concrete trailers** or U-cart rentals billed strictly by dispensed yardage.
- **Excess Water Weakening:** Adding extra water for easier screeding slashes 28-day PSI strength and causes surface scaling. Stick to manufacturer water limits (`~3 quarts` / `2.8 L` per 80 lb bag) and specify **4,000 PSI air-entrained concrete** (5%–7% air) in freeze-thaw regions.
- **Post Displacement:** When setting fence posts, subtract buried `4×4` timber volume (`0.085 ft³/ft`) across large runs and slope the concrete collar slightly above grade to shed water.
