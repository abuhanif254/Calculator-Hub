# Currency Calculator: Exchange Rates Explained

If you travel from the United States to Europe, you cannot buy a coffee with a US Dollar. You must exchange your Dollars for Euros. But the amount of Euros you receive changes every single day!

Why does money constantly change value? In this guide, we will explain the foreign exchange market (Forex), what a bank spread is, and how to use our live [Currency Calculator](/en/calculators/currency-calculator).

---

## 💱 How Are Exchange Rates Set?

Unlike a stock price which is set by a single exchange (like the NYSE), currencies are traded on the **Foreign Exchange (Forex) Market**, a decentralized global network of banks. 

A currency's value is purely based on **supply and demand**.
If the US economy is booming, foreign investors want to invest their money in American businesses. To do that, they must buy US Dollars. Because millions of people are trying to buy Dollars at the same time, the "price" of the Dollar goes up compared to other currencies.

Conversely, if a country prints too much money (inflation), the supply of that currency is too high, and its value drops.

---

## 🏦 The Hidden Fee: The Spread

If the official exchange rate is **1 USD = 0.90 EUR**, and you give a bank 100 USD, you *should* get 90 EUR. But if you actually do this at an airport kiosk, they might only give you 85 EUR! Where did the other 5 Euros go?

This is called the **Spread**.
The official rate you see on Google is the "Mid-Market Rate" (the rate banks use to trade with *each other*). 
When a bank trades with a normal consumer, they offer a slightly worse exchange rate, pocketing the difference as profit. Always check the spread before exchanging cash!
