# Graphing Functions: Parabolas and Slopes

Math can sometimes feel like a bunch of meaningless numbers on a page. **Graphing** is the tool we use to bring those numbers to life, allowing us to physically see the relationship between variables. 

In this guide, we will explain the basics of graphing linear and quadratic equations, and show you how to use our free [Graphing Calculator](/en/calculators/graphing-calculator) to visualize your homework.

---

## 📈 Linear Equations (y = mx + b)

A linear equation will always draw a perfectly straight line on a graph. The standard format for a linear equation is **y = mx + b**.

* **y and x:** The coordinates on the grid. (x goes left/right, y goes up/down).
* **b (The y-intercept):** This is exactly where the line crosses the vertical y-axis. If the equation is *y = 2x + 5*, the line will cross the y-axis at exactly 5.
* **m (The Slope):** This tells you how steep the line is. It is often called "rise over run." A slope of 2 means that for every 1 step you move to the right, the line goes up 2 steps. 

---

## 🎢 Quadratic Equations (Parabolas)

If you add an exponent to your x variable (like **x²**), the line will no longer be straight. It will curve into a U-shape called a **Parabola**. 

Quadratic equations are used constantly in physics to calculate the trajectory of thrown objects. 
* If you throw a baseball into the air, its path is a perfect parabola. 
* The peak of the parabola (the vertex) is the maximum height the ball reaches before gravity pulls it back down.
