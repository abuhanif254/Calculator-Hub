# Rent vs. Buy: The 5% Rule and Unrecoverable Costs

"Renting is just throwing money away!" You have likely heard this from parents, real estate agents, and financial gurus. The logic seems sound: when you rent, you pay your landlord's mortgage. When you buy, you build your own equity. 

However, this is a massive oversimplification. Buying a house comes with massive **unrecoverable costs** that you will never see again. In this guide, we will break down the math of renting vs. buying and show you how to use our free [Rent vs. Buy Calculator](/en/calculators/rent-vs-buy-calculator) to make the right choice for your local market.

---

## 💸 What are "Unrecoverable Costs"?

An unrecoverable cost is money that disappears from your net worth forever. 

**When you rent**, 100% of your rent payment is an unrecoverable cost. (If your rent is $2,000, you have $2,000 in unrecoverable costs).

**When you buy**, you are building equity, but you also take on massive unrecoverable costs that renters never have to pay:
1. **Property Taxes:** Typically 1% to 2% of the home's value every year.
2. **Maintenance:** Roofs, HVACs, and plumbing. Typically 1% of the home's value every year.
3. **Cost of Capital:** If you put $100,000 into a down payment, that money is trapped in the house. It is no longer growing at 8% in the stock market. You "lose" that potential growth.
4. **Mortgage Interest:** In the first 5 years of a mortgage, almost all of your payment is pure interest paid to the bank. 

---

## ⚖️ The 5% Rule of Thumb

To quickly compare renting vs. buying without a complex spreadsheet, financial experts use the **5% Rule**. It estimates the unrecoverable costs of homeownership at roughly 5% of the property's total value per year.
* 1% Property Tax + 1% Maintenance + 3% Cost of Capital = 5%.

**How to use the rule:**
1. Take the price of the home you want to buy (e.g., $500,000).
2. Multiply it by 5% ($500,000 × 0.05 = $25,000).
3. Divide by 12 months to get the Monthly Breakeven Point ($25,000 / 12 = **$2,083**).

**The Verdict:** If you can rent an equivalent home in that same neighborhood for *less* than $2,083 a month, renting is mathematically the better financial decision! If rent costs *more* than $2,083, you should buy.

---

## 🛑 The "Time Horizon" Factor

Even if the math favors buying, you should **never buy a house if you plan to move within 5 years.**
When you sell a house, you pay roughly 6% in agent commissions and closing costs. If you buy a $500,000 house and sell it 3 years later, you will lose $30,000 just in transaction fees, instantly wiping out any equity you built.
