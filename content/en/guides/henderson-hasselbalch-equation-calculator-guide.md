# Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation: Buffer Solutions

If you add a single drop of strong acid to a glass of pure water, the pH will plummet from a neutral 7 to a highly acidic 3. But human blood has a pH of 7.4. If you drink a glass of acidic orange juice, why doesn't your blood pH drop and kill you? 

Because your blood is a **Buffer Solution**. Buffers have a chemical "shield" that resists changes in pH. To calculate exactly how this shield works, chemists use the **Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation**. In this guide, we will explain this equation and how to use our free [Henderson-Hasselbalch Calculator](/en/calculators/henderson-hasselbalch-equation-calculator).

## 🛡️ What is a Buffer Solution?

A buffer solution is created by mixing two things together:
1. A **Weak Acid** (which has extra protons to give away).
2. Its **Conjugate Base** (which acts like a sponge, ready to absorb extra protons).

If a dangerous acid enters your blood, the "sponge" (conjugate base) absorbs all the extra acid. If a dangerous base enters your blood, the weak acid donates protons to neutralize it. This tag-team effort keeps the pH perfectly stable.

---

## 🧮 The Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation

This equation allows you to calculate the exact pH of a buffer solution if you know the ratio of the weak acid to its conjugate base.

**The Formula:**
**$pH = pKa + \log\left(\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]}\right)$**

* **$pKa$** = The acid dissociation constant of the weak acid.
* **$[A^-]$** = The concentration of the Conjugate Base (the sponge).
* **$[HA]$** = The concentration of the Weak Acid.

*Notice:* If the concentration of the acid and the base are exactly equal, the ratio is 1. Since $\log(1) = 0$, the pH will be exactly equal to the pKa!
