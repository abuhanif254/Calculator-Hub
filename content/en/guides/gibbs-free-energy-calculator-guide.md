# Gibbs Free Energy Calculator: Spontaneous Reactions

If you drop a piece of iron in a damp forest, it will spontaneously rust over time. But if you stare at a pile of rust, it will never spontaneously turn back into shiny iron. Why do some chemical reactions happen on their own, while others require an enormous amount of external energy to force them to happen?

To predict whether a reaction will be spontaneous, chemists calculate **Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta G$)**. In this guide, we will explain the forces of enthalpy and entropy, and how to use our free [Gibbs Free Energy Calculator](/en/calculators/gibbs-free-energy-calculator).

---

## ⚖️ Enthalpy vs. Entropy

Whether a reaction happens spontaneously depends on a tug-of-war between two fundamental forces in the universe:

1. **Enthalpy ($\Delta H$):** This is the total heat energy in a system. The universe is lazy; it prefers things to be at a lower energy state. Therefore, reactions that *release* heat (exothermic, $-\Delta H$) are favorable.
2. **Entropy ($\Delta S$):** This is the measure of chaos and disorder in a system. The universe loves chaos. Think of your bedroom: it gets messy all by itself, but requires energy to clean! Therefore, reactions that *increase* disorder ($+\Delta S$) are favorable.

---

## 🧮 The Gibbs Free Energy Equation

In 1873, Josiah Willard Gibbs combined Enthalpy and Entropy into a single equation to predict spontaneity.

**The Formula:**
**$\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$**

* **$\Delta G$** = Change in Gibbs Free Energy
* **$\Delta H$** = Change in Enthalpy (Heat)
* **$T$** = Temperature (in Kelvin)
* **$\Delta S$** = Change in Entropy (Disorder)

**How to read the result:**
* **$\Delta G$ is Negative ($< 0$):** The reaction is **Spontaneous**! It will happen on its own (like iron rusting).
* **$\Delta G$ is Positive ($> 0$):** The reaction is **Non-Spontaneous**. It will not happen unless you constantly add external energy.
* **$\Delta G$ is exactly Zero:** The reaction is at perfect equilibrium.
