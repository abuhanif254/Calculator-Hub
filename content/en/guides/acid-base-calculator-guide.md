# Acid-Base Calculator: Mastering Neutralization

What happens when you mix a dangerous acid (like Hydrochloric Acid) with a dangerous base (like Sodium Hydroxide)? They destroy each other! 

This is called a **Neutralization Reaction**. The acid and the base cancel each other out, leaving behind nothing but harmless saltwater. In this guide, we will explain how these reactions work, the math behind chemical titrations, and how to use our free [Acid-Base Calculator](/en/calculators/acid-base-calculator).

---

## 💥 The Neutralization Reaction

The fundamental rule of acid-base chemistry is:
**Acid + Base $\rightarrow$ Salt + Water**

* The acid donates a Hydrogen ion ($H^+$).
* The base donates a Hydroxide ion ($OH^-$).
* They combine to form $H_2O$ (Water).
* The leftover atoms combine to form a salt (like $NaCl$, table salt).

---

## 🧪 What is a Titration?

In a chemistry lab, you often have an acid of an *unknown* concentration. To find out how strong it is, you perform a **Titration**. 

You slowly drip a base (of a *known* concentration) into the acid until the solution becomes perfectly neutral (pH 7). By measuring exactly how much base you had to add, you can calculate the original strength of the acid!

**The Titration Formula:**
**$M_a \times V_a = M_b \times V_b$**

* $M_a$ = Molarity of the Acid
* $V_a$ = Volume of the Acid
* $M_b$ = Molarity of the Base
* $V_b$ = Volume of the Base

*(Note: If the acid or base releases multiple ions, like $H_2SO_4$, you must also multiply by the number of ions).*
