# Understanding Depreciation: How Assets Lose Value Over Time

Whether you are buying a car for personal use or a piece of heavy machinery for your business, almost all physical assets lose value as they age. This process is called **Depreciation**.

For consumers, depreciation is a hidden cost that destroys wealth. For business owners, depreciation is a powerful tax deduction that legally shields your income from the IRS. In this guide, we will break down the two most common methods of depreciation and show you how to use our free [Depreciation Calculator](/en/calculators/depreciation-calculator).

---

## 📉 Straight-Line Depreciation (The Simple Method)

Straight-line depreciation assumes that an asset loses the exact same amount of value every single year until it reaches its "Salvage Value" (what it can be sold for as scrap at the end of its useful life).

**The Formula:**
*(Purchase Price - Salvage Value) / Useful Life*

**Example:** You buy a company delivery van for $30,000. You expect to use it for 5 years, after which it will be worth $5,000. 
* ($30,000 - $5,000) / 5 = **$5,000 per year.**
* Every year for the next 5 years, your business can claim a $5,000 depreciation expense on its taxes.

---

## 🎢 Declining Balance Depreciation (The Accelerated Method)

In reality, most assets do not lose value perfectly evenly. A brand new car loses 20% of its value the second you drive it off the dealership lot, but it might only lose 5% of its value in its 8th year.

The Declining Balance method mimics this reality. It allows businesses to take a massive depreciation tax deduction in the first few years of owning the asset, which frees up cash flow immediately. 

**Example (20% Declining Balance):**
* **Year 1:** $30,000 × 20% = **$6,000** depreciation deduction. (Remaining Book Value: $24,000).
* **Year 2:** $24,000 × 20% = **$4,800** depreciation deduction. (Remaining Book Value: $19,200).
* Notice how the deduction shrinks every year, but the upfront tax savings are much higher than the Straight-Line method.
