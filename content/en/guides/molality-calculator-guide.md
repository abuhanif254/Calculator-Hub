# Molality: The Temperature-Proof Concentration

In high school chemistry, almost all solutions are measured using Molarity (moles per Liter). However, when you enter an advanced college lab or a chemical engineering plant, you will start seeing a different measurement: **Molality (m)**. 

Why do we need two different ways to measure concentration? The answer lies in thermodynamics. In this guide, we will explain the Molality formula and how to use our free [Molality Calculator](/en/calculators/molality-calculator).

---

## 🌡️ The Flaw of Molarity

Molarity is based on the **Volume** of a liquid (Liters). 

The problem is that physical volume changes based on temperature. If you heat a beaker of water, the water physically expands and takes up more space. If the volume of the liquid increases, the Molarity of the solution actually drops, even though you didn't add or remove any chemicals!

If a chemist needs to boil a solution or conduct an experiment in a freezing environment, they cannot use Molarity because their concentration calculations will constantly shift.

---

## ⚖️ The Solution: Molality (m)

**Molality (m)** ignores volume completely. Instead, it measures concentration using the **Mass** of the solvent (the liquid). 

Because the mass (weight) of water does not change when you heat it or freeze it, Molality is completely immune to temperature changes. It is the perfect unit of measurement for experiments involving boiling point elevation or freezing point depression.

**The Formula:**
**m = n / kg**

* **m** = Molality (mol/kg)
* **n** = Moles of the solute (the powder you are dissolving)
* **kg** = Mass of the solvent (the liquid) in Kilograms.

*(Note: Molality uses a lowercase 'm', while Molarity uses an uppercase 'M').*
