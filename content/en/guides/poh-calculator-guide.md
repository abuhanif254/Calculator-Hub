# pOH Calculator: The Inverse of pH

Most people are familiar with the pH scale, which measures how *acidic* a solution is by counting Hydrogen ions ($H^+$). But what if you want to measure how *basic* (alkaline) a solution is? 

For strong bases like bleach, ammonia, and lye, chemists use the **pOH Scale**. In this guide, we will explain what pOH is, how it relates to pH, and how to use our free [pOH Calculator](/en/calculators/poh-calculator).

---

## 🧪 What is pOH?

Just like pH stands for the "power of Hydrogen," pOH stands for the **"power of Hydroxide."**
Instead of measuring Hydrogen ions ($H^+$), the pOH scale measures the concentration of **Hydroxide ions ($OH^-$)**.

Bases are chemicals that release Hydroxide ions into water. The more Hydroxide ions a solution has, the more basic (alkaline) it is.

**The pOH Scale:**
* **pOH < 7 (Basic):** High concentration of Hydroxide (e.g., Soap).
* **pOH = 7 (Neutral):** Pure water.
* **pOH > 7 (Acidic):** Low concentration of Hydroxide (e.g., Lemon Juice).

*Notice how this is the exact opposite of the pH scale!*

---

## 🧮 The Mathematics of pOH

Just like pH, pOH is calculated using a logarithmic scale because ion concentrations are incredibly small numbers.

**The pOH Formula:**
**pOH = $-\log[OH^-]$**

*(Where $[OH^-]$ is the molar concentration of Hydroxide ions).*

---

## ⚖️ The Magic Rule: pH + pOH = 14

Because of the natural chemical properties of water at 25°C, there is a strict mathematical relationship between hydrogen and hydroxide ions. If you know the pH of a solution, you instantly know its pOH!

**pH + pOH = 14**

* If a cup of coffee has a pH of 5 (Acidic)...
* Its pOH must be exactly 9 (14 - 5 = 9).
* If a bottle of bleach has a pH of 12 (Basic)...
* Its pOH must be exactly 2 (14 - 12 = 2).
