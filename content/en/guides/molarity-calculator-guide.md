# Molarity Calculator: Measuring Chemical Concentration

If you pour a spoonful of sugar into a glass of water, you create a solution. But how do you accurately describe exactly *how sweet* that water is to another chemist? 

In chemistry, we don't use terms like "a spoonful" or "very sweet." Instead, we use **Molarity (M)**. In this guide, we will explain the mathematical formula for molarity, the difference between a solute and a solvent, and how to use our free [Molarity Calculator](/en/calculators/molarity-calculator).

---

## 🧪 What is Molarity?

**Molarity (M)**, also known as molar concentration, is the standard unit chemists use to measure how concentrated a chemical solution is. 

It is defined simply as the number of **moles of solute** divided by the total **liters of solution**.

* **Solute:** The solid powder you are dissolving (e.g., the sugar or salt).
* **Solvent:** The liquid you are dissolving the powder into (usually water).
* **Solution:** The final mixed liquid (The solute + solvent combined).

If a bottle of Hydrochloric Acid is labeled **"2.0 M HCl"**, it means there are exactly 2.0 moles of pure HCl dissolved into every 1 Liter of that liquid.

---

## 🧮 The Molarity Formula

To calculate Molarity by hand, use the following formula:

**M = n / V**

* **M** = Molarity (mol/L)
* **n** = Number of moles of solute
* **V** = Total volume of the solution in Liters (L)

*Note: You must ensure your volume is in Liters! If your lab beaker only has 500 milliliters (mL) of water, you must divide by 1000 to convert it to 0.5 Liters before doing the math.*

---

## 🛑 Molarity vs. Molality

A common trap for chemistry students is confusing Molarity with Molality. They sound identical, but they use different denominators.
* **Molar**ity (M) uses the **Volume** of the entire solution in Liters.
* **Molal**ity (m) uses the **Mass** of just the solvent in Kilograms.
Because liquids expand when they are heated, Molarity changes slightly depending on the temperature of the room! Molality does not.
