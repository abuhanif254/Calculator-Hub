# Carbohydrate Calculator: Managing Your Macros

Whether you are trying to lose weight on a Keto diet, or trying to build muscle by "bulking," managing your daily carbohydrates is the most important factor in your nutrition plan. 

Carbohydrates are your body's primary source of energy, but eating too many will cause your body to store the excess as fat. In this guide, we will explain how to calculate your daily carb limit, what "net carbs" are, and how to use our free [Carbohydrate Calculator](/en/calculators/carbohydrate-calculator).

---

## 🍞 Why Do We Need Carbohydrates?

When you eat carbs (bread, pasta, sugar, fruit), your digestive system breaks them down into glucose (blood sugar). Your cells, muscles, and brain use this glucose for immediate energy. 

However, your body can only store a limited amount of glucose in your muscles and liver (as glycogen). If you eat a massive bowl of pasta and sit on the couch all day, your glycogen stores fill up. Any leftover glucose in your blood is converted into body fat. 

---

## 🥗 Keto, Cutting, and Bulking

Depending on your fitness goals, your daily carb intake will change drastically:

* **Keto Diet:** You restrict carbs so heavily (usually under 20g-50g per day) that your body runs out of glucose entirely. It is forced to burn stored body fat for energy, a state called "Ketosis."
* **Cutting (Weight Loss):** You eat a moderate amount of carbs (e.g., 100g-150g). You have enough energy to lift weights, but you are in a caloric deficit, causing slow, steady fat loss.
* **Bulking (Muscle Gain):** You eat a massive amount of carbs (e.g., 300g+). This ensures your muscles are always full of glycogen, giving you the maximum possible energy to lift heavy weights and build new muscle.

---

## ⚖️ What are "Net Carbs"?

If you look at a nutrition label for broccoli, it might say it has 10g of carbohydrates. But broccoli is incredibly healthy! 

This is because of **Fiber**. Fiber is a type of carbohydrate that your body cannot actually digest. It passes through your system without turning into glucose or fat.
To find the carbs that actually matter, you subtract the fiber:

**Total Carbs - Dietary Fiber = Net Carbs**
*(Example: 10g Total Carbs - 6g Fiber = 4g Net Carbs).*
