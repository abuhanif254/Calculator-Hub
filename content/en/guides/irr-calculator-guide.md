# Understanding Internal Rate of Return (IRR): A Complete Guide

When evaluating an investment, most people look at **ROI (Return on Investment)**. If you invest $100 and make $120, your ROI is 20%. However, ROI ignores a crucial factor: *Time*. Making 20% in one year is fantastic; making 20% over 10 years is terrible.

This is where the **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** comes in. IRR calculates the annualized percentage rate earned on each dollar invested for each period it is invested. In this guide, we will break down how IRR works and how to use our free [IRR Calculator](/en/calculators/irr-calculator) for your financial analysis.

---

## 📈 What Exactly is IRR?

In technical finance terms, the Internal Rate of Return is the discount rate that makes the Net Present Value (NPV) of all cash flows from a particular project equal to zero. 

In simple terms: **IRR is your true annual growth rate.**

If a real estate investment has an IRR of 12%, it means that, on average, the money tied up in that investment is growing by 12% every single year until the project ends.

### IRR vs. ROI
* **ROI (Return on Investment):** Looks at the total profit divided by the total cost. It does not care if the investment took 1 year or 50 years.
* **IRR (Internal Rate of Return):** Factors in the time value of money. Getting your cash back earlier drastically improves your IRR because you can reinvest that cash elsewhere.

---

## 🏢 Common Use Cases for IRR

1. **Real Estate Investing:** Real estate involves an initial massive cash outflow (down payment, renovations), followed by small positive cash flows (rental income), and finally a large cash inflow (selling the property). IRR helps investors compare the profitability of two different properties.
2. **Private Equity & Venture Capital:** Investors use IRR to evaluate the performance of funds over a 5 to 10-year lifespan.
3. **Corporate Finance:** When a company decides whether to buy a new factory or upgrade software, they calculate the project's IRR. If the IRR is higher than their cost of capital (WACC), they approve the project.

## ⚠️ The Limitations of IRR

While IRR is the gold standard for investment analysis, it has a few blind spots:
1. **The Reinvestment Assumption:** IRR mathematically assumes that all interim cash flows (like yearly rent) are reinvested at the *exact same IRR rate*. In reality, if your property yields a 20% IRR, it might be hard to find another 20% investment to put your rental income into.
2. **Multiple IRRs:** If an investment has alternating positive and negative cash flows (e.g., you make money in Year 1, but have to inject more cash in Year 2 for repairs), the math can actually output multiple different IRRs, making it confusing. In these cases, MIRR (Modified Internal Rate of Return) is a better metric.
