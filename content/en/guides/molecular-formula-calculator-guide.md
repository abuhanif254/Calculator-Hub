# Molecular Formula: Finding the True Compound

If a lab analysis tells you that a mysterious powder has an empirical formula of **$CH_2O$**, you still don't actually know what the powder is. 

$CH_2O$ is just a *ratio* (1 Carbon, 2 Hydrogens, 1 Oxygen). The true molecule could be $C_2H_4O_2$ (Acetic Acid / Vinegar), or it could be $C_6H_{12}O_6$ (Glucose / Sugar). 
To find out exactly what the chemical is, you must calculate its **Molecular Formula**. In this guide, we will explain how to do that, and how to use our free [Molecular Formula Calculator](/en/calculators/molecular-formula-calculator).

---

## 🔬 Empirical vs. Molecular

* **Empirical Formula:** The simplest, reduced ratio of atoms (e.g., $CH_2O$).
* **Molecular Formula:** The *actual* number of atoms in a single molecule of the compound (e.g., $C_6H_{12}O_6$).

To find the true Molecular Formula, you need exactly two pieces of information:
1. The Empirical Formula.
2. The **True Molar Mass** of the entire compound (usually given to you by a mass spectrometer in a lab, or by your chemistry teacher on a test).

---

## 🧮 Calculating the Molecular Formula

Let's say our mysterious $CH_2O$ powder was run through a mass spectrometer, and we were told its True Molar Mass is **180.16 g/mol**.

**Step 1: Calculate the Empirical Mass**
Calculate the molar mass of the small $CH_2O$ empirical piece:
* Carbon (C): 12.01 g/mol
* Hydrogen (H): 1.008 × 2 = 2.016 g/mol
* Oxygen (O): 16.00 g/mol
* Total Empirical Mass = `12.01 + 2.016 + 16.00 = 30.026 g/mol`.

**Step 2: Find the Multiplier (n)**
Divide the True Mass by the Empirical Mass to find the multiplier.
* Multiplier = (True Mass) / (Empirical Mass)
* Multiplier = 180.16 / 30.026 = **6**

**Step 3: Multiply the Empirical Formula**
Multiply every subscript in the empirical formula by the multiplier (6).
* $C_1 \times 6 = C_6$
* $H_2 \times 6 = H_{12}$
* $O_1 \times 6 = O_6$

The true molecular formula is **$C_6H_{12}O_6$** (Glucose)! The mysterious powder was just sugar.
