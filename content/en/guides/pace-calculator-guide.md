# How to Calculate Running Pace: The Math Behind Your Marathon

Whether you are training for your first 5K or trying to qualify for the Boston Marathon, understanding and controlling your running pace is the key to race day success. 

"Pace" is the amount of time it takes to cover a specific distance (usually a mile or a kilometer). In this guide, we will break down the pace formula, explain how to predict your finish times, and show you how to use our free [Pace Calculator](/en/calculators/pace-calculator) to build your perfect training plan.

---

## 🏃‍♂️ The Pace Formula

The mathematical formula for pace is incredibly simple, but the time conversions can make it tricky to calculate in your head while running.

**Pace = Time ÷ Distance**

* **Example:** You ran a 5K (which is 3.1 miles) in exactly 30 minutes.
* 30 minutes ÷ 3.1 miles = 9.67 minutes per mile.
* *The trick:* 0.67 of a minute is not 67 seconds! You must multiply 0.67 by 60 seconds. (0.67 × 60 = 40).
* **Final Pace:** 9 minutes and 40 seconds per mile (9:40/mi).

---

## 🏎️ Speed vs. Pace

While pace is measured in "Time per Distance" (minutes per mile), **Speed** is measured in "Distance per Time" (miles per hour, or MPH). 

Treadmills often display Speed, while GPS watches display Pace.
* A 10:00/mi pace = 6.0 MPH
* An 8:00/mi pace = 7.5 MPH
* A 6:00/mi pace = 10.0 MPH

---

## 🏁 Predicting Race Finish Times

If you know your target pace, you can easily predict your finish time by flipping the formula:
**Time = Pace × Distance**

Let's say you want to run a Half Marathon (13.1 miles) at an 8:30/mi pace.
* 8.5 minutes × 13.1 miles = 111.35 minutes.
* 111 minutes is 1 hour and 51 minutes.
* 0.35 of a minute is 21 seconds.
* **Finish Time:** 1 hour, 51 minutes, and 21 seconds.
