# When Should I Take Social Security? Maximizing Your Benefits

For millions of Americans, Social Security is the bedrock of their retirement income. But the system is complex, and the age at which you choose to start claiming your benefits will permanently alter your monthly payout for the rest of your life.

In this guide, we will break down how Social Security calculates your benefit, the massive difference between claiming at 62 versus 70, and how to use our free [Social Security Calculator](/en/calculators/social-security-calculator) to find your optimal retirement age.

---

## 🏛️ How Social Security is Calculated

The Social Security Administration (SSA) looks at your entire working history and selects your **35 highest-earning years** (adjusted for inflation) to determine your average indexed monthly earnings (AIME). 

If you worked for fewer than 35 years, the SSA will factor in $0 for the missing years, which drastically drags down your average!

---

## 📅 The Critical Concept: Full Retirement Age (FRA)

Your Full Retirement Age (FRA) is the age at which you are entitled to receive 100% of your calculated benefit. For anyone born in 1960 or later, your FRA is exactly **67 years old**.

### Claiming Early (The Penalty)
You are legally allowed to start claiming Social Security as early as age 62. However, if you claim before your FRA, you are hit with a permanent penalty.
* If your FRA is 67 and you claim at 62, your monthly benefit is permanently reduced by **30%**.
* *Example:* A $2,000/month FRA benefit drops to $1,400/month for the rest of your life.

### Claiming Late (The Bonus)
If you delay claiming your benefits *past* your FRA, the government rewards you with "Delayed Retirement Credits."
* Your benefit increases by **8% for every year** you delay, up until age 70.
* *Example:* A $2,000/month FRA benefit grows to $2,480/month if you wait until 70. 
* There is no financial incentive to delay past age 70.

---

## ⚖️ The Breakeven Point

The most common question retirees ask is: *"Should I take less money now, or wait for more money later?"*

This comes down to life expectancy. If you claim at 62, you receive hundreds of small checks before the person who waited until 70 receives their first big check. The "breakeven point" is the age at which the total lifetime cash received by waiting surpasses the total lifetime cash received by claiming early. 
* The typical breakeven point between claiming at 62 and 70 is around age **80**. 
* If you believe you will live well past 80, waiting until 70 mathematically guarantees you more total money.
