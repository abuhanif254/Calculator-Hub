# Car Leasing Explained: How to Calculate Your True Monthly Payment

Leasing a car can be a great way to drive a new vehicle every few years with a lower monthly payment than buying. However, the math behind leasing is notoriously confusing, often hiding the true cost of the vehicle.

In this guide, we will translate dealership jargon into plain English and show you how to use our free [Lease Calculator](/en/calculators/lease-calculator) to ensure you are getting a fair deal.

---

## 🚗 The Three Pillars of a Lease Payment

Unlike buying a car where you pay for the whole thing, when you lease, you are only paying for the **depreciation** (the value the car loses while you drive it) plus a **finance charge** (interest).

### 1. Capitalized Cost (Cap Cost)
This is the negotiated selling price of the car. If you negotiate the price down, your lease payment drops. 
* **Cap Cost Reduction:** Any money you put down (down payment, trade-in equity, or rebates) reduces the Cap Cost.

### 2. Residual Value
This is the estimated value of the car at the end of the lease. It is set by the bank as a percentage of the MSRP and is non-negotiable.
* *Example:* A $50,000 car with a 60% residual value will be worth $30,000 at the end of a 3-year lease. 
* You are paying for the $20,000 difference (depreciation). **A higher residual value means a lower monthly payment!**

### 3. Money Factor
This is the interest rate on the lease. Dealerships express it as a tiny decimal (e.g., 0.00125). 
* **The Trick:** To convert the money factor into a standard APR interest rate, simply multiply it by **2400**. 
* *Example:* 0.00125 x 2400 = 3.0% APR.
