# The Ultimate Guide to Calculating Your GPA

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is one of the most important metrics of your academic career. It is used by high schools, colleges, and employers to evaluate your performance over time.

In this guide, we will break down the math behind your GPA, explain the difference between weighted and unweighted scores, and show you how to use our [GPA Calculator](/en/calculators/gpa-calculator) to stay on track.

---

## 🎓 How the GPA System Works

A GPA is a single number that represents your average grade across all your classes. Most schools in the United States use a 4.0 scale.

Here is the standard unweighted conversion scale:
* **A (90-100%)** = 4.0 points
* **B (80-89%)** = 3.0 points
* **C (70-79%)** = 2.0 points
* **D (60-69%)** = 1.0 points
* **F (Below 60%)** = 0.0 points

To calculate your GPA, you convert each letter grade into its point value, add all the points together, and divide by the total number of classes.

---

## ⚖️ Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

If you are in high school, you have likely heard of "weighted" GPAs. 

### Unweighted GPA
An unweighted GPA treats every class equally, regardless of its difficulty. The highest possible score is a 4.0. An 'A' in regular Biology is worth the same as an 'A' in AP Biology.

### Weighted GPA
A weighted GPA rewards students for taking harder classes (like Honors, AP, or IB courses). In a weighted system, the scale is typically shifted up by 0.5 or 1.0 points for advanced classes.
* An 'A' in an AP class might be worth **5.0 points** instead of 4.0.
* Because of this, it is possible to have a weighted GPA higher than 4.0!
