## 🌙 What is a Sleep Cycle?

During a night's sleep, your brain cycles through multiple distinct stages. One complete cycle takes approximately **90 minutes**. Each cycle consists of:

1. **Stage 1 (Light Sleep):** You are drowsy, easy to wake. Lasts just a few minutes.
2. **Stage 2 (True Sleep):** Heart rate slows, body temperature drops. You spend most of your night here.
3. **Stage 3 (Deep Sleep / Slow-Wave Sleep):** The most restorative stage. Your body repairs tissue, builds bone and muscle, and strengthens your immune system.
4. **REM Sleep (Rapid Eye Movement):** Your brain is nearly as active as when awake. This is when you dream, and when memories are consolidated and learning is processed.

---

## ⏰ Why Timing Matters

The key insight is this: **it is much better to wake up at the END of a complete cycle than in the middle of one.**

If your alarm wakes you in the middle of **deep sleep (Stage 3)**, you will experience **sleep inertia** — that heavy, disoriented, groggy feeling that can last hours. However, if you wake at the end of a complete 90-minute cycle, you wake up during the light sleep between cycles, feeling naturally alert and refreshed.

---

## 🧮 The Sleep Cycle Math

An average adult needs **5 to 6 complete sleep cycles** per night, plus approximately **15 minutes** to fall asleep.

**Optimal Sleep = (Number of Cycles × 90 minutes) + 15 minutes (to fall asleep)**

* 5 cycles = (5 × 90) + 15 = **7 hours 45 minutes** of sleep
* 6 cycles = (6 × 90) + 15 = **9 hours 15 minutes** of sleep
