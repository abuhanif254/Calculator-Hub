# Mutual Funds Explained: How to Build a Diversified Portfolio

Investing all your money into a single company's stock is incredibly risky. If that company goes bankrupt, your life savings are wiped out. The secret to long-term wealth is **Diversification**—spreading your money across hundreds of different companies. 

The easiest way to achieve instant diversification is by investing in a **Mutual Fund**. In this guide, we will explain how mutual funds work, the hidden fees to watch out for, and how to use our free [Mutual Fund Calculator](/en/calculators/mutual-fund-calculator).

---

## 🧺 What is a Mutual Fund?

Imagine a giant basket. Thousands of individual investors pool their money together and throw it into this basket. A professional money manager then takes that massive pool of cash and buys shares in hundreds of different companies (like Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and Tesla). 

When you buy one "share" of a mutual fund, you are actually buying a tiny slice of that giant basket.
* **Instant Diversification:** With just $100, you can own a fraction of 500 different companies. If one company fails, the other 499 companies keep your portfolio afloat.
* **Active vs. Passive:** "Active" funds have a manager trying to hand-pick winning stocks to beat the market. "Passive" funds (Index Funds) simply use a computer algorithm to buy every company in a specific market index (like the S&P 500).

---

## 🛑 The Wealth Killer: Expense Ratios

Mutual funds are not free. The company running the fund charges you an annual fee called an **Expense Ratio**. 

This fee is taken out of your total balance every single year, regardless of whether the fund made money or lost money.
* **Passive Index Funds:** Usually charge around **0.05%** to **0.10%** (Very cheap).
* **Active Mutual Funds:** Usually charge around **0.75%** to **1.50%** (Very expensive).

**Why it matters:** A 1% fee might sound small, but over 30 years of compounding, a 1% expense ratio can eat up **25% to 30% of your total lifetime returns**. Over decades, high-fee mutual funds will siphon hundreds of thousands of dollars out of your pocket and give it to Wall Street managers.
