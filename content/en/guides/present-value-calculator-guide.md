# The Math of Present Value: Are You Making a Good Investment?

If someone offered to give you $100,000 today, or $100,000 in ten years, you would obviously take the money today. But what if they offered you $100,000 today, or $150,000 in ten years? Which is the better deal? 

To answer that question, financial professionals use a mathematical concept called **Present Value (PV)**. In this guide, we will break down the concept of discounting cash flows and show you how to use our free [Present Value Calculator](/en/calculators/present-value-calculator) to evaluate any investment.

---

## 📉 Discounting: Reverse Engineering the Future

Present Value is the exact opposite of Future Value. Instead of seeing how much a current sum of money will grow to become, Present Value looks at a *future* sum of money and shrinks it down to see what it is worth *today*. 

This shrinking process is called **Discounting**. We discount future money because:
1. **Inflation:** $100,000 in ten years will buy significantly fewer goods than $100,000 today.
2. **Opportunity Cost:** If you take the $100,000 today, you could invest it in the stock market. You have to compare the $150,000 future offer against what you could have generated on your own.

**The Formula:**
*PV = FV / (1 + r)^n*
(Where **FV** is Future Value, **r** is the discount rate, and **n** is the number of years).

---

## ⚖️ Solving the $100,000 vs $150,000 Dilemma

Let's solve the scenario from the introduction. We need to find the Present Value of that future $150,000. 

Assume you could take the $100,000 today and invest it in an index fund that safely yields **7%** a year. Therefore, our "discount rate" is 7%.
* PV = 150,000 / (1 + 0.07)^10
* PV = 150,000 / 1.967
* **PV = $76,258**

**The Verdict:** That future $150,000 is only worth $76,258 in today's dollars. The mathematically correct choice is to take the $100,000 today, because your own investments will beat the future offer!
