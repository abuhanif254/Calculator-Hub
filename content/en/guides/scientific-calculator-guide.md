# How to Master the Scientific Calculator: Functions & Shortcuts

Scientific calculators are essential tools for high school math, engineering, and physics. However, seeing dozens of buttons labeled with abbreviations like `sin`, `log`, and `deg` can be intimidating.

In this guide, we will break down the core functions of our [Scientific Calculator](/en/calculators/scientific-calculator) so you can solve complex equations with confidence.

---

## 📐 Trigonometry and Angles (SIN, COS, TAN)

Trigonometric functions relate the angles of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. 

* **sin, cos, tan:** The primary functions (Sine, Cosine, Tangent).
* **asin, acos, atan:** The inverse functions (Arcsine, Arccosine, Arctangent). Used to find an angle when you know the sides.

**Crucial Step:** Always check your angle mode! 
If a problem is in **Degrees (deg)** and your calculator is set to **Radians (rad)**, your answer will be completely wrong. Use the toggle button on our calculator to switch between `deg` and `rad` before starting.

---

## 📈 Logarithms and Exponents

Logarithms are the inverse of exponents. They are heavily used in algebra, calculus, and computing.

* **$x^y$:** Raises a number to the power of another. (e.g., $2^3 = 8$).
* **log:** The common logarithm (base 10). If $10^x = 100$, then $\log(100) = x = 2$.
* **ln:** The natural logarithm (base $e \approx 2.718$). Widely used in continuous growth models.
* **$e^x$:** The exponential function, the inverse of `ln`.

## ⚙️ Advanced Features & Keyboard Shortcuts

Our online calculator is designed for speed. You can click the buttons, but power users can type directly on their keyboard!

* **Parentheses `( )`:** Crucial for enforcing the order of operations (PEMDAS).
* **Factorial `n!`:** Multiplies a number by every integer below it (e.g., $4! = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24$).
* **Pi `π` & Euler's Number `e`:** Built-in constants for geometry and algebra.

### Shortcuts
* Type `sin`, `cos`, or `log` directly on your keyboard.
* Press `^` for exponents.
* Press `Enter` to solve.
* Use the `Ans` button to use the result of your previous calculation in a new formula!
