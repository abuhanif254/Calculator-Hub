# How Much House Can I Afford? The 28/36 Rule Explained

Just because a bank approves you for a $500,000 mortgage doesn't mean you can actually afford a $500,000 house. Banks are perfectly happy to let you become "house poor," where your mortgage payment consumes so much of your income that you have nothing left for groceries, vacations, or emergencies.

To protect yourself, you need to understand exactly how underwriters calculate affordability. In this guide, we will break down the famous 28/36 Rule and show you how to use our free [House Affordability Calculator](/en/calculators/house-affordability-calculator) to set a safe budget.

## ⚖️ The 28/36 Rule of Thumb

When you apply for a mortgage, lenders look closely at your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio. The industry standard for a conventional loan is the 28/36 Rule.

### The Front-End Ratio (28%)
This rule states that your maximum housing payment should not exceed **28% of your gross monthly income** (your income *before* taxes).
* **The "Housing Payment" includes PITI:** Principal, Interest, Property Taxes, and Homeowners Insurance. It also includes HOA fees and PMI if applicable.
* *Example:* If you earn $10,000 a month gross, your total monthly housing payment should not exceed **$2,800**.

### The Back-End Ratio (36%)
This rule states that your housing payment *plus all your other monthly debts* should not exceed **36% of your gross monthly income**.
* **"Other Debts" include:** Minimum credit card payments, student loans, and auto loans. (It does *not* include groceries, cell phone bills, or Netflix).
* *Example:* If you earn $10,000 a month, your total debt (including the new mortgage) cannot exceed **$3,600**. 
* If you already pay $1,000 a month for student loans and a car, you only have $2,600 left for a mortgage ($3,600 - $1,000 = $2,600). The back-end ratio often limits buyers more than the front-end ratio!

## 🛑 The Hidden Costs of Homeownership

First-time buyers often make the mistake of comparing a mortgage payment directly to their rent payment. This is dangerous because rent is a *maximum* monthly payment, while a mortgage is a *minimum* monthly payment.

When calculating your affordability, you must leave room for:
* **Maintenance & Repairs:** The general rule is to save 1% to 2% of the home's value every year for repairs (a new roof, a broken HVAC).
* **Closing Costs:** You will need to bring 2% to 5% of the purchase price in cash to the closing table, *in addition* to your down payment.
