# Combined Gas Law Calculator: Changing Conditions

If you take a deep breath of air at the surface of the ocean and then dive deep underwater, the pressure increases drastically. If the water is freezing, the temperature also drops. What happens to the volume of the air in your lungs?

When a gas undergoes a change where multiple variables (Pressure, Volume, Temperature) change all at once, chemists use the **Combined Gas Law**. In this guide, we will explain how the law works and how to use our free [Combined Gas Law Calculator](/en/calculators/combined-gas-law-calculator).

---

## 🔗 The Three Laws Combined

The Combined Gas Law is just a mashup of three simpler laws that keep the amount of gas (Moles) constant:
1. **Boyle's Law:** Pressure and Volume are inversely proportional ($P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$).
2. **Charles's Law:** Volume and Temperature are directly proportional ($V_1/T_1 = V_2/T_2$).
3. **Gay-Lussac's Law:** Pressure and Temperature are directly proportional ($P_1/T_1 = P_2/T_2$).

When you squish them all together, you get the Combined Gas Law!

---

## 🧮 The Combined Equation

Because the number of moles ($n$) doesn't change, the ratio of Pressure $\times$ Volume divided by Temperature must always stay exactly the same, no matter what you do to the gas.

**The Formula:**
**$\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$**

* **$P_1, V_1, T_1$** = The Initial Pressure, Volume, and Temperature.
* **$P_2, V_2, T_2$** = The Final Pressure, Volume, and Temperature.

*Crucial Rule:* Just like all gas laws, your Temperature ($T$) **MUST** be converted to Kelvin before doing any math. If you use Celsius, the equation will break!
