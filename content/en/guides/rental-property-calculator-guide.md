# The Math of Real Estate: How to Analyze a Rental Property

Buying a rental property is one of the most powerful ways to build generational wealth. However, if you buy the wrong house in the wrong neighborhood at the wrong price, it can become a financial nightmare.

Successful real estate investors do not buy properties based on emotion; they buy them based on cold, hard math. In this guide, we will explain the key metrics every investor uses, and show you how to use our free [Rental Property Calculator](/en/calculators/rental-property-calculator) to analyze your next deal.

---

## 📏 The 1% Rule of Thumb

When you are quickly scanning Zillow or Redfin, you don't have time to run a deep mathematical analysis on every single house. Investors use the **1% Rule** as a quick filter.

**The Rule:** The monthly rent should be at least 1% of the total purchase price.
* If a house costs $200,000, it should rent for at least $2,000 per month.
* If a house costs $400,000, it should rent for at least $4,000 per month.

If a property doesn't come close to the 1% rule, you will likely lose money on it every month (negative cash flow), and you should pass on the deal.

---

## 🏢 Net Operating Income (NOI)

Once a property passes the 1% rule, you need to calculate its Net Operating Income. 
NOI is the total income the property generates minus all operating expenses (excluding your mortgage payment).

**Income:** Rent, parking fees, laundry machines.
**Expenses (The 50% Rule):** Property taxes, insurance, maintenance, property management, HOA fees, and vacancy rates. As a rule of thumb, expect these expenses to consume about 50% of your gross rental income.

* *Gross Rent:* $2,000/month
* *Operating Expenses:* $1,000/month
* *NOI:* **$1,000/month**

---

## 💰 Cash Flow and Cash-on-Cash Return

Finally, you must factor in your mortgage payment to determine your actual profit.
If your NOI is $1,000/month, and your mortgage payment (Principal + Interest) is $800/month, your **Cash Flow is $200/month**.

**Cash-on-Cash Return (CoC):** This is the ultimate metric. It tells you exactly what kind of return you are getting on the cash you invested.
* If you put down a total of $50,000 in cash (down payment + closing costs + repairs).
* And the property generates $2,400 in yearly cash flow ($200 × 12).
* Your CoC Return = $2,400 / $50,000 = **4.8%**. 

(A 4.8% return is decent, but many investors aim for 8% to 12%).
