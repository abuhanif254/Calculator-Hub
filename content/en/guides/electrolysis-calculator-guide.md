# Electrolysis Calculator: Using Electricity to Drive Chemistry

Imagine you want to extract pure aluminum metal from aluminum oxide (bauxite ore). Left to their own devices, aluminum ions in a solution would never just turn into metal — it's not spontaneous. However, if you force a massive electric current through the solution, you can **overpower chemistry itself** and make it happen anyway.

This process is called **Electrolysis**. It's how we produce aluminum, chlorine gas, hydrogen fuel, and how we electroplate jewelry and car parts. In this guide, we will explore how it works and how to use our free [Electrolysis Calculator](/en/calculators/electrolysis-calculator).

---

## ⚡ Electrolysis vs. Galvanic Cells

* **Galvanic Cell (Battery):** A spontaneous reaction produces electricity. The reaction *wants* to happen.
* **Electrolytic Cell (Electrolysis):** An external power supply *forces* a non-spontaneous reaction. You are using electricity to drive chemistry.

The cell potential of an electrolytic process is **negative**, meaning it requires energy input. The external voltage source must supply enough voltage to overcome the negative cell potential.

---

## 🧮 Calculating Electrolysis Quantities

Electrolysis calculations use the same **Faraday's Law** equations:

**$m = \frac{I \times t \times M}{n \times F}$**

Where:
* **$m$** = Mass deposited or produced (grams)
* **$I$** = Current in Amperes
* **$t$** = Time in seconds
* **$M$** = Molar Mass of substance (g/mol)
* **$n$** = Electrons transferred per formula unit
* **$F$** = 96,485 C/mol (Faraday's Constant)

---

## 🏭 Real World Applications

* **Aluminum production:** Requires over 15,000 Amperes of current!
* **Electroplating:** Depositing a thin layer of gold or silver onto jewelry.
* **Water splitting:** Producing hydrogen gas for clean fuel cells.
* **Chlorine production:** For water treatment and plastics manufacturing.
