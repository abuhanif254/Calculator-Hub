# The Ultimate Guide to Probability: Formulas, Events, and Rules

Probability is the mathematical study of chance. Whether you are playing cards, investing in stocks, or checking the weather forecast, probability helps you understand the likelihood of an outcome.

In this guide, we will break down the fundamental rules of probability, the difference between dependent and independent events, and how to use our free [Probability Calculator](/en/calculators/probability-calculator).

---

## 🎲 The Basic Probability Formula

The probability of any single event is calculated by dividing the number of favorable outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes.

**P(A) = Favorable Outcomes / Total Possible Outcomes**

*Example:* What is the probability of rolling a '4' on a standard 6-sided die?
* Favorable outcomes = 1 (there is only one '4')
* Total possible outcomes = 6
* P(4) = 1/6 = 0.1667 (or 16.67%)

## 🔗 Types of Events and Rules

When you start combining events, the math gets slightly more complex.

### 1. Independent Events (The "AND" Rule)
Two events are independent if the outcome of one does not affect the outcome of the other (e.g., flipping two coins).
To find the probability of both events happening, you **multiply** their individual probabilities:
**P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)**

### 2. Mutually Exclusive Events (The "OR" Rule)
Two events are mutually exclusive if they cannot both happen at the same time (e.g., rolling a '2' or a '5' on a single die).
To find the probability of *either* event happening, you **add** their individual probabilities:
**P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)**

### 3. Dependent Events (Conditional Probability)
Two events are dependent if the outcome of the first affects the outcome of the second (e.g., drawing two cards from a deck *without* replacement).
The probability of B given that A has already happened is written as P(B|A).
