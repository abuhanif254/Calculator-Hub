# Margin vs. Markup: The Ultimate Guide for Retail Pricing

Pricing your products correctly is the lifeblood of any retail business. However, thousands of business owners make a critical mathematical error every day: confusing **Margin** with **Markup**.

In this guide, we will break down the formulas, explain why mixing them up destroys profitability, and show you how to use our [Margin Calculator](/en/calculators/margin-calculator) to set perfect prices.

---

## 📈 The Difference Between Margin and Markup

Both margin and markup deal with the relationship between your Cost (what you paid for the product) and your Revenue (what you sell it for).

### Gross Margin
Margin looks at profit as a percentage of your **Revenue (Selling Price)**. It answers the question: *"For every dollar I make in sales, how much is profit?"*
* **Formula:** (Revenue - Cost) / Revenue
* *Example:* You buy a shirt for $10 and sell it for $20. Your profit is $10. Your margin is $10 / $20 = **50%**.

### Markup
Markup looks at profit as a percentage of your **Cost**. It answers the question: *"How much did I increase the cost to get my selling price?"*
* **Formula:** (Revenue - Cost) / Cost
* *Example:* You buy a shirt for $10 and sell it for $20. Your profit is $10. Your markup is $10 / $10 = **100%**.

---

## ⚠️ The Dangerous Pricing Trap

Imagine you want a 30% profit margin on a product that costs you $100.
If you simply mark up the cost by 30% (multiplying $100 x 1.3), you get a selling price of $130. 

Did you achieve a 30% margin? No! 
Your profit is $30, and your selling price is $130. Your actual margin is $30 / $130 = **23%**. 

By confusing markup with margin, you just lost 7% of your expected profitability!
