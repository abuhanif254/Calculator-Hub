# Faraday's Law of Electrolysis Calculator

When you pass an electric current through a solution containing dissolved metals, something remarkable happens: the metal atoms start depositing on the electrode! This is the basis of **electroplating** — how manufacturers coat cheap metal objects with a thin, shiny layer of gold, silver, or chrome.

The exact amount of metal deposited is precisely predicted by **Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis**. In this guide, we will explore these laws and how to use our free [Faraday's Law Calculator](/en/calculators/faradays-law-calculator).

---

## 🔌 Faraday's Two Laws

**First Law:** The mass of a substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (charge) passed through the solution. More current, more metal!

**Second Law:** The masses of different substances deposited by the same quantity of electricity are proportional to their molar masses divided by their number of electrons transferred.

Together, they produce one unified equation:

---

## 🧮 The Faraday Equation

**The Formula:**
**$m = \frac{Q \times M}{n \times F}$**

* **$m$** = Mass of substance deposited or dissolved (grams)
* **$Q$** = Total electric charge passed ($Q = I \times t$, where $I$ = current in Amperes, $t$ = time in seconds)
* **$M$** = Molar Mass of the substance (g/mol)
* **$n$** = Number of electrons transferred per ion (e.g., for $\text{Cu}^{2+}$, $n=2$)
* **$F$** = Faraday's Constant = **96,485 Coulombs/mol** (the charge of one mole of electrons)
