# Percent Off Calculator: Retail Math Explained

Black Friday sales and clearance racks are full of confusing red tags. If a $120 jacket is "30% Off", how much does it actually cost? What if you also have a coupon for an "Additional 20% Off"?

Retailers intentionally make discount math confusing so you feel like you are saving more money than you actually are. In this guide, we will teach you how to calculate discounts in your head, explain the trick of "double discounts," and show you how to use our free [Percent Off Calculator](/en/calculators/percent-off-calculator).

---

## 🧮 How to Calculate Discounts in Your Head

You don't always need a calculator when you are standing in a store. The easiest way to calculate a discount is to find **10%** first.

To find 10% of any number, just move the decimal point one place to the left.
* 10% of $120.00 = **$12.00**
* 10% of $45.00 = **$4.50**

If a $120 jacket is **30% Off**, just find 10% ($12) and multiply it by 3!
* $12 \times 3 = **$36 Off**. 
* The jacket will cost $120 - $36 = **$84**.

## 🛑 The "Double Discount" Trick

Retailers love to advertise things like: **"Clearance! 30% Off, Plus Take an Additional 20% Off at the Register!"**

Most people add the percentages together and think they are getting 50% off. **This is a mathematical illusion.** You are actually getting much less.
The second discount is taken off the *new, lowered* price, not the original price.

**Let's do the math on a $100 shirt:**
1. **First 30% Off:** The shirt drops from $100 to **$70**.
2. **Additional 20% Off:** You take 20% off of $70 (which is $14).
3. **Final Price:** $70 - $14 = **$56**.

You saved $44, meaning your total discount was only **44%**, not 50%!
