# Buffer Calculator: Maintaining pH Stability

In chemistry, a sudden drop in pH can ruin an entire experiment. In biology, a sudden drop in blood pH can be fatal. To prevent this, scientists use **Buffer Solutions** to lock the pH of a liquid in place.

In this guide, we will explore the mechanics of buffers, how to prepare them using weak acids, and how to use our free [Buffer Calculator](/en/calculators/buffer-calculator).

---

## 🛡️ The Mechanics of a Buffer

A buffer solution is a chemical mixture that **resists changes in pH** when a small amount of strong acid or strong base is added to it. 

Buffers are always made by mixing two components:
1. **A Weak Acid** (e.g., Acetic Acid, $CH_3COOH$). This component is responsible for neutralizing any dangerous *bases* that enter the solution.
2. **Its Conjugate Base** (e.g., Acetate, $CH_3COO^-$). This component is responsible for absorbing and neutralizing any dangerous *acids* that enter the solution.

Because a buffer has both an "acid-fighter" and a "base-fighter", it can handle attacks from either direction, keeping the pH stable!

---

## ⚖️ Buffer Capacity

A buffer is not invincible. It has a limit called **Buffer Capacity**. 
If you dump an entire gallon of Hydrochloric Acid into a small beaker of buffer, the conjugate base (the "acid-fighter") will be completely consumed. Once the conjugate base is gone, the buffer is "broken," and the pH will plummet rapidly.

To increase buffer capacity, you simply need higher concentrations of the weak acid and conjugate base.
