# Electrochemical Cell Calculator: Batteries Explained

When you put a battery into a flashlight, you are utilizing a miniature **Electrochemical Cell**. Inside that tiny metal cylinder, a chemical reaction is aggressively pushing electrons through a wire to light the bulb.

To calculate exactly how much voltage (electrical push) a specific combination of metals will produce, chemists use **Standard Reduction Potentials**. In this guide, we will explain how these cells work and how to use our free [Electrochemical Cell Calculator](/en/calculators/electrochemical-cell-calculator).

---

## 🔋 How Does a Galvanic Cell Work?

A Galvanic (Voltaic) cell is composed of two different metals submerged in liquid, connected by a wire. 

1. **The Anode (Oxidation):** One metal is "weaker" and gives up its electrons. This process is called Oxidation. (Remember: *An Ox*).
2. **The Cathode (Reduction):** The other metal is "stronger" and pulls those electrons through the wire toward itself. This process is called Reduction. (Remember: *Red Cat*).

The flow of electrons from the Anode to the Cathode is what we call electricity! 

---

## 🧮 Calculating Standard Cell Potential

Every chemical has a specific "Standard Reduction Potential" ($E^\circ_{red}$) measured in Volts. This number tells you how badly that chemical wants to gain electrons. 

To find the total voltage of the battery, you subtract the potential of the Anode from the potential of the Cathode.

**The Formula:**
**$E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}$**

* **Positive $E^\circ_{cell}$:** The reaction happens spontaneously! (You built a working battery).
* **Negative $E^\circ_{cell}$:** The reaction will not happen on its own. (You built a dud, or an electrolytic cell that requires a power source).
