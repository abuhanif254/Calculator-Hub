# Projectile Motion: The Physics of Trajectories

If you fire a cannonball perfectly horizontally out of a cannon, and simultaneously drop a second cannonball from the exact same height, which one hits the ground first? 

Surprisingly, they both hit the ground at the exact same time. This mind-bending fact is the core of **Projectile Motion**. In this guide, we will explain how independent axes work and how to use our free [Projectile Motion Calculator](/en/calculators/projectile-motion-calculator).

---

## ✂️ The Secret of Projectile Motion: Independence of Axes

The secret to solving any projectile motion problem is realizing that vertical motion (up and down) and horizontal motion (left and right) are **completely independent** of each other. 

Once an object is launched into the air, gravity only pulls it *down*. Gravity does not pull it left or right. 

* **The Horizontal Axis (X):** Because there is no force pushing or pulling the object left or right (ignoring air resistance), its horizontal velocity remains constant forever. 
* **The Vertical Axis (Y):** Gravity pulls the object down at 9.8 m/s². Its vertical velocity is constantly changing.

Because the horizontal and vertical motions don't affect each other, the fired cannonball and the dropped cannonball both fall at the exact same vertical rate and hit the ground simultaneously!

---

## 📐 The Importance of Launch Angle

When you launch a projectile (like a football or an arrow), the angle at which you launch it determines how far it will go.
* **Launch at 90°:** The object goes straight up and comes straight down. Distance = 0.
* **Launch at 45°:** In a vacuum, a 45-degree angle will always produce the absolute maximum horizontal distance.
