# Ohm's Law Explained: The Foundation of Electronics

If you want to understand how a smartphone works, how to wire a house, or how to fix a broken flashlight, you have to start with **Ohm's Law**. 

Formulated by German physicist Georg Ohm in 1827, this single equation is the absolute foundation of all electrical circuitry. In this guide, we will use a simple water analogy to explain electricity, break down the formula, and show you how to use our free [Ohm's Law Calculator](/en/calculators/ohms-law-calculator).

---

## 🚰 The Water Pipe Analogy

The easiest way to understand electricity is to imagine a water tank connected to a hose. 
* **Voltage (V):** This is the *Water Pressure*. It is the force pushing the water through the hose. 
* **Current (I):** This is the *Water Flow*. It is the actual amount of water moving through the hose at any given moment.
* **Resistance (R):** This is the *Hose Size* or a kink in the hose. It restricts the flow of water. 

If you want more water to flow out of the hose (higher Current), you can either increase the pressure in the tank (higher Voltage) or use a wider hose (lower Resistance). 

---

## 📐 The Equation (V = IR)

Ohm's Law mathematically connects these three properties together. It states that the Current through a conductor is directly proportional to the Voltage across it.

**V = I * R**

* **V (Voltage):** Measured in Volts (V).
* **I (Current):** Measured in Amperes or Amps (A).
* **R (Resistance):** Measured in Ohms (Ω).

If you have a 9-Volt battery and you connect it to a circuit with 3 Ohms of resistance, you will generate exactly 3 Amps of current. *(9 = 3 * 3)*.

---

## ⚠️ Why Short Circuits are Dangerous

What happens if you connect a wire directly from the positive end of a battery to the negative end, with no lightbulb or motor in between? 

You have created a circuit with essentially **zero Resistance**. 
Look at the math: If Voltage is 9V, and Resistance is 0.001Ω, the Current (I) shoots up to 9,000 Amps! This massive surge of current creates extreme heat, which will instantly melt the wire, destroy the battery, and potentially start a fire. This is known as a **Short Circuit**.
