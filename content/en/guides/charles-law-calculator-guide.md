# Charles's Law Calculator: Volume vs. Temperature

If you leave a basketball outside on a freezing winter night, it will look slightly deflated by morning. But if you bring it inside and warm it up, it will plump right back up! Why does temperature change the size of the ball?

This phenomenon was studied by Jacques Charles in the 1780s, who discovered the direct relationship between the temperature of a gas and its volume. In this guide, we will explore how **Charles's Law** works and how to use our free [Charles's Law Calculator](/en/calculators/charles-law-calculator).

---

## 🔥 The Science of Expansion

Charles's law states that if the pressure and the amount of gas stay constant, **Volume and Temperature are directly proportional**. 
* As a gas gets hotter, the molecules move faster and push outward harder, causing the gas to **expand**.
* As a gas cools down, the molecules slow down and huddle closer together, causing the gas to **shrink**.

**Real World Example:** 
Hot air balloons work entirely on Charles's Law! A giant burner heats the air inside the balloon. As the temperature rises, the volume of the air expands drastically. This makes the air inside the balloon less dense than the cold air outside, causing the massive balloon to float!

---

## 🧮 The Charles's Law Equation

Because they are directly proportional, if you divide the Volume by the Temperature, the answer will always be exactly the same constant number.

**The Formula:**
**$\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$**

* **$V_1$ and $T_1$** = The Initial Volume and Temperature.
* **$V_2$ and $T_2$** = The Final Volume and Temperature.

*Warning:* You can use any units for Volume (Liters, mL), but you **MUST** use Kelvin for Temperature! If you use Celsius or Fahrenheit, your math will be completely wrong because those scales can be zero or negative.
