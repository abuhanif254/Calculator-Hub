# Solubility Calculator: Dissolving Chemistry

If you stir sugar into a cup of cold iced tea, the sugar often sinks to the bottom and refuses to dissolve. But if you stir sugar into a hot cup of coffee, it dissolves instantly. Why does heat change how much sugar you can dissolve? 

To understand how solids, liquids, and gases mix together, chemists study **Solubility**. In this guide, we will explain the factors that affect solubility, how to calculate molar solubility, and how to use our free [Solubility Calculator](/en/calculators/solubility-calculator).

---

## 🌡️ The Golden Rule: Temperature Matters

Solubility is defined as the maximum amount of a substance (the **solute**) that can dissolve in a given amount of liquid (the **solvent**) before the liquid becomes saturated.

For solid solutes (like sugar or salt), **temperature is the most important factor**.
* **Hot Liquids:** When you heat a liquid, the water molecules move faster and spread farther apart. This creates more "space" for solid molecules to fit in. Therefore, heating a liquid *increases* the solubility of solids.
* **Cold Liquids:** The molecules are packed tightly together, leaving less room for the solute.

*(Note: The opposite is true for gases! If you heat up a soda, the dissolved carbon dioxide gas escapes quickly, making the soda flat. Heating a liquid *decreases* the solubility of gases).*

---

## 🧮 Calculating Molar Solubility

Instead of measuring solubility in "grams per cup," chemists measure it in **Moles per Liter (Molarity)**. This is called Molar Solubility. 

To find the Molar Solubility, you need to know the mass of the solid that dissolved, the molar mass of that solid (from the periodic table), and the total volume of the liquid.

**The Steps:**
1. Convert the mass of the dissolved solid into Moles. *(Moles = Mass / Molar Mass)*
2. Divide the Moles by the Volume of the liquid (in Liters).
3. The result is your Molar Solubility!
