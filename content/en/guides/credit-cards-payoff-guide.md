# The Snowball vs. Avalanche Method: How to Pay Off Credit Cards

Credit card debt is the most toxic form of debt in existence. With average interest rates hovering around 24%, making only the minimum monthly payments guarantees that you will remain in debt for decades, paying thousands of dollars in interest for items you bought years ago.

If you want to achieve financial freedom, you need a strategy. In this guide, we will break down the two most famous debt-destruction methods and show you how to use our free [Credit Card Payoff Calculator](/en/calculators/credit-cards-payoff) to escape the trap.

---

## ❄️ The Debt Snowball Method

Popularized by financial guru Dave Ramsey, the Debt Snowball method focuses on **psychology and motivation** rather than pure math.

**How it works:**
1. List all your credit card balances from the smallest amount to the largest amount, regardless of the interest rate.
2. Pay the absolute minimum payment on all large cards.
3. Throw every extra dollar you have at the **smallest balance**.
4. Once the smallest card is paid off, take the money you were paying on it and "snowball" it into the next smallest card.

**Why it works:** Human beings need quick wins. Paying off a $500 balance gives you a massive dopamine hit and the motivation to keep going, even if a $10,000 card has a higher interest rate.

---

## 🏔️ The Debt Avalanche Method

The Debt Avalanche method ignores human psychology and focuses purely on **mathematical efficiency**.

**How it works:**
1. List all your credit card balances from the highest interest rate to the lowest interest rate, regardless of the balance size.
2. Pay the absolute minimum on all cards.
3. Throw every extra dollar you have at the card with the **highest interest rate**.
4. Once the highest-rate card is paid off, move to the next highest rate.

**Why it works:** Mathematically, this is the absolute fastest way to get out of debt. By killing the most expensive debt first, you save the maximum amount of money on interest.

---

## ⚖️ Which Method is Better?

* Choose the **Snowball Method** if you get discouraged easily and need to feel a sense of progress quickly.
* Choose the **Avalanche Method** if you are highly disciplined, love spreadsheets, and want to save every possible penny.
