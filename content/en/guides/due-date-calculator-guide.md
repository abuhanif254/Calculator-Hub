An Estimated Due Date (EDD) marks **40 weeks and 0 days (280 days)** of gestational age from the first day of your Last Menstrual Period (LMP) on a 28-day cycle, or **38 weeks (266 days)** from the day of fertilization. Although only 4% to 5% of babies arrive on the exact EDD, clinical dating anchors every prenatal milestone—from first-trimester genetic screening and the 20-week anatomy scan to third-trimester growth monitoring and induction timing.

Standard dating begins with Naegele's Rule, then refines the calendar projection using your actual menstrual cycle length, exact assisted-reproduction transfer dates, or a first-trimester Crown-Rump Length (CRL) ultrasound. ## Naegele's Rule, Cycle Adjustments, and IVF Equations

Formulated by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele in 1812, classical **Naegele's Rule** estimates the EDD by taking LMP Day 1, adding 7 days, subtracting 3 months, and adding 1 year. While convenient on paper, subtracting 3 calendar months introduces a 1-to-3-day error depending on whether February (28 or 29 days) or 30-day months fall inside the interval. Modern clinical calculators eliminate month-length drift by counting exact calendar days and adjusting for cycles that deviate from 28 days:

```text
Standard LMP Method        = LMP Day 1 + 280 days
Cycle-Adjusted LMP Method  = LMP Day 1 + 280 + (Cycle Length − 28) days
Conception Date Method     = Conception Date + 266 days
IVF Day-3 Embryo Transfer  = Transfer Date + 263 days   (266 − 3)
IVF Day-5/6 Blastocyst     = Transfer Date + 261 days   (266 − 5)
```

### Parameter Definitions and Clinical Logic

- **LMP Day 1:** The first day of normal menstrual bleeding. For cycles longer or shorter than 28 days, the adjustment `(Cycle Length − 28)` assumes a stable 14-day post-ovulatory luteal phase, shifting the EDD forward for long cycles and backward for short cycles.
- **Conception Anchor (`266 days`):** Represents median human gestation from fertilization to spontaneous delivery (`38 weeks × 7 days`).
- **IVF Day-5/6 Equivalence:** Under American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) and American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM) standards, both Day-5 and Day-6 expanded blastocysts are assigned a 261-day offset from transfer (`equivalent LMP = Transfer Date − 19 days`), because Day-6 embryos typically reflect slower pre-implantation cleavage rather than an extra day of post-implantation development.

## Worked Numerical Examples: Natural Cycle vs. IVF Transfer

### Example 1: Cycle-Adjusted LMP Calculation (33-Day Cycle)

Suppose your LMP began on **January 10, 2026** (a non-leap year) and cycles average **33 days**:

1. **Cycle Adjustment & Total Offset:** `33 − 28 = +5 days`, so total days added = `280 + 5 = 285 days`.
2. **Exact Day Count from January 10, 2026:** Remaining days in January (`21`) plus February through September (`242`) equals `263` cumulative days, leaving `22` days (`285 − 263`) in October -> **October 22, 2026**.

Applying paper Naegele's Rule (`Jan 10 + 7 days = Jan 17`, minus 3 months) produces **October 17, 2026**—five days early, which risks misclassifying a late-October fetus as post-term.

### Example 2: Frozen Day-5 Blastocyst IVF Transfer

Suppose a patient undergoes a Day-5 frozen embryo transfer on **February 14, 2026**:

1. **Select IVF Offset:** Add `261 days` (`266 − 5`).
2. **Exact Day Count from February 14, 2026:** Remaining days in February (`14`) plus March through October (`245`) equals `259` cumulative days, leaving `2` days (`261 − 259`) in November -> **November 2, 2026**.
3. **Equivalent LMP for Charting:** `February 14 − 19 days = January 26, 2026` (`January 26 + 280 days = November 2, 2026`).

## Gestational Milestones, Trimesters, and Term Classifications

Clinicians divide the 40-week timeline into three trimesters and four formal term classifications defined by ACOG and the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM). Each classification carries specific neonatal outcome benchmarks:

| Pregnancy Stage | Gestational Age Range | Key Clinical Screening & Milestones | Neonatal / Delivery Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| First Trimester | `0w0d` to `13w6d` | Viability scan, CRL dating, NIPT / NT screening (10–13w) | Highest miscarriage risk; major organogenesis completes |
| Second Trimester | `14w0d` to `27w6d` | Anatomy ultrasound (18–22w), gestational diabetes test (24–28w) | Fetal movement felt (16–22w); viability threshold ~23–24w |
| Third Trimester | `28w0d` to `40w+` | RhoGAM if Rh-negative (28w), Tdap (27–36w), GBS swab (36–37w) | Rapid fetal weight gain (~0.5 lb/week after 34w) |
| Early Term | `37w0d` to `38w6d` | Non-indicated elective delivery generally avoided before `39w0d` | Slightly higher NICU respiratory support vs. full term |
| Full Term | `39w0d` to `40w6d` | Optimal window for scheduled induction or repeat C-section | Lowest overall neonatal morbidity and mortality |
| Late Term & Post-Term | `41w0d` to `41w6d` / `42w0d+` | Biophysical profiles, non-stress tests, induction counseling | Rising risk of placental aging and meconium aspiration |

## ACOG Redating Guidelines, Multiples, and Clinical Caveats

Because late-ovulation pregnancies routinely fail standard LMP assumptions, ACOG Committee Opinion 700 establishes strict thresholds for **ultrasound redating**. Before `9w0d`, an EDD is updated if the Crown-Rump Length differs from LMP math by **more than 5 days**; between `9w0d` and `13w6d`, the threshold widens to **more than 7 days**. Between `14w0d` and `15w6d`, clinicians redate only at **>10 days** discrepancy, and between `16w0d` and `21w6d` at **>14 days**.

Two exceptions override ultrasound redating. First, **IVF pregnancies are never redated** by ultrasound measurements, because fertilization timing is known to the hour; if a fetus measures smaller than expected in an IVF pregnancy, clinicians evaluate growth restriction rather than shifting the calendar. Second, **twin and triplet pregnancies** retain the standard 40-week EDD for growth tracking, even though spontaneous labor or medically indicated delivery occurs earlier—typically `37w`–`38w` for dichorionic-diamniotic twins and `34w`–`37w` for monochorionic twins.

Finally, treat your EDD as the center of a normal statistical window rather than a hard deadline. First-time parents without medical inductions commonly deliver a few days past `40w0d`, while subsequent pregnancies trend closer to `39w`–`40w`.
