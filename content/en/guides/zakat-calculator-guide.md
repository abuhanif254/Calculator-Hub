# Zakat Calculator: Understanding Nisab and Wealth

Zakat is the third pillar of Islam, requiring adult Muslims whose wealth exceeds a specific threshold (Nisab) to donate 2.5% of their eligible wealth to those in need. 

Because wealth is stored in many different forms today—cash, gold, stocks, and business inventory—calculating exactly how much you owe can be complicated. In this guide, we will explain the concept of Nisab, what assets are Zakatable, and how to use our free [Zakat Calculator](/en/calculators/zakat-calculator).

---

## ⚖️ What is the Nisab?

The **Nisab** is the minimum threshold of wealth you must own for one full lunar (Hijri) year before you are required to pay Zakat. If your total wealth drops below this threshold during the year, you do not owe Zakat.

The Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) set the Nisab based on two precious metals:
1. **The Gold Standard:** 87.48 grams of pure gold.
2. **The Silver Standard:** 612.36 grams of pure silver.

Because the price of silver is currently much lower than gold, the Silver Nisab threshold is much lower. Many modern Islamic scholars recommend using the **Silver Standard** to calculate your Nisab. This ensures more people pay Zakat, maximizing the benefit to the poor.

---

## 💰 What Assets Are "Zakatable"?

You do not pay Zakat on everything you own. Personal items you use for daily life (like your house, your car, your clothes, and your smartphone) are **exempt** from Zakat.

You must pay Zakat (2.5%) on wealth that has the potential to grow:
* **Cash:** In bank accounts or under your mattress.
* **Gold and Silver:** Jewelry bought for investment or hoarding (scholarly opinions vary on jewelry for daily wear).
* **Investments:** Stocks, mutual funds, and crypto (specifically the dividend or the total value, depending on intent).
* **Business Assets:** Inventory meant for sale, and raw materials.

*(Note: Debts you owe to others should generally be subtracted from your total wealth before calculating Zakat).*
