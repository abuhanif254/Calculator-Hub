# Calorimetry Calculator: Measuring Heat Transfer

If you want to know exactly how many calories are in a piece of food, you can't just guess. Scientists figure it out by literally setting the food on fire and measuring how much heat it produces! 

The device used to capture and measure this heat is called a **Calorimeter**. In this guide, we will explain the science of calorimetry, the math behind it, and how to use our free [Calorimetry Calculator](/en/calculators/calorimetry-calculator).

---

## 🔬 How Does a Calorimeter Work?

A calorimeter is essentially a highly insulated container (like a high-tech thermos). Inside the container is a specific amount of water, and a thermometer.

When a chemical reaction (or combustion) occurs inside the calorimeter, the heat has nowhere to escape. It is forced to go into the surrounding water. By measuring exactly how much the temperature of the water rises, scientists can calculate the exact amount of energy the reaction produced.

---

## 🧮 The Calorimetry Equation

To calculate the heat energy ($q$), you need three pieces of information about the substance absorbing the heat (usually water):
1. **Mass ($m$):** How much water is there?
2. **Specific Heat Capacity ($c$):** How hard is it to heat up this substance? (For water, $c = 4.184 \text{ J/g}^\circ\text{C}$).
3. **Change in Temperature ($\Delta T$):** The final temperature minus the initial temperature.

**The Formula:**
**$q = m \times c \times \Delta T$**

*(Note: Because of the Law of Conservation of Energy, the heat gained by the water is exactly equal to the heat lost by the reaction! $q_{water} = -q_{reaction}$).*
