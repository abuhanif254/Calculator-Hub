# The Energy of Motion: Understanding Kinetic Energy

Everything that moves possesses energy. A rolling bowling ball, a flying bullet, and a running cheetah all have something in common: **Kinetic Energy**. 

In physics, kinetic energy is the energy an object has due to its motion. In this guide, we will break down the formula, explain why speed is so incredibly dangerous in car crashes, and show you how to use our free [Kinetic Energy Calculator](/en/calculators/kinetic-energy-calculator).

---

## 🏃 What is Kinetic Energy?

Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred. 
When you throw a baseball, your arm transfers energy into the ball, giving it *Kinetic Energy*. When the ball hits the catcher's mitt, it transfers that kinetic energy into the glove, creating a loud *smack* and pushing the catcher's hand backward.

**The Equation:** *KE = ½ * m * v²*

* **KE:** Kinetic Energy, measured in **Joules (J)**.
* **m:** Mass of the object in kilograms (kg).
* **v:** Velocity (speed) of the object in meters per second (m/s).

---

## 💥 Why Speed Kills: The V² Factor

Look closely at the formula: *KE = ½ * m * v²*. 
Notice that mass (m) is normal, but velocity (v) is **squared**. This has terrifying implications for car crashes.

If you double the *mass* of a car, you double its kinetic energy. 
But if you double the *speed* of a car, you **quadruple** its kinetic energy!

* **Example 1:** A 1,000 kg car driving at 20 m/s (45 mph).
  * KE = ½ * 1000 * (20 * 20) = **200,000 Joules**.
* **Example 2:** The same car driving at 40 m/s (90 mph).
  * KE = ½ * 1000 * (40 * 40) = **800,000 Joules**.

By just doubling your speed from 45 mph to 90 mph, your car now has 4 times as much destructive energy that must be absorbed by the airbags and crumple zones if you hit a wall!
