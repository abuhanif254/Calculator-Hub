# Solar Panels: Sizing Your Off-Grid System

Transitioning your home or RV to solar power is an exciting project, but it requires a lot of careful math. If you underestimate your daily power consumption or overestimate the amount of sunlight you get, you will run out of power exactly when you need it most.

In this guide, we will explain the basics of solar power generation, the crucial concept of "Peak Sun Hours," and how to use our free [Solar Panel Calculator](/en/calculators/solar-panel-calculator).

---

## ☀️ How Do Solar Panels Work?

Solar panels are made of silicon photovoltaic (PV) cells. When sunlight hits these cells, it knocks electrons loose from their atoms, generating an electrical current. 
This current is sent to a **Charge Controller**, which safely feeds the electricity into a massive battery bank for storage.

A 100-Watt solar panel can, in theory, generate 100 Watts of power per hour. But there is a catch.

---

## ⛅ The "Peak Sun Hours" Catch

You might think that if the sun is up for 12 hours a day, a 100W panel will generate 1,200 Watt-hours of energy. **This is completely false.** 

Solar panels only generate their maximum rated wattage when the sun is directly overhead in a clear sky. During the early morning, late afternoon, or on cloudy days, they generate a fraction of their rated power. 

To simplify this, solar installers use **Peak Sun Hours**. A Peak Sun Hour is equivalent to one hour of full, 100% intensity sunlight. 
Even if the sun is technically up for 12 hours, your specific location might only receive **4 to 5 Peak Sun Hours** per day! 

Therefore, a 100W panel getting 5 Peak Sun Hours will only generate 500 Watt-hours of usable energy per day.

---

## 🛑 Factoring in Efficiency Losses

Solar systems are not perfectly efficient.
* The panels get hot (heat reduces efficiency).
* There is natural resistance in the wiring.
* The Charge Controller wastes some energy.
* The Inverter wastes energy converting DC to AC.

A good rule of thumb is to assume a **20% system loss** when designing your solar array.
