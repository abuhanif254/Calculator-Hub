# Balancing Chemical Equations: The Law of Conservation

If you bake a cake with 500 grams of ingredients, the final cake will weigh exactly 500 grams. The ingredients didn't disappear; they just rearranged into a different shape. 

The same applies to chemistry! The **Law of Conservation of Mass** states that matter cannot be created or destroyed. In any chemical reaction, the atoms you start with must perfectly match the atoms you end up with. In this guide, we will explain how to balance equations and how to use our free [Balancing Chemical Equations Calculator](/en/calculators/balancing-chemical-equations-calculator).

---

## ⚖️ Subscripts vs. Coefficients

When looking at a chemical formula like $2 \text{ H}_2\text{O}$, you will see two different types of numbers:

1. **Subscripts (The little numbers):** The small "$2$" in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ means there are two Hydrogen atoms bonded to one Oxygen atom. **NEVER change subscripts!** If you change $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ to $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, it's no longer water; it's toxic hydrogen peroxide!
2. **Coefficients (The big numbers):** The large "$2$" at the front means there are *two separate molecules* of water. When balancing an equation, you are only allowed to change these big coefficients!

---

## 🧮 How to Balance an Equation

1. **Count the Atoms:** Make a list of every element on the left side (Reactants) and the right side (Products).
2. **Add Coefficients:** Place numbers in front of the molecules to multiply their atoms. (e.g., placing a 3 in front of $\text{CO}_2$ gives you 3 Carbons and 6 Oxygens).
3. **Recount:** Update your list.
4. **Repeat:** Continue bouncing back and forth until the left side matches the right side perfectly!

*Pro-Tip:* Always leave Oxygen and Hydrogen for last, as they tend to appear in multiple molecules and can get messy.
