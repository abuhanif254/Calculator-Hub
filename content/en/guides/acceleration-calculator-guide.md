# The Physics of Acceleration: More Than Just Going Fast

When you press the gas pedal in your car, you feel yourself being pushed back into your seat. That feeling is not speed—it is **Acceleration**. 

In physics, acceleration is the rate at which an object changes its velocity. It is a fundamental concept in mechanics and kinematics. In this guide, we will break down how acceleration is calculated, the concept of deceleration, and how to use our free [Acceleration Calculator](/en/calculators/acceleration-calculator).

---

## 🏎️ What Exactly is Acceleration?

Just as velocity is the rate of change of *position*, acceleration is the rate of change of *velocity*. 

If you are driving down a highway at a constant 60 mph on cruise control, your velocity is 60 mph, but your acceleration is **zero**. Because your speed is not changing, you are not accelerating. You only accelerate when you speed up, slow down, or change direction.

**The Formula:**
*a = (v_f - v_i) / Δt*
*(Acceleration = Change in Velocity / Change in Time)*

* **a:** Acceleration (measured in meters per second squared, m/s²).
* **v_f:** Final Velocity.
* **v_i:** Initial Velocity.
* **Δt:** Time it took for the change to occur.

---

## 📉 Deceleration (Negative Acceleration)

In physics, "deceleration" is just acceleration with a negative sign. 
Because velocity is a vector quantity (it has a direction), acceleration is also a vector quantity. 

If you are driving forward at 20 m/s and you slam on the brakes, your velocity is decreasing. Your final velocity will be smaller than your initial velocity, resulting in a negative number when you plug it into the formula. This negative acceleration means the force of acceleration is pushing in the *opposite* direction of your movement.

---

## 🍎 The Constant Acceleration of Gravity

The most famous example of acceleration is gravity. 
If you drop an apple from a tall building, it doesn't fall at a constant speed. It accelerates. 
On Earth, the acceleration due to gravity is approximately **9.8 m/s²**. 
This means that for every second the apple is falling, its velocity increases by 9.8 meters per second!
