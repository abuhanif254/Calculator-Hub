# Cash Back vs. Low Interest: Which Car Deal is Better?

When you walk into a car dealership to buy a new vehicle, you are often presented with a difficult choice: **Take a large Cash Back Rebate** OR **Take a Low (or 0%) Interest Rate.**

Car manufacturers rarely let you combine these two offers. At first glance, a 0% interest rate seems like an unbeatable deal. Why pay thousands in interest when you can pay none? But skipping a massive cash rebate means financing a much larger initial loan amount. 

In this guide, we will break down the math behind these dealer incentives and show you how to use our free [Cash Back vs. Low Interest Calculator](/en/calculators/cash-back-vs-low-interest-calculator) to guarantee you get the best deal.

---

## 💵 Option A: The Cash Back Rebate

When you choose the cash back option, the manufacturer directly subtracts a lump sum (e.g., $3,000) from the purchase price of the car. 

* **The Catch:** Because you took the rebate, you must now finance the car at a standard, higher bank interest rate (e.g., 6%).
* **When it wins:** The cash rebate is usually the better option if you plan to pay the loan off quickly, or if you are bringing a large down payment to the table. By lowering the principal amount immediately, you owe less overall, and the higher interest rate has less time (and less money) to compound against you.

---

## 📉 Option B: The Low Interest Rate (0% Financing)

When you choose the low interest rate, the dealer subsidizes your loan, often dropping the interest rate to 0% or 0.9% for the duration of the term.

* **The Catch:** Because you took the low rate, you forfeit the $3,000 cash rebate. You must borrow the full price of the car.
* **When it wins:** The low interest rate is usually better if you are taking out a long loan (e.g., 60 or 72 months) and putting very little money down. Over 6 years, a standard 6% interest rate can easily cost more than $3,000, making the 0% financing the mathematically superior choice.

---

## 🧮 How to Compare the Offers

To truly know which offer is better, you cannot just look at the monthly payment. You must calculate the **Total Out-of-Pocket Cost** over the entire life of the loan for both scenarios.

**Scenario:** $30,000 Car. 
**Offer 1:** $3,000 Cash Back + 6% Interest for 60 months.
**Offer 2:** $0 Cash Back + 0% Interest for 60 months.

* **Offer 1 Math:** You finance $27,000 at 6%. The total interest paid over 5 years is roughly $4,320. Total out-of-pocket: **$31,320**.
* **Offer 2 Math:** You finance $30,000 at 0%. You pay $0 in interest. Total out-of-pocket: **$30,000**.

In this specific scenario, the 0% interest saves you $1,320!
