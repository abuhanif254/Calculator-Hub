# Battery Runtime: How Long Will It Last?

Whether you are building a custom drone, wiring a camper van, or just buying a power bank for your phone, the most important question is always: **"How long will this battery last?"**

In this guide, we will explain how battery capacity is measured, why you can never actually use 100% of a battery's rated capacity, and how to use our free [Battery Runtime Calculator](/en/calculators/battery-runtime-calculator).

---

## 🔋 Understanding Battery Capacity (mAh & Ah)

Battery capacity is simply a measure of how much electrical energy a battery can store. It is usually measured in **Ampere-hours (Ah)** or **Milliampere-hours (mAh)** for smaller devices. 

* **1 Ampere-hour (1 Ah):** The battery can deliver exactly 1 Amp of current for 1 hour before dying.
* **100 Ampere-hours (100 Ah):** A massive RV battery that can deliver 1 Amp for 100 hours, OR 10 Amps for 10 hours. (It is a straight ratio).

If your smartphone battery is rated for **3,000 mAh**, it means it can deliver 3,000 milliamps (3 Amps) of current for exactly one hour. 

---

## 📉 Peukert's Law and Efficiency Losses

In a perfect mathematical world, a 100 Ah battery would run a 10 Amp device for exactly 10 hours. In the real world, this is false. 

**1. High Draw Penalty (Peukert's Law):** 
If you drain a battery very quickly, it becomes less efficient. A 100 Ah lead-acid battery might provide 10 Amps for 10 hours, but if you try to pull a massive 100 Amps from it, it will *not* last for 1 hour. It will likely die in 30 minutes! The faster you pull current, the less total capacity the battery has.

**2. Inverter Efficiency:**
If you are using a 12V battery to power a 120V household TV, you have to use an Inverter. Inverters are not 100% efficient; they usually waste about 10% to 15% of the battery's energy as heat during the conversion process.

---

## 🛑 The Depth of Discharge (DoD) Rule

You should almost never drain a battery to 0%. 
For traditional Lead-Acid batteries (like car batteries), draining them below 50% capacity causes permanent chemical damage and ruins their lifespan. You only actually get to use half of their rated capacity!

Lithium-ion batteries (like in your phone) are much better, but routinely draining them to 0% will still degrade them faster. It is best to treat a lithium battery as having a 20% "unusable" reserve at the bottom.
