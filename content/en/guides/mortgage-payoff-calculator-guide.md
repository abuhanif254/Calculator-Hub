# How to Pay Off Your Mortgage Years Early and Save Thousands

When you sign a 30-year mortgage, the bank front-loads the interest. For the first several years, almost your entire monthly payment goes directly into the bank's pockets, while only a tiny fraction actually pays down the principal balance of your house. 

To beat the bank at their own game, you have to break the schedule. In this guide, we will show you how making extra payments destroys the bank's amortization schedule, and how to use our free [Mortgage Payoff Calculator](/en/calculators/mortgage-payoff-calculator) to see your savings.

---

## 🔨 The Power of Extra Principal Payments

Because the bank charges interest based on your *current* principal balance, reducing that balance faster than scheduled has a massive ripple effect.

Every time you make an extra payment directly toward the "Principal Only," you are not just paying off debt—you are permanently canceling all the future interest that the bank was going to charge you on that specific money for the next 30 years.

**The Math:** 
Imagine you have a $300,000 mortgage at 7% interest for 30 years.
Your normal monthly payment (Principal & Interest) is $1,995.
If you simply pay the normal $1,995 every month, you will end up paying **$418,000 in pure interest** over the life of the loan.

* But, if you add just **$200 extra per month** toward the principal:
* You will pay the house off **6 years early**.
* You will save over **$100,000 in interest**!

---

## 📅 The Bi-Weekly Payment Hack

If you cannot afford to add a flat $200 extra per month, there is a psychological trick you can use to trick yourself into paying off the mortgage early.

Instead of making one full payment every month, set up your account to pay **half** of your mortgage every **two weeks**. 

Because there are 52 weeks in a year, you will end up making 26 half-payments. This equals exactly 13 full monthly payments per year. 
By seamlessly making one "extra" payment a year without even feeling it in your budget, you will shave roughly **4 to 5 years** off your 30-year mortgage and save tens of thousands of dollars in interest.
