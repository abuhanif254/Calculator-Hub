# Understanding VAT: The Global Standard for Sales Tax

If you run a business in Europe, Asia, or almost anywhere outside the United States, you must deal with **Value Added Tax (VAT)**. 

Unlike a simple sales tax that is only charged once at the cash register, VAT is collected at every single stage of the supply chain. In this guide, we will explain the mechanics of VAT and show you how to use our free [VAT Calculator](/en/calculators/vat-calculator) to properly price your goods.

---

## 🌍 How VAT Works (The Supply Chain)

Imagine a wooden chair is manufactured and sold in a country with a 20% VAT rate.
1. **The Lumberjack:** Chops down a tree and sells the wood to a factory for $100 + $20 VAT = $120. The lumberjack sends that $20 to the government.
2. **The Factory:** Builds a chair and sells it to a retailer for $200 + $40 VAT = $240. The factory sends $40 to the government, *but* they claim a credit for the $20 VAT they already paid the lumberjack. 
3. **The Retailer:** Sells the chair to a customer for $300 + $60 VAT = $360. The retailer sends $60 to the government, but claims a credit for the $40 VAT they paid the factory.

At the end of the chain, the end consumer bears the entire burden of the $60 tax, but the government collected it in small, manageable pieces along the way.

---

## 🧾 Inclusive vs. Exclusive Pricing

When you travel to the United States, the price on the shelf says $10, but when you get to the register, you are charged $10.70 because sales tax is added at checkout. This is **Exclusive Pricing**.

In countries with VAT, the price on the shelf is almost always **Inclusive**. If the tag says €10, you hand the cashier exactly €10. The 20% VAT is already baked into that price. 
* As a business owner, you must know how to extract the VAT from your gross sales to know what your actual revenue is.
