# How Annuities Work: Buying a Guaranteed Monthly Paycheck

The biggest fear most retirees have is outliving their savings. If the stock market crashes early in your retirement, selling stocks to pay your bills can permanently devastate your portfolio.

To eliminate this risk, many retirees purchase an **Annuity**. In simple terms, an annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You give them a lump sum of money today, and they guarantee to send you a fixed monthly paycheck for the rest of your life. 

In this guide, we will break down the different types of annuities and show you how to use our free [Annuity Calculator](/en/calculators/annuity-calculator) to see if buying one is the right move for you.

## 🛡️ The Three Main Types of Annuities

Not all annuities are created equal. They generally fall into three categories:

1. **Fixed Annuities (Safest):** You give the insurance company your money, and they guarantee a specific, fixed interest rate. Your monthly payouts will never change, regardless of what the stock market does. This provides ultimate peace of mind, but inflation may slowly erode the purchasing power of that fixed check.
2. **Variable Annuities (High Risk/High Reward):** Your money is invested into mutual funds. If the market goes up, your monthly payouts go up. If the market crashes, your monthly payouts will drop.
3. **Indexed Annuities (The Middle Ground):** Your returns are tied to a stock market index (like the S&P 500), but with a "floor" and a "cap." If the market crashes 20%, your floor guarantees you don't lose any money. However, if the market goes up 20%, your cap might limit your gains to only 6%. 

---

## 💸 The Pros and Cons

**Pros:**
* **Guaranteed Income:** With a fixed annuity, you can literally never run out of money. Even if you live to be 110 and the insurance company pays out double what you originally gave them, they must keep writing the checks.
* **Tax-Deferred Growth:** Like a 401(k), the money inside an annuity grows without being taxed until you withdraw it.

**Cons:**
* **High Fees:** Variable and Indexed annuities often come with complex, hidden fees that can eat into your returns.
* **Illiquidity:** Once you give the insurance company your lump sum, you usually cannot get it back in an emergency without paying massive surrender charges.
* **Loss of Inheritance:** With a standard annuity, when you die, the payments stop. The insurance company keeps whatever is left of your original lump sum; you cannot pass it on to your children (unless you purchase a specific "survivor" rider).
