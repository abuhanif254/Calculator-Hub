# Business Loan Guide: Financing Your Company's Growth

Whether you are a solo entrepreneur buying your first piece of heavy machinery, or a growing startup needing capital to hire employees, business loans are the fuel that powers commercial expansion. 

However, commercial debt operates very differently than consumer debt. In this guide, we will break down the types of business financing, how banks assess commercial risk, and how to use our free [Business Loan Calculator](/en/calculators/business-loan-calculator) to ensure your new debt doesn't destroy your cash flow.

---

## 🏢 Types of Commercial Financing

Business financing is not a one-size-fits-all product. Choosing the wrong type of loan can severely hinder your daily operations.

### 1. Traditional Term Loans
This is the standard model. A bank gives you a lump sum of cash (e.g., $100,000) upfront. You agree to pay it back over a fixed term (e.g., 5 years) with a fixed interest rate.
* **Best for:** Large, one-time investments with a predictable return on investment (ROI), such as buying a new warehouse, purchasing a fleet of vehicles, or acquiring a competitor.

### 2. Business Lines of Credit (LOC)
A line of credit acts more like a massive credit card. The bank approves you for a maximum limit (e.g., $50,000). You only draw the money when you need it, and you *only pay interest on the money you have actually drawn*.
* **Best for:** Smoothing out unpredictable cash flow, covering payroll during slow seasonal months, or handling unexpected emergency repairs.

### 3. SBA Loans (Small Business Administration)
In the United States, the SBA partially guarantees loans issued by participating lenders. Because the government is reducing the bank's risk, these loans often feature the lowest interest rates and longest repayment terms available on the market.
* **The Catch:** The application process is notoriously rigorous, often taking months to get approved.

## ⚖️ How Lenders Assess Business Risk (The 5 C's)

When applying for a commercial loan, underwriters look far beyond just your personal credit score. They analyze the "5 C's of Credit":
1. **Character:** The business owner's personal credit history and industry experience.
2. **Capacity:** The business's historical cash flow. Can the company's revenue support the new monthly loan payment?
3. **Capital:** How much "skin in the game" the owner has (personal money invested).
4. **Collateral:** The physical assets (real estate, equipment, inventory) the bank can seize if the business defaults.
5. **Conditions:** The current economic climate of your specific industry.
