# Electrical Energy: Understanding Your Power Bill

Every month, you receive a bill from your utility company. They don't bill you for the *Power* you use; they bill you for the **Energy** you consume. What is the difference? 

In this guide, we will explain how electrical energy is measured, the difference between Watts and Watt-hours, and how to use our free [Electrical Energy Calculator](/en/calculators/electrical-energy-calculator) to predict your next power bill.

---

## ⚡ Power vs. Energy

* **Power (Watts):** The *rate* at which electricity is being used at this exact second. (e.g., A 100-Watt lightbulb).
* **Energy (Joules or Watt-hours):** The total *amount* of electricity used over a specific period of time. 

If you turn on a 100-Watt lightbulb for 1 hour, it consumes 100 Watt-hours (Wh) of energy. If you leave that same 100-Watt bulb on for 10 hours, it consumes 1000 Watt-hours (1 kWh) of energy. 

The power of the bulb never changed (it's always 100W), but the Energy consumed increased as time passed.

---

## 🧾 What is a Kilowatt-Hour (kWh)?

A Joule is a very tiny amount of energy, so utility companies use a much larger unit called the **Kilowatt-Hour (kWh)**. 
* 1 Kilowatt (kW) = 1,000 Watts
* 1 Kilowatt-Hour (kWh) = 1,000 Watts of power sustained for 1 full hour. (Which is equal to 3,600,000 Joules).

When you look at your electricity bill, you are charged a specific rate (e.g., $0.15) for every kWh of energy your home consumed that month. 

---

## 🔋 Battery Capacity (mAh)

If you look at the specs for a smartphone battery, you will see a rating like "3,000 mAh" (Milliampere-hours). This is also a measure of Energy! It means the battery can deliver 3,000 milliamps of current for exactly 1 hour before dying (or 1,500 milliamps for 2 hours, etc.).
