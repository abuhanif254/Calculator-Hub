# HELOC Explained: Should You Use Your House as an ATM?

If you have owned your home for a few years, you have likely built up significant equity. A **Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)** allows you to borrow against that equity without having to sell your house or refinance your primary mortgage.

While HELOCs are a popular way to fund home renovations or pay off high-interest credit card debt, they come with unique structural risks that trap many homeowners. In this guide, we will explain exactly how a HELOC works and show you how to use our free [HELOC Calculator](/en/calculators/heloc-calculator) to avoid the "payment shock."

---

## 💳 What is a HELOC?

A HELOC is essentially a giant credit card where your house serves as the collateral. 
Unlike a standard loan where you receive a lump sum of cash on day one, a HELOC gives you a "credit limit" (e.g., $50,000). You can borrow from that limit whenever you want, pay it back, and borrow it again.

Because the loan is secured by your house, the interest rates are usually much lower than personal loans or credit cards. However, **if you fail to make your HELOC payments, the bank can foreclose on your house.**

---

## ⏳ The Two Phases of a HELOC

The biggest danger of a HELOC lies in its two-part structure.

**Phase 1: The Draw Period (Usually 10 Years)**
During the first 10 years, you are allowed to borrow money from the credit line. More importantly, the bank usually only requires you to make **Interest-Only Payments**. 
* *The Trap:* Because you are not required to pay down the principal, your monthly payments are incredibly low. This tricks people into borrowing more than they can afford.

**Phase 2: The Repayment Period (Usually 20 Years)**
After 10 years, the draw period abruptly ends. You can no longer borrow money. Now, you must pay back everything you borrowed, *plus* interest, on an accelerated 20-year schedule.
* *The Shock:* Because you are now being forced to pay down the principal, your monthly payment will suddenly skyrocket, often doubling or tripling overnight!

---

## 🛑 Variable Interest Rates

Unlike your primary 30-year fixed mortgage, almost all HELOCs have **variable interest rates**. This means your rate is tied to the Federal Reserve. 
If inflation spikes and the Fed raises interest rates, your HELOC rate will increase automatically, making your monthly payment even more expensive.
