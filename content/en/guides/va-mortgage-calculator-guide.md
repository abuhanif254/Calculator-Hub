A VA-backed mortgage allows eligible active-duty service members, veterans, National Guard and Reserve personnel, and qualifying surviving spouses to buy a primary residence with 0% down and zero monthly Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI). Because the Department of Veterans Affairs guarantees up to 25% of the loan against lender loss, VA mortgage rates typically run 0.25% to 0.50% below comparable conventional loans with flexible credit underwriting.

Instead of monthly PMI, non-exempt borrowers pay a one-time **VA Funding Fee** (1.25% to 3.30% of the base loan), which is usually rolled into principal at closing. ## Core Mechanics: Entitlement, Funding Fee, and Amortization

A VA mortgage calculation differs from a conventional loan because the financed principal includes the capitalized VA Funding Fee unless the borrower qualifies for an exemption or pays the fee in cash at closing.

```text
Base Loan (P_base)  = Purchase Price − Down Payment
Funding Fee (F)     = P_base × Funding Fee Rate
Total Loan (P_tot)  = P_base + F   (when financed)
Monthly P&I (M)     = P_tot × [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n − 1]
```

Where:
- `P_base` is purchase price minus cash down payment (`$0` for 100% financing).
- `Funding Fee Rate` depends on down payment (`<5%`, `5%–9.9%`, or `≥10%`) and prior VA loan usage (2.15% first-time vs. 3.30% subsequent at `<5%` down; 1.50% at `5%–9.9%`; 1.25% at `≥10%`).
- `r` is the monthly rate (`Annual Interest Rate / 12`) and `n` is total monthly payments (`360` for a 30-year term).

Total monthly housing cost (`PITI`) adds monthly property taxes, homeowners insurance, and HOA dues to `M`, while monthly mortgage insurance (`MI`) stays `$0` at any loan-to-value ratio. VA Streamline Refinances (IRRRL) carry a flat 0.50% fee, whereas VA cash-out refinances revert to 2.15% (first-time) or 3.30% (subsequent).

## Worked Example: $400,000 Home with Zero Down

Suppose a non-exempt, first-time VA buyer purchases a `$400,000` home with `$0` down on a 30-year fixed loan at `6.25%` APR, with `$4,800/year` property taxes (`$400/month`) and `$1,800/year` insurance (`$150/month`).

1. **Base Loan:** `P_base = $400,000 − $0 = $400,000`.
2. **First-Time Funding Fee (2.15%):** `F = $400,000 × 0.0215 = $8,600`.
3. **Total Financed Principal:** `P_tot = $400,000 + $8,600 = $408,600`.
4. **Rate and Term:** `r = 0.0625 / 12 = 0.00520833`, `n = 360`.
5. **Monthly P&I:** `M = $408,600 × [0.00520833 × (1.00520833)^360] / [(1.00520833)^360 − 1] = $2,515.82`.
6. **Total Monthly PITI:** `$2,515.82 + $400.00 + $150.00 + $0.00 (PMI) = $3,065.82`.

On the same `$400,000` house, a 5% down conventional loan (`$20,000` cash down, `$380,000` principal) at `6.625%` APR with `0.65%` PMI (`$205.83/month`) costs `$2,433.18` P&I plus `$205.83` PMI (`$2,639.01` combined)—`$123.19` more per month than the zero-down VA loan while locking up `$20,000` in upfront cash.

## Underwriting Rules, Pitfalls, and Regulatory Caveats

Even with full entitlement and no statutory loan cap, borrowers must navigate four operational constraints:

- **Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs):** VA appraisals verify safety and livability alongside market value. Peeling lead paint, roof leaks, missing handrails, or active termite issues must be remediated before closing.
- **Occupancy and Non-Allowable Fees:** VA financing applies strictly to owner-occupied primary residences (typically requiring move-in within 60 days). Lenders cannot charge borrowers attorney, escrow, or unapproved junk fees beyond the VA 1% origination limit.
- **Partial Entitlement and Second Homes:** Holding an active VA loan while buying another home caps zero-down eligibility based on local FHFA county conforming limits; any excess above 4× remaining entitlement requires a 25% down payment on the difference.
- **Retroactive Disability Refunds and Overlays:** Borrowers awarded VA disability compensation with an effective date before closing can claim a full refund of their Funding Fee.
