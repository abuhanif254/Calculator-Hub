# Understanding CDs: How to Lock in High Interest Rates

When interest rates are high, leaving your cash in a regular checking account is like setting it on fire. However, putting that cash into the stock market might be too risky if you need the money soon for a house down payment or a wedding.

A **Certificate of Deposit (CD)** offers the perfect middle ground. In this guide, we will break down how CDs work, the strategy of "CD Laddering," and how to use our free [CD Calculator](/en/calculators/cd-calculator) to maximize your risk-free returns.

---

## 🔒 What is a Certificate of Deposit?

A CD is a special type of savings account offered by banks and credit unions. 
In exchange for agreeing to leave your money untouched for a specific period of time (the "term"), the bank guarantees you a fixed interest rate (APY) that is usually higher than a standard savings account.

* **Terms:** CDs typically range from 3 months to 5 years.
* **Guaranteed Rate:** If you open a 12-month CD at 5.00% APY, you will get exactly 5.00%, even if the Federal Reserve cuts interest rates to zero the very next day.
* **The Catch:** If you withdraw your money before the term ends, the bank will hit you with an Early Withdrawal Penalty (usually sacrificing several months of earned interest).

---

## 🪜 The "CD Ladder" Strategy

Locking all your money into a 5-year CD is risky because you lose access to your cash. To solve this, savvy savers build a **CD Ladder**.

Instead of putting $10,000 into one 5-year CD, you split it into five different $2,000 CDs with staggered terms:
* CD 1: $2,000 for a 1-year term.
* CD 2: $2,000 for a 2-year term.
* CD 3: $2,000 for a 3-year term.
* CD 4: $2,000 for a 4-year term.
* CD 5: $2,000 for a 5-year term.

**Why this works:** You get to take advantage of the higher interest rates of the longer-term CDs, but a portion of your money becomes available (matures) every single year!

---

## HYSAs vs. CDs: Which is Better?

A High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA) allows you to withdraw your money anytime without penalty. A CD locks your money up. 

**So why choose a CD?**
HYSA interest rates are *variable*. If the economy crashes, the bank can lower your HYSA rate from 5% to 1% overnight. 
A CD rate is *fixed*. If you lock in a 5-year CD at 5%, the bank is legally obligated to pay you 5% every year for the next five years, no matter what happens to the economy.
