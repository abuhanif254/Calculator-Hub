# Understanding Voltage: The Pressure of Electricity

If you look closely at a standard AA battery, you will see it is labeled "1.5V". If you look at a car battery, it is labeled "12V". 
What exactly does this "V" mean, and why does a car battery have so much more of it than a AA battery?

In this guide, we will break down the concept of **Voltage**, explain electric potential difference, and show you how to use our free [Voltage Calculator](/en/calculators/voltage-calculator).

---

## ⚡ What is Voltage?

Voltage, formally known as **Electric Potential Difference**, is the pressure that pushes electrons through a conductive wire.

Without voltage, electrons sit perfectly still. They need a "push" to start flowing and doing useful work (like lighting a bulb or turning a motor). Voltage provides that push. 
* High Voltage = High Pressure (A powerful push).
* Low Voltage = Low Pressure (A weak push).

---

## 🔋 How a Battery Works

A battery has two terminals: positive (+) and negative (-). 
Inside the battery, a chemical reaction creates an intense buildup of electrons on the negative terminal, while stripping electrons from the positive terminal.

Because electrons repel each other, the crowded electrons on the negative side desperately want to cross over to the empty positive side. This "desire" to cross over is Voltage! 
The greater the difference in electrons between the two sides, the higher the Voltage. As soon as you connect a wire between the two terminals, the electrons rush across.

---

## ⚠️ Is High Voltage Dangerous?

There is an old saying in electrical engineering: *"It's not the volts that kill you, it's the amps."*
While high voltage (pressure) is what forces the electricity into your body, it is the actual flow of electrons (Current / Amps) that causes physical damage. A static shock from a doorknob can easily be 20,000 Volts, but because the Current is almost zero, it is completely harmless!
