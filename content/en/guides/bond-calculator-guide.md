# Understanding Bonds: The Foundation of a Safe Portfolio

While stocks offer high potential returns, they come with wild volatility. To stabilize a portfolio, investors buy **Bonds**. In simple terms, a bond is a loan you make to a corporation or the government. In exchange, they promise to pay you regular interest and return your original money on a specific date.

In this guide, we will explain the mechanics of bond pricing and show you how to use our free [Bond Calculator](/en/calculators/bond-calculator) to evaluate fixed-income investments.

---

## 📜 The Anatomy of a Bond

When you buy a bond when it is first issued, the math is very simple. 
* **Face Value (Par Value):** The amount the bond is worth at maturity (usually $1,000).
* **Coupon Rate:** The fixed annual interest rate the bond pays. A $1,000 bond with a 5% coupon rate pays you $50 a year.
* **Maturity Date:** The date the issuer must return your $1,000.

If you buy a newly issued bond and hold it until maturity, you simply collect your $50 a year, get your $1,000 back at the end, and you are done. 

---

## ⚖️ The Seesaw Effect: Interest Rates vs. Bond Prices

Bonds get complicated because you don't have to hold them until maturity. You can sell them to other investors on the secondary market. 

On the secondary market, **Bond Prices and Interest Rates move in opposite directions.**

Imagine you own a bond paying **5%**. Suddenly, the Federal Reserve raises interest rates, and brand new bonds are now being issued at **7%**. 
If you try to sell your 5% bond, nobody will buy it for the full $1,000, because they could just go buy a new bond paying 7%. To convince someone to buy your bond, you have to sell it at a **Discount** (e.g., $950). 

Conversely, if interest rates *drop* to 3%, your 5% bond becomes incredibly valuable. Investors will pay a **Premium** (e.g., $1,050) just to get their hands on your higher coupon rate.

---

## 🎯 Yield to Maturity (YTM)

Because bond prices fluctuate on the secondary market, the "Coupon Rate" no longer tells the whole story. If you buy a bond at a discount, your actual return will be higher than the coupon rate. If you buy at a premium, your actual return will be lower.

This actual return is called the **Yield to Maturity (YTM)**. It is the most important metric in bond investing.
