# Marriage Tax Calculator: Bonus or Penalty?

When you get married, the government changes how you file your taxes. You can choose to file as "Married Filing Jointly" or "Married Filing Separately." 

For many couples, combining their incomes into a joint tax return results in a massive tax cut (The Marriage Bonus). However, for other couples, getting married actually forces them to pay *more* taxes than they did when they were single (The Marriage Penalty). In this guide, we will explain why this happens and how to use our free [Marriage Tax Calculator](/en/calculators/marriage-tax-calculator).

---

## 💍 What is the Marriage Bonus?

A **Marriage Bonus** happens when a couple's combined tax bill is lower than the sum of their individual tax bills if they had remained single.

This almost always occurs when two people have wildly different incomes. 
For example, if Partner A makes $150,000 and Partner B makes $0 (a stay-at-home spouse), Partner A's high income is suddenly stretched across a much larger joint tax bracket. A massive portion of Partner A's income that used to be taxed at 24% will drop down into the 12% bracket! 

---

## 🛑 What is the Marriage Penalty?

A **Marriage Penalty** happens when a couple owes more taxes simply because they got married. 

This usually happens to couples who earn very similar, high incomes.
If Partner A and Partner B both earn $200,000, their combined income is $400,000. When they were single, they were safely below the highest tax brackets. But when they combine their incomes, that $400,000 pushes them into the highest possible tax bracket (e.g., 35% or 37%), forcing them to pay thousands of dollars in extra taxes just for tying the knot!
