# Potential Energy: Stored Energy Waiting to be Released

Imagine a roller coaster car slowly clicking its way up to the very top of the highest hill. At the peak, just before it drops, it is barely moving, yet it is full of energy. This stored energy is called **Potential Energy**.

In physics, potential energy is the energy an object has due to its position or state. In this guide, we will focus specifically on Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE) and show you how to use our free [Potential Energy Calculator](/en/calculators/potential-energy-calculator).

---

## 🧗 Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE)

When you lift an object off the ground, you are doing work *against* gravity. That work doesn't disappear; it gets stored in the object as Gravitational Potential Energy. The higher you lift the object, and the heavier the object is, the more potential energy it stores.

**The Equation:** *PE = m * g * h*

* **PE:** Potential Energy, measured in **Joules (J)**.
* **m:** Mass of the object in kilograms (kg).
* **g:** Acceleration due to gravity (On Earth, this is a constant **9.8 m/s²**).
* **h:** Height of the object in meters (m).

---

## 🎢 Converting Potential to Kinetic

One of the most beautiful concepts in physics is how energy transforms. 
As that roller coaster car drops down the hill, it loses height (losing Potential Energy), but it gains speed (gaining Kinetic Energy). 

If you drop a bowling ball from a 10-meter roof, at the exact moment before it hits the ground, its height is zero, meaning its Potential Energy is zero. 100% of its stored potential energy has successfully converted into Kinetic Energy! This is the Law of Conservation of Energy in action.

---

## 🪐 Gravity on Other Planets

While 'g' is 9.8 m/s² on Earth, it changes if you travel through the solar system!
* **The Moon:** 1.62 m/s²
* **Mars:** 3.71 m/s²
* **Jupiter:** 24.79 m/s²

An object lifted 10 meters on Jupiter will store vastly more potential energy than the same object lifted 10 meters on Earth.
