# Retirement Planning 101: How Much Do You Really Need?

Will you have enough money to retire comfortably? It's the most common financial question, yet many people guess at the answer. 

In this guide, we will break down the fundamental math of retirement planning, explain the famous "4% Rule," and show you how to use our free [Retirement Calculator](/en/calculators/retirement-calculator) to build a solid financial plan.

---

## 🎯 Determining Your "Nest Egg" Goal

Before you can plan how to get there, you need to know where you are going. How much money do you actually need in your retirement account on the day you retire?

### The 80% Rule
A common rule of thumb is that you will need to replace about **80% of your pre-retirement income** to maintain your current lifestyle. 
* If you make $100,000 a year now, you should aim to generate $80,000 a year in retirement (from investments, Social Security, and pensions).

### The 4% Rule (The 25x Rule)
Once you know your annual income need, how large must your portfolio be to generate that income without running out of money?
The **4% Rule** (based on the Trinity Study) suggests you can safely withdraw 4% of your portfolio each year, adjusted for inflation, for 30 years.
* Mathematically, this means you need a nest egg equal to **25 times your annual withdrawal**.
* *Example:* If you need $80,000 a year, $80,000 x 25 = $2,000,000.

---

## 📈 The Power of Compounding

The secret to reaching a multi-million dollar goal is **compound interest**. By investing a portion of your income into a diversified portfolio (like an S&P 500 index fund), your money earns returns, and then those returns earn their own returns.

This is why starting early is the most critical factor in retirement planning. Time does the heavy lifting for you.
