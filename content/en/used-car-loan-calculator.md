Buying a two- to four-year-old car lets the original owner absorb the steepest 20% to 35% depreciation hit, yet pre-owned financing follows stricter lending math than buying new. Banks treat older vehicles as riskier collateral, resulting in wider APR spreads, tighter loan-to-value (LTV) ceilings, and shorter maximum repayment windows. Running your numbers in this **Used Car Loan Calculator** before visiting a dealership separates the true price of the car from financing markups.

## How Used Auto Loan Payments Are Calculated

Most U.S. used car loans from banks, credit unions, and captive lenders are fixed-rate, simple-interest amortizing loans. Your monthly bill stays identical through payoff, while the interest-to-principal ratio shifts each cycle.

```text
M = P × [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]
```

Where:
- **M** is the fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment.
- **P** is the financed principal — your vehicle's out-the-door price (sale price plus sales tax, title, and doc fees) minus your cash down payment and positive trade-in equity.
- **r** is the monthly periodic interest rate (`APR / 100 / 12`).
- **n** is the total number of monthly payments (typically `36`, `48`, `60`, or `72`).

Because interest accrues daily on the unpaid principal balance, early payments carry a larger interest share. Sending extra principal-only payments early cuts total finance charges directly, provided your Truth in Lending contract carries no prepayment penalty.

## Worked Example: Financing an $18,500 Used Sedan

Suppose you negotiate an **$18,500** out-the-door price on a three-year-old Honda Accord and put **$2,500** down, leaving a financed principal of **$16,000**. Your credit union approves a **48-month** loan at **7.49% APR**.

1. **Monthly rate (`r`):** `7.49 / 100 / 12 = 0.00624167`.
2. **Compounding factor (`(1 + r)^48`):** `(1.00624167)^48 = 1.348063`.
3. **Monthly payment (`M`):** `$16,000 × (0.00624167 × 1.348063) / (1.348063 - 1) = $386.79`.
4. **Month 1 split:** Interest takes **$99.87** (`$16,000 × 0.00624167`), while **$286.92** cuts your principal right away.
5. **Four-year totals:** Across 48 payments, you pay **$18,565.81** to the lender — **$2,565.81** in total interest and **$21,065.81** in total vehicle cost including your down payment.

Compare that against stretching the same **$16,000** loan to **72 months**, where lenders routinely add a 0.75% rate surcharge to **8.25% APR**. Your monthly payment drops $104.30 to **$282.49**, yet total interest climbs to **$4,339.21** — an extra **$1,773.40** paid to the bank while still making payments on a nine-year-old car likely needing tires, brakes, and suspension work.

## Underwriting Rules Unique to Pre-Owned Vehicles

### Book Value and Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ceilings
Instead of MSRP, lenders underwrite used vehicles against **J.D. Power (NADA) Clean Retail** or **Kelley Blue Book** valuations. Prime credit unions generally cap LTV at **110% to 125%** of clean retail value, inclusive of tax, title, and rolled-in add-ons. Overpaying above book value or carrying negative equity from a trade-in often forces a larger cash down payment to clear underwriting.

### Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) Promotional Rates
Franchise dealers selling factory Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) models occasionally offer subsidized APRs (3.99% to 5.49% for 36–48 months) that beat credit union used rates. Compare the CPO price markup — often $1,500 to $2,500 over an identical non-certified car — against the interest saved in the calculator before assuming CPO financing wins.

## Five Used-Car Financing Traps to Avoid

- **Dealer Rate Markup ("Reserve"):** Indirect lenders approve a wholesale "buy rate" (say 7.25%) and permit dealers to pad it by 1% to 2.5%. Bring a written **credit union pre-approval** as your rate ceiling.
- **Financing F&I Add-Ons:** Rolling third-party extended warranties ($2,000–$3,500), paint sealants, VIN etching, or marked-up dealer GAP into principal means paying 7% to 14% interest on high-margin extras.
- **Skipping a Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI):** Spending $150 to $200 on an independent mechanic before closing prevents financing a car hiding a $4,000 transmission or head-gasket repair.
- **Buy-Here-Pay-Here (BHPH) Lots:** In-house BHPH dealers routinely charge 20% to 29% APR on inflated stickers and frequently skip reporting timely payments to major credit bureaus.
- **Expecting a Federal Tax Write-Off:** The 2025–2028 federal car-loan interest deduction applies exclusively to brand-new U.S.-assembled vehicles — never factor a tax deduction into a used-car budget.
