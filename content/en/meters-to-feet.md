To convert meters (`m`) to feet (`ft`), divide the metric length by `0.3048`: `ft = m ÷ 0.3048`. Equivalently, you can multiply by `3.280839895` (`1 ÷ 0.3048`). For example, `1 m` equals `3.28084 ft` (about `3 ft 3.37 in`), and `10 m` equals `32.8084 ft`. Using division by the exact international constant `0.3048` instead of a truncated multiplier like `3.28` preserves full precision across structural engineering, athletic track specifications, and personal height conversions.

While the metric meter is the global standard across science, manufacturing, and international sports, US construction blueprints, lumber sizing, elevation profiles, and everyday height measurements still rely on feet and inches. This guide details the NIST and ISO standards governing the conversion, walks through three practical step-by-step examples, provides a ready-to-use conversion table, and highlights common mistakes—such as reading decimal feet as inches.

## Exact Conversion Formula and International Standard

Under the International System of Units (SI), the meter is the base unit of length, standardized by the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) and codified for technical communication in ISO 80000-3 and ISO 80000-2, as well as NIST Special Publication 811 in the United States.

Because the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement defined `1 international yard = 0.9144 m` and `1 foot = 1/3 yard`, the international foot is fixed at exactly `0.3048 m`. Inverting that definition yields the meters-to-feet formula:

- `ft = m ÷ 0.3048` (exact rational form: `m × 1250 ÷ 381`)
- `1 m ≈ 3.280839895013123 ft`
- `1 m = 39.37007874... in`

Notice that `1 ÷ 0.3048` produces an infinitely repeating decimal (`3.280839895...`). Dividing by `0.3048` in spreadsheets, CAD formulas, or calculators ensures you start from the exact treaty constant rather than introducing premature rounding.

## Step-by-Step Worked Practical Examples

### 1. Construction: Steel Beam Span in Feet and Inches

Suppose a metric structural schedule specifies a `4.5 m` steel I-beam, and the framing crew on site works with an imperial tape measure graduated in feet and inches.

1. Divide meters by `0.3048`: `4.5 ÷ 0.3048 = 14.76378 ft`.
2. Separate the whole feet: `14 ft`.
3. Multiply the decimal remainder by `12` to get inches: `0.76378 × 12 = 9.165 in` (roughly `9-3/16 in`).
4. Result: `14 ft 9.17 in` (or `14 ft 9-3/16 in` on a carpenter's tape).

### 2. Sports: Olympic Pool and Soccer Pitch Dimensions

International swimming and football (soccer) governing bodies publish venue dimensions in meters, while North American broadcasters frequently cite them in feet and yards.

1. For a `50 m` Olympic swimming pool: `50 ÷ 0.3048 = 164.04199 ft` (`164 ft 0.5 in`).
2. For a standard `105 m` FIFA international pitch length: `105 ÷ 0.3048 = 344.48819 ft`. Dividing by `3` converts this to `114.83 yards`, demonstrating why international pitches are longer than a 100-yard American football field.

### 3. Human Height: Converting 1.78 Meters to Feet and Inches

Medical charts and driver's licenses in metric countries list height in meters or centimeters, such as `1.78 m` (`178 cm`).

1. Divide by `0.3048`: `1.78 ÷ 0.3048 = 5.839895 ft`.
2. Keep the integer part: `5 ft`.
3. Multiply the remainder by `12`: `0.839895 × 12 = 10.0787 in`.
4. Result: `5 ft 10.1 in` (commonly stated as `5'10"`).

## Industry Use Cases, Regional Pitfalls, and Common Mistakes

### Reading Decimal Feet as Feet and Inches

The most frequent jobsite error is reading decimal feet as inches. For instance, `5.5 ft` is `5 ft 6 in` (`0.5 × 12 = 6`), not `5 ft 5 in`. Likewise, `1.75 m` equals `5.74147 ft`, or `5 ft 8.9 in`, not `5 ft 7.4 in`. Confusing `14.76 ft` (`14 ft 9.1 in`) with `14 ft 7.6 in` introduces a `1.5-inch` fabrication error. Always multiply the fractional remainder by `12`.

### Truncated Multipliers (`3.28` vs. `1 ÷ 0.3048`)

Mental estimates often use `1 m ≈ 3.28 ft`. While adequate for a patio (`5 m × 3.28 = 16.40 ft` vs. `16.4042 ft`), over a `1,000 m` bridge span `3.28` returns `3,280 ft` instead of `3,280.84 ft`—nearly `10 inches` short. In spreadsheets, use `=A1/0.3048` instead of `=A1*3.28`.

### Construction Lumber and Nominal Sizing

Builders adapting metric plans to North American framing must distinguish unit conversion from lumber dimensions. A `2.4 m` ceiling (`2.4 ÷ 0.3048 = 7.874 ft`, or `94.5 in`) fits standard 8-foot (`96 in`) framing studs with plates, whereas a `2.5 m` ceiling (`8.202 ft`) requires 9-foot studs.

### Sports Records and Geodetic Elevations

World Athletics records field events to `0.01 m` (`0.39 in`), so publishing imperial equivalents requires dividing by exact `0.3048` rather than rounding twice through intermediate estimates.
