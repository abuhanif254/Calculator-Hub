To convert inches to centimeters, multiply the inch measurement by `2.54` using the exact equation `cm = in × 2.54`. One inch equals exactly `2.54 cm` (`25.4 mm`), making `12 in` (`1 ft`) equal to `30.48 cm` and `36 in` (`1 yd`) equal to `91.44 cm`.

Because `2.54` is an exact terminating decimal defined by international treaty, any finite decimal or binary-fraction inch value (`1/2`, `1/4`, `1/8`, `1/16`) produces an exact metric result with zero conversion approximation.

## Exact Conversion Formula & International Standard

Before 1959, slight discrepancies separated the British inch (`≈ 2.5399978 cm`) from the US Mendenhall inch (`≈ 2.5400051 cm`). To unify aerospace, manufacturing, and scientific tooling, standards agencies in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa signed the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement. NIST codified the international yard as `0.9144 m`, establishing the exact definition used in ISO 80000-2 and NIST Special Publication 811:

`cm = in × 2.54`

Converting inches to centimeters is purely multiplicative. When reading tape measures marked in sixteenths or thirty-seconds of an inch, convert the fraction to a decimal (`n ÷ d`), add any whole inches, and multiply by `2.54`. Because binary fractions (`1/2 = 0.5`, `1/4 = 0.25`, `1/8 = 0.125`, `1/16 = 0.0625`) terminate, multiplying by `2.54` always produces an exact centimeter result.

## Step-by-Step Worked Practical Examples

### 1. Sizing a 65-Inch 16:9 Television in Centimeters

Media consoles often list width and height clearances in centimeters, while TV specs state diagonal inches. For a `65-inch` 16:9 screen:

1. Convert the diagonal: `cm = 65 × 2.54 = 165.10 cm`.
2. Compute the 16:9 hypotenuse: `√(16² + 9²) = √337 ≈ 18.35756`.
3. Calculate viewable width: `65 × (16 ÷ 18.35756) = 56.6524 in`, or `143.90 cm`.
4. Calculate viewable height: `65 × (9 ÷ 18.35756) = 31.8670 in`, or `80.94 cm`.
5. Add `1 cm` to `3 cm` for outer bezels before finalizing niche clearances.

### 2. Converting Sheet Goods and Plywood Thickness (48 × 96 × 3/4 in)

North American sheet goods use imperial sizing, whereas metric track saws and CNC routers run in centimeters or millimeters. For a `4 ft × 8 ft` sheet of `3/4-inch` plywood:

1. Convert thickness to decimal: `3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 in`.
2. Multiply by `2.54`: `0.75 × 2.54 = 1.905 cm` (`19.05 mm`).
3. Convert width (`48 in`): `48 × 2.54 = 121.92 cm`.
4. Convert length (`96 in`): `96 × 2.54 = 243.84 cm`.
5. Measure real sheet thickness first, as nominal `3/4-inch` plywood often mics near `23/32 in` (`1.8256 cm`).

### 3. Translating Mixed Cabinetry Countertop Dimensions (36 1/2 in & 24 1/2 in)

Remodel blueprints routinely mix whole inches and fractions. For a `36 1/2 in` counter height and `24 1/2 in` depth:

1. Convert height: `36.5 × 2.54 = 92.71 cm`.
2. Convert depth: `24.5 × 2.54 = 62.23 cm`.
3. Check router bits: a `5/8 in` (`0.625 in`) groove equals `1.5875 cm` (`15.875 mm`), leaving a `0.125 mm` gap with a `16 mm` metric bit.

## Industry Use Cases, Regional Pitfalls & Common Conversion Mistakes

Although `cm = in × 2.54` is exact, industry naming conventions create frequent real-world traps.

* **Rounding `2.54` down to `2.5`:** Mental math using `2.5 cm` per inch introduces a `-1.57%` error. On a `96-inch` wall or countertop, `96 × 2.5 = 240.00 cm` instead of `243.84 cm`—a `3.84 cm` (`1.51 in`) shortage.
* **Nominal vs. actual lumber dimensions:** Never convert softwood framing sizes literally. A North American `2×4` stud measures `1.5 in × 3.5 in` after planing, which equals `3.81 cm × 8.89 cm`, not `5.08 cm × 10.16 cm`.
* **Confusing diagonal screen inches with width:** A `55-inch` TV (`139.70 cm` diagonal) is not `139.70 cm` wide; its 16:9 horizontal glass width is `47.94 in` (`121.77 cm`), plus bezel width.
* **Architectural feet-inch notation errors:** Drawings marked `5'-8"` mean `5 ft + 8 in = 68 in` (`172.72 cm`), not `5.8 ft` (`69.6 in = 176.78 cm`). Convert feet to inches first (`ft × 12`).
* **CSS and print DPI assumptions:** The W3C CSS standard fixes `1 in = 96 px` regardless of physical screen DPI. Print PDF templates at `100%` actual size rather than relying on CSS physical units for woodworking cuts.
