To convert US customary cups to milliliters (`mL`), multiply the number of cups by `236.588`. Using the formula `mL = US cups × 236.588`, one standard US cup equals `236.59 mL`, whereas one Metric cup (used in Australia, Canada, and New Zealand) equals exactly `250 mL`.

Whether you are scaling an American baking formula to a metric measuring jug, adapting European cookbooks, or tracking beverage portions, identifying which "cup" standard a recipe assumes prevents watery batters, dense loaves, and inaccurate nutrition logs.

## Exact Conversion Formula & International Standard

In the United States, liquid and culinary volume units are governed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST Handbook 44 and NIST SP 811), anchored to the US liquid gallon. By federal definition, `1 US gallon = 231 cubic inches` exact, and `1 international inch = 2.54 centimeters` exact, making `1 US gallon = 3,785.411784 mL`.

Because a US customary cup equals `1/16` of a US gallon (`8 US fluid ounces`), dividing `3,785.411784` by `16` yields the exact NIST figure of `236.5882365 mL` (standardly abbreviated as `236.588 mL`). By contrast, ISO 80000-3 and SI-aligned culinary standards avoid customary cups in favor of milliliters (`mL` or `cm³`) and liters (`L`), while Commonwealth metric kitchens adopt a rounded `250 mL` Metric cup:

- **US Customary Cup to mL:** `mL = US cups × 236.588` (exact NIST value: `236.5882365 mL`)
- **Metric Cup to mL:** `mL = Metric cups × 250` (exact)
- **US FDA Legal Cup (nutrition labels):** `mL = FDA cups × 240` (exact statutory rounding under 21 CFR 101.9)
- **mL to US Customary Cups:** `US cups = mL ÷ 236.5882365` (approximately `mL × 0.00422675`)

## Step-by-Step Worked Practical Examples

The three examples below show exact arithmetic for precision baking, savory cooking, and beverage batching.

### 1. Artisan Sourdough Hydration Water (1.5 US cups)

A US bread recipe calls for `1.5 cups` of lukewarm water for `500 g` of bread flour, targeting roughly `71%` hydration.

1. Identify the recipe volume: `1.5 US cups`.
2. Multiply by the US constant: `1.5 × 236.588 = 354.882 mL` (round to `355 mL` on a digital scale, since `1 mL` of water weighs `1 g`).
3. Contrast with a Metric cup: `1.5 × 250 = 375 mL`, which would add `20.12 mL` extra water and push hydration from `71%` up to `75%`.

### 2. Soup & Sauce Reduction Scaling (3.25 US cups)

You are doubling part of a stock base and need `3.25 US cups` (`3 1/4 cups`) measured into a metric liter pitcher.

1. Identify the volume: `3.25 US cups`.
2. Multiply by `236.588`: `3.25 × 236.588 = 768.911 mL`.
3. Result: `768.91 mL` (or `0.769 L`), comfortably under the `775 mL` mark.

### 3. Cold-Brew Syrup Batching (0.75 US cups)

A coffee-shop syrup formulation specifies `3/4 cup` (`0.75 US cups`) of simple syrup per carafe.

1. Identify the volume: `0.75 US cups`.
2. Multiply by `236.588`: `0.75 × 236.588 = 177.441 mL` (`177.44 mL`).
3. Note for food labeling: under US FDA rounding (`240 mL` legal cup), `0.75` of a label serving equals `180 mL`.

## Industry Use Cases, Regional Pitfalls & Common Conversion Mistakes

Culinary errors rarely stem from arithmetic—almost all mistakes happen when cooks or recipe apps mix incompatible regional cup definitions or confuse volume with mass.

- **Five Different "Cups" Worldwide:** Alongside the `236.588 mL` US customary cup, `240 mL` FDA nutrition-label cup, and `250 mL` Metric cup (Australia, New Zealand, Canada, South Africa), older UK cookbooks reference a `284.13 mL` **Imperial cup** (half an imperial pint), while Japanese recipes use a `180 mL` *go* rice cup or a `200 mL` modern Japanese culinary cup. Check a recipe's origin before converting.
- **Volume (mL) Is Not Weight (Grams):** Only water near `4 °C` maintains `1 mL = 1 g`. One US cup (`236.588 mL`) of packed brown sugar weighs roughly `213 g`, granulated sugar `~200 g`, and spooned all-purpose flour `~120–125 g`. Never treat `236.59 mL` as `237 g` for dry baking ingredients—use a digital scale or density table.
- **US vs. Imperial Fluid Ounces:** One US cup holds `8 US fluid ounces` (`29.5735 mL` each), whereas an Imperial fluid ounce is `28.4131 mL` despite the larger Imperial pint (`568.26 mL`). Measuring `8 Imperial fl oz` yields `227.30 mL`, falling nearly `10 mL` short.
- **Liquid Pitchers vs. Dry Scoops:** Clear spouted pitchers are calibrated for eye-level meniscus reading; solid scoop cups are made for leveling dry goods. Measuring hot liquids in dry scoops frequently causes `5–10%` surface-tension overfills.
