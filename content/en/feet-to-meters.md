To convert feet (`ft`) to meters (`m`), multiply the length in feet by the exact international constant `0.3048`: `m = ft × 0.3048`. For example, a 10-foot ceiling equals `3.048 m`, and a 6-foot doorframe measures `1.8288 m`. Because `0.3048` is an exact terminating decimal by international treaty rather than a rounded approximation, multiplying by `0.3048` yields a mathematically exact metric value before rounding.

Whether you are translating architectural blueprints for metric fabrication, verifying aircraft flight levels against metric airspace charts, or recording human height for passport and medical forms, accuracy matters. This guide covers the international standards behind the foot-to-meter constant, three worked calculations, a quick-reference chart, and common pitfalls such as the retired US survey foot and mixed feet-and-inches notation.

## Exact Conversion Formula and International Standard

The modern foot-to-meter relationship follows the International System of Units (SI), documented in NIST SP 811, NIST Handbook 44, and the ISO 80000 standards (ISO 80000-2 and ISO 80000-3). The meter is the SI base unit of length, while the international foot is anchored to it by treaty.

Under the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement signed by national laboratories—including the US National Bureau of Standards (now NIST) and the UK National Physical Laboratory—the international yard was fixed at `0.9144 m`. Since one foot equals one-third of a yard and twelve inches:

- `1 ft = 0.9144 ÷ 3 = 0.3048 m` (exact)
- `1 in = 0.3048 ÷ 12 = 0.0254 m` (exact)
- `m = ft × 0.3048`

Whenever you convert international feet to meters, `0.3048` is an exact rational identity.

## Step-by-Step Worked Practical Examples

### 1. Architecture: Commercial Lobby Ceiling and Span

Suppose an interior specification calls for a lobby clear height of `12.5 ft` and a structural glazing span of `24.5 ft` for an overseas fabrication shop working in millimeters and meters.

1. Multiply the clear height in feet by `0.3048`: `12.5 × 0.3048 = 3.81 m` (exact, or `3,810 mm`).
2. Multiply the glazing span by `0.3048`: `24.5 × 0.3048 = 7.4676 m`.
3. Round to the nearest millimeter for shop drawings: `7.468 m` (`7,468 mm`).

### 2. Aviation: Runway Length and Cruising Altitude

Outside North America and parts of international aviation, runway declared distances are published in meters, while flight crews regularly reference altitudes in feet. Consider an `8,500 ft` runway and a `36,000 ft` cruising altitude (FL360).

1. Convert runway length: `8,500 × 0.3048 = 2,590.8 m`. Performance planners typically round conservatively downward or retain one decimal place (`2,590.8 m`).
2. Convert cruising altitude: `36,000 × 0.3048 = 10,972.8 m`, or approximately `10.97 km`.

### 3. Personal Height: Mixed Feet and Inches

A common error occurs when treating `5 ft 10 in` as `5.10 ft`. Because a foot has 12 inches rather than 10, you must convert the inches first.

1. Convert total height to inches: `(5 × 12) + 10 = 70 in`.
2. Multiply total inches by `0.0254 m/in` (or divide 70 by 12 to get `5.833333... ft` and multiply by `0.3048`): `70 × 0.0254 = 1.778 m`.
3. Express in centimeters or rounded meters: `177.8 cm` (`1.78 m`).

## Industry Use Cases, Regional Pitfalls, and Common Mistakes

### Architecture and Structural Engineering

In global AEC workflows, US imperial drawings often enter metric BIM models set to millimeters. Using a truncated shortcut like `0.305` instead of `0.3048` introduces a `0.066%` error—nearly `20 mm` across a `100 ft` grid—pushing curtain walls and anchor bolts out of tolerance. Always configure CAD transforms to `0.3048` and round only at the final dimension label.

### US Survey Foot vs. International Foot

For over a century, geodetic surveying in several US states relied on the US survey foot (`1200 ÷ 3937 m`, or `0.3048006096... m`). Across a `1,000,000 ft` state coordinate grid, that `2 ppm` difference creates a `0.61 m` (`2 ft`) offset. Effective January 1, 2023, NIST retired the US survey foot in favor of `0.3048 m`, though legacy GIS files still require verification.

### Aviation and Dual-Unit Airspace

Most global aviation assigns altitudes in feet (except metric-altitude airspace such as China and Mongolia) while publishing runway lengths in meters. Pilots must never divide feet by 3 as a mental shortcut, because `3,000 ft ÷ 3 = 1,000 m` overstates true altitude (`914.4 m`) by `85.6 m`.

### Feet-and-Inches Decimal Confusion

Never enter `5 ft 6 in` as `5.6 ft`. `5.6 ft` equals `1.7069 m`, whereas `5 ft 6 in` (`5.5 ft`) equals `1.6764 m`—a `3 cm` error. Convert inches to twelfths of a foot first.
