A headline salary looks generous on an offer letter, yet your true earning power depends on how many hours you actually trade for that check. Converting an annual salary into an hourly figure strips away the illusion of big round numbers—revealing whether a `$85,000` role requiring 55-hour weeks actually pays less per hour than a strict 40-hour job at `$70,000`.

## Decoding Your True Hourly Worth From an Annual Salary Offer

Salaried compensation fixes your annual gross pay, which means every extra hour worked beyond 40 dilutes your effective wage—while every paid day off elevates what you earn per active hour on the clock.

The calculator breaks down an annual offer into weekly, daily, biweekly, monthly, and hourly equivalents using these relationships:

```text
Paid Weeks per Year  = 52 - Unpaid Weeks
Weekly Gross Salary  = Annual Salary / Paid Weeks per Year
Biweekly Gross Pay   = Weekly Gross Salary × 2
Monthly Gross Pay    = Annual Salary / 12
Effective Hourly     = Weekly Gross Salary / Hours per Week
Daily Rate           = Effective Hourly × (Hours per Week / Days per Week)
```

For a standard full-time employee with zero unpaid leave (`52 paid weeks`) and a `40-hour, 5-day` workweek, the formula simplifies to **Annual Salary ÷ 2,080** for hourly pay and **Annual Salary ÷ 260** for daily pay. Adjusting **Hours per Week** upward exposes hidden unpaid overtime; entering **Unpaid Time Off** models academic contracts, seasonal layoffs, or unpaid sabbaticals without distorting your active weekly rate.

## Step-by-Step Breakdown: A $65,000 Salary Under Real Workweek Conditions

Take a common U.S. mid-career offer of **$65,000 per year** on a **5-day schedule** and test it under three realistic working patterns.

### 1. The Nominal Payroll Baseline (40 Hours/Week, 52 Paid Weeks)
- **Weekly Pay:** `$65,000 / 52 = $1,250.00`
- **Biweekly Paycheck:** `$1,250.00 × 2 = $2,500.00`
- **Monthly Gross:** `$65,000 / 12 = $5,416.67`
- **Nominal Hourly Rate:** `$1,250.00 / 40 = $31.25/hour`
- **Daily Rate (8 hours):** `$31.25 × 8 = $250.00/day`

### 2. The Exempt Overtime Reality (48 Actual Hours/Week)
Suppose the role is classified as FLSA-exempt and routinely demands early meetings and evening wrap-ups averaging **48 hours per week** (`2,496 hours/year`):
- **Effective Hourly Rate:** `$65,000 / 2,496 = $26.04/hour`
- **Wage Erosion:** Working 8 unpaid hours each week slashes your true hourly pay by **$5.21/hour**—a `16.7%` effective pay cut hidden inside the exact same `$65,000` salary.

### 3. Pay per Actually Worked Hour (Factoring In Generous PTO)
Conversely, if that same `$65,000` job strictly respects a 40-hour cap and provides 15 days of PTO plus 10 paid federal holidays (`5 paid weeks off`, leaving `47 active workweeks` or `1,880 worked hours`):
- **Pay per Worked Hour:** `$65,000 / 1,880 = $34.57/hour`

Measuring both your *actual weekly hours* and *paid days off* exposes a `$8.53/hour` spread between two roles carrying the identical `$65,000` salary tag.

### Framing Salary Raises in Hourly Terms

Every `$1.00/hour` increase adds `$2,080` per year on a 40-hour schedule. Flipping that equation helps ground performance-review negotiations: requesting a `$5,000` annual raise equals `$2.40/hour` on a 40-hour workweek, but drops to just `$1.92/hour` if you regularly put in 50 hours per week. Translating a proposed raise into dollars per actual hour worked clarifies whether the increase truly compensates for expanded responsibilities.

## Four Blind Spots When Evaluating Salaried Job Offers

Before accepting or countering a salaried offer based on its hourly conversion, audit four hidden variables that distort side-by-side comparisons:

- **Federal and State FLSA Exemption Thresholds:** After federal courts vacated the 2024 Department of Labor threshold increase, the federal FLSA minimum salary for exempt executive, administrative, and professional roles remains **$684/week ($35,568/year)** in 2026, alongside a strict duties test. However, states including California, New York, Washington, and Colorado enforce much higher exempt salary floors. Never assume a salaried title legally strips your right to overtime pay.
- **Uncompensated Commute Time:** Driving 45 minutes each way five days a week adds `7.5 hours/week` of job-locked time. Adding commute hours to a 40-hour `$65,000` desk role (`47.5 committed hours/week`, or `2,470 hours/year`) drops compensation per committed hour from `$31.25` to `$26.32`—before fuel, tolls, and depreciation. A slightly lower remote salary often wins on true hourly economics.
- **Total Compensation Load:** Employer-sponsored health insurance, HSA seed funds, and 401(k) matching contributions (toward the 2026 IRS employee elective deferral cap of `$24,500`) represent real, tax-advantaged wages. A `5%` 401(k) match on `$85,000` adds `$4,250/year`, or `$2.04/hour` in deferred compensation.
- **On-Call and Off-Clock Creep:** Slack messages, weekend client emails, and travel days rarely show up in job descriptions. Track your actual time for two representative weeks before entering your weekly hours so you negotiate raises or promotions using real data rather than an idealized 40-hour assumption.
