## Why Format SQL Queries?

In software development and database engineering, **Structured Query Language (SQL)** is often dynamically constructed by application frameworks, ORMs, and analytics dashboards. This frequently results in dense, unformatted blocks of code where statements, clauses, and conditions run across hundreds of characters on a single line.

Unformatted SQL is difficult to read, challenging to debug, and prone to costly logical mistakes—such as missing `JOIN` criteria or incorrect `WHERE` grouping.

A dedicated **SQL Formatter** restores structural clarity to your database queries by enforcing consistent indentation rules, placing major clauses (`SELECT`, `FROM`, `WHERE`, `GROUP BY`, `ORDER BY`) on their own lines, and aligning subqueries.

---

## Dialect-Aware Formatting

Different database systems have distinct syntactic features and reserved keywords. Our formatter is aware of dialect-specific nuances:
* **PostgreSQL:** Handles type casting operators (`::`), JSONB arrow operators (`->`, `->>`), and window functions.
* **MySQL & MariaDB:** Handles backtick quoting, custom procedural keywords, and regex pattern matching.
* **Microsoft SQL Server (T-SQL):** Accurately indents square bracket identifiers (`[Table]`), CTEs (`WITH` clauses), and system stored procedures.
* **Google BigQuery & Snowflake:** Supports nested analytical expressions, arrays, and complex analytical functions.
