Spreadsheets operate on a virtually unbounded two-dimensional coordinate plane—modern Excel worksheets span up to 1,048,576 rows by 16,384 columns—whereas physical paper and PDF viewers enforce strict rectangular boundaries. Without disciplined scaling, column fitting, and sheet curation, converting a financial model or operational ledger produces fractured printouts where totals spill onto orphaned trailing sheets. Local browser-based conversion bridges that gap by translating cell grids, formatting trees, and merged spans directly into structured PDF pages without sending proprietary numbers across the internet.

## How SpreadsheetML and Legacy BIFF8 Workbooks Map to Fixed Pages

When you drop a spreadsheet into the workspace, the parser inspects its container format and routes it through one of two client-side engines operating strictly in browser RAM:

- **OOXML `.xlsx` (ECMA-376 SpreadsheetML)**: Unpacked as a ZIP archive and parsed via `ExcelJS`. The engine reads `xl/workbook.xml` for sheet order, resolves shared text indices from `xl/sharedStrings.xml`, interprets fonts, borders, alignments, and hex/theme fills from `xl/styles.xml`, and reconstructs merged regions (`<mergeCells>`) into HTML table `rowSpan` and `colSpan` attributes. Each cell node exposes both its formula string (`<f>`) and its cached evaluated value (`<v>`).
- **Legacy Binary `.xls` (BIFF8 / OLE2)**: Pre-2007 Excel workbooks store records inside binary Compound File streams rather than XML. For `.xls` inputs, the converter falls back automatically to `SheetJS`, extracting sheet matrices, values, and dimensions so older accounting archives remain convertible without desktop software.

After constructing an isolated DOM table for each selected worksheet—with an automatic OKLCH-to-sRGB computed-style proxy preventing CSS Color 4 serialization failures—the tool captures paginated canvases at your chosen density (`Compact` `1.0x`, `Standard` `1.5x`, `High` `2.0x`, or `Print` `3.0x`) and compiles them into an ISO 32000 PDF through `pdf-lib`.

## Pagination Scaling Modes Compared

Selecting the proper scaling algorithm is the single biggest lever for spreadsheet readability. Over-compressing a 40-column ledger onto a single portrait sheet makes currency digits microscopic; leaving auto-scaling active on a 12-column forecast slices December off the right margin.

| Scaling Mode | Geometric Behavior | Primary Advantage | Watch-Out / Trade-Off | Best-Fit Workbook Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fit All Columns** | Scales horizontal width to 100% of printable page width; rows paginate vertically | Eliminates horizontal page splits; preserves natural row-by-row reading | Very wide sheets (25+ columns) shrink font size significantly | Monthly budgets, P&L statements, invoice ledgers |
| **Fit All Rows** | Scales total vertical height to fit one page; columns flow horizontally | Keeps row categories aligned on a single vertical band | Splits wide date horizons across multiple pages | Short KPI dashboards with long multi-year columns |
| **Fit Entire Sheet** | Forces both total width and total height onto a single PDF page | Guarantees a one-page executive snapshot per tab | Becomes illegible if the tab exceeds ~60 rows or ~18 columns | Cover summaries, executive dashboards, single-page quotes |
| **Auto Pagination** | Renders at natural 100% column widths and breaks at page edges | Preserves native cell typography without downscaling | Wide sheets tile across both X and Y page axes | Pre-formatted print templates already sized to paper |
| **Custom Scale (%)** | Applies an explicit user-defined percentage multiplier | Fine-grained manual tuning between legibility and page count | Requires preview inspection to verify page breaks | Standardized regulatory forms and recurring templates |

## Formula Auditing Mode, Gridlines, and Dynamic Print Headers

Standard PDF exports flatten spreadsheets into opaque visual figures, hiding how totals were derived. Financial analysts, auditors, and engineering teams often need proof of underlying logic alongside final numbers. Enabling **Display Formulas** instructs the renderer to print each cell's raw SpreadsheetML formula (`=SUMIFS(Ledger!D:D, Ledger!B:B, "Q4")`) rather than its evaluated cache, transforming a workbook into a verifiable audit artifact.

For everyday business reporting, toggling **Show Gridlines** adds subtle structural borders even when source worksheets lack explicit cell rules. Dynamic header and footer slots automatically inject page indices (`Page 1 of 4`), source filenames, and generation timestamps into the page margins—critical for version control when multiple budget revisions circulate within the same week.

## Handling Wide Financial Models Without Horizontal Splitting

Most business workbooks are wider than they are tall, making **Landscape** orientation (`792 x 612 pt` for Letter or `842 x 595 pt` for A4) the natural default. When working with 12-month rolling forecasts plus variance columns:

1. Switch **Margins** from `Normal (1 in)` to `Narrow (0.5 in)` to reclaim `72 pt` (a full inch) of horizontal table space.
2. Hide unused scratchpad tabs in the multi-sheet selector so only presentation-ready tabs enter the PDF.
3. For ultra-wide operational tables (20+ columns), step up to **A3 Landscape** (`1191 x 842 pt`) so small tabular numbers stay legible on screen and on tabloid office printers.

## Common Excel Export Pitfalls and Diagnostic Fixes

### Missing Values in Programmatically Generated XLSX Files
Python (`openpyxl`), Node.js, or ERP report generators often write formula cells (`ws["C10"] = "=SUM(C2:C9)"`) without computing cached `<v>` results. Because browser converters do not execute Excel's VBA or volatile calculation engine, uncached cells appear blank unless evaluated first. In automated pipelines, force LibreOffice to recalculate and resave before sharing:

```bash
soffice --headless --convert-to xlsx --outdir ./evaluated ./raw_report.xlsx
```

### Excess Blank Trailing Rows or Columns
Formatting an entire column (`A:A`) or accidentally typing a space in cell `Z5000` inflates Excel's `<dimension ref="A1:Z5000"/>` bounding box, causing **Fit Entire Sheet** to zoom out into a tiny dot. Delete empty formatting beyond your active data range (`Ctrl + End` in Excel reveals the true last cell) and re-save before converting.

### Conditional Formatting and External Workbook Links
Dynamic data bars, icon sets, and cross-file external references (`='[Master.xlsx]Sheet1'!A1`) rely on Excel's desktop runtime evaluator. Always break or update external links inside Excel prior to archival export so every figure reflects verified point-in-time numbers.
